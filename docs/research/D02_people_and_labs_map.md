# D02 · People, Labs and Lineages Map — Multi-Person Camera Perception, Real and Simulated

**Scope.** A who's-who and genealogy of the research communities whose output this repository depends on: pedestrian detection, multi-object tracking (MOT), multi-camera tracking and multiview detection, person re-identification (including clothes-changing and gait), video anomaly detection, action/activity detection in surveillance-grade video (ActEV/MEVA), synthetic data for computer vision, crowd analysis, camera calibration in the wild, video–language retrieval, surveillance human factors, and neuromorphic/event vision.

**Status:** research dossier. Not committed. Companion to `R01`–`R06` and `H01`.

2. **Egress blocking.** `arxiv.org`, `dvl.cit.tum.de`, `www.avss2026.org`, `www.aicitychallenge.org` and `vap.aau.dk` were all refused by the network egress proxy. `github.com` fetches worked and were used. Where a fact rests on a search-result snippet rather than a primary page, that is visible in the link.
3. **People policy.** Only public professional information appears here: institutional affiliation, published work, public company roles, public talks, public repositories. No personal contact details beyond what a maintainer publishes in a repo for bug reports, no personal life, no inference about individuals' views.

**Confidence markers used below:**

- *(no marker)* — supported by a linked source in this document.
- `[VERIFY]` — plausible and widely believed in the field, but **not** confirmed by a source retrieved in this session. Treat as a hypothesis to check.
- `[SCALE]` — citation counts and h-indices. These drift monthly and differ by database (Google Scholar vs. Scopus vs. ResearchGate routinely disagree by 20–40%). Where a number appears, the database and approximate date are named. Never quote a bare h-index in a customer-facing document.

A final framing note. "Lineage" in computer vision is rarely a clean advisor→student tree. It is closer to three overlapping structures: (a) the formal doctoral tree; (b) the *benchmark* tree — who curated the dataset everyone then optimised against, which confers more downstream influence than most supervisions; and (c) the *codebase* tree — who wrote the repository that 6,000 later papers forked. This map tracks all three, because in this field the person who shipped `TrackEval` or `OpenGait` shaped more research-hours than many full professors.

## 1. Pedestrian detection — the founding subfield

Person detection is the oldest of these threads and the one whose intellectual history is most often flattened. Three generations are visible: the handcrafted-feature generation (HOG, ICF), the CNN generation (Faster R-CNN derivatives, occlusion-aware losses), and the current transformer/foundation generation where "detection" is increasingly a downstream head on a general encoder.

**Navneet Dalal** and **Bill Triggs** are the origin point for the modern formulation. Their CVPR 2005 HOG paper was produced at INRIA Grenoble as Dalal's doctoral work, [co-advised by Bill Triggs and Cordelia Schmid](https://sites.google.com/view/navneetdalal/home) — a detail that matters, because it connects the detection lineage directly into the Schmid lineage that later dominated video–language retrieval (§11). Dalal's own account is that the work "improved the then state-of-art by 100-1000x and became the de-facto standard for almost a decade, with over 25,000 citations" `[SCALE]`. His subsequent career is a clean example of the vision-to-product pipeline: he [co-founded Flutter (gesture recognition), which Google acquired in 2013, then worked at Google AI and at Nest leading the algorithms team for Nest Cams, and is now co-founder and CEO of Matician](https://sites.google.com/view/navneetdalal/home), a robotics company. Anyone building CCTV analytics should note that the author of HOG spent years productising consumer camera analytics — the failure modes in R04 are ones he met commercially.

**Piotr Dollár** and **Pietro Perona** (Caltech) defined how the field *measures* pedestrian detection. The Caltech Pedestrian Dataset paper — [Dollár, Christian Wojek, Bernt Schiele and Perona, "Pedestrian Detection: A Benchmark", CVPR 2009](https://pdollar.github.io/files/papers/DollarCVPR09peds.pdf) — introduced a dataset "two orders of magnitude larger than existing datasets" and, more importantly, the log-average miss-rate-vs-FPPI evaluation convention that still governs the subfield. Dollár went on to Facebook AI Research, where his name appears on the whole detection-and-segmentation canon and, latterly, on [SAM 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00714) alongside Piotr Dollár and Ross Girshick — the same person who co-authored the 2009 benchmark is a co-author of the 2024 promptable video segmentation foundation model. That single fact is the best available illustration of continuity in this field. His [research page](https://pdollar.github.io/research.html) remains the canonical index of the Integral Channel Features / Fastest Pedestrian Detector in the West line, which is still the right baseline for CPU-only edge deployment `[VERIFY]` on current-generation edge boxes.

**Bernt Schiele** (Max Planck Institute for Informatics, Saarbrücken) is the hinge between the Caltech era and the deep era. His group produced [CityPersons](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1702.05693) (CVPR 2017) with **Shanshan Zhang** and **Rodrigo Benenson**, a set of person annotations layered onto Cityscapes whose stated purpose was cross-benchmark generalisation: "the diversity of CityPersons allows training a single CNN model that generalizes well over multiple benchmarks." The same trio produced the ["How Far are We from Solving Pedestrian Detection?"](https://www.mpi-inf.mpg.de/departments/computer-vision-and-machine-learning/research/people-detection-pose-estimation-and-tracking/how-far-are-we-from-solving-pedestrian-detection/) error-analysis line, which is the most honest work in the subfield and the one this repository should imitate methodologically: it decomposes residual error into localisation, background, and annotation noise rather than reporting a single number. **Shanshan Zhang** is now [a professor at Nanjing University of Science and Technology](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@shanshan-zhang-zhang-shan-shan) — a transplant of the MPI pedestrian-detection agenda into the Chinese system, which is how several of these lineages propagated. Schiele's Google Scholar profile is [here](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=z76PBfYAAAAJ&hl=en); he is among the most-cited people-detection researchers alive `[SCALE]`.

**Shuai Shao**, **Xiangyu Zhang** and the late **Jian Sun** at Megvii produced the other dataset that every crowd-detection paper now reports: [CrowdHuman (arXiv 2018)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.00123), authored by "Shuai Shao, Zijian Zhao, Boxun Li, Tete Xiao, Gang Yu, Xiangyu Zhang, and Jian Sun", with 470,000 human instances in train+val and the visible/full-body/head triple-box annotation scheme that made occlusion-aware detection tractable. Shao's public account is that he "initially worked at Megvii Research under the supervision of Dr. Jian Sun" and then "served ByteDance for four years" ([sshao.com](https://www.sshao.com/)). Jian Sun's own legacy is larger than detection: he was [Chief Scientist and Managing Director of Research at Megvii and Dean of the College of AI at Xi'an Jiaotong University, author of ResNet, Faster R-CNN and ShuffleNet](https://www.alphaxiv.org/organizations/megvii/alumni). His death in 2022 `[VERIFY]` removed the single most important node in the Chinese detection lineage, and the Megvii diaspora — into ByteDance, into academia — is one of the main mechanisms by which that lineage is now distributed.

**Others to know in this subfield.** *Rodrigo Benenson* (post-docs at KU Leuven and MPI Informatik; Google since 2016 per his [Google Research page](https://research.google/people/rodrigobenenson/); Cityscapes co-creator) — the "Ten Years of Pedestrian Detection" survey and the annotation-quality critiques `[audit R4 2026-09-26: affiliation confirmed]`. *Xiaogang Wang* (CUHK, then SenseTime), whose group's occlusion-handling and part-based detectors fed directly into the SenseTime product line; he is [co-founder of SenseTime and Managing Director of SenseTime Research, and his SenseTime–CUHK team won multiple ILSVRC championships in 2016 and 2017](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/xiaogang-wang). *Wanli Ouyang* (CUHK → Sydney → Shanghai AI Lab) `[VERIFY]` — DeepID-Net and the joint-deep-learning pedestrian detectors. *Xingyi Zhou* (CenterNet, CenterTrack; later Google DeepMind) `[VERIFY]` — anchor-free detection is what made the 2021–2023 trackers simple enough to ship. *Zheng Ge / Songtao Liu* (Megvii, YOLOX) `[VERIFY]` — YOLOX is the detector ByteTrack is built on, per the [ByteTrack repository](https://github.com/FoundationVision/ByteTrack).

**Reading the subfield honestly.** The lesson from the Schiele line is that pedestrian-detection progress on benchmarks has been substantially annotation-quality progress. When you evaluate SurvSim-trained detectors, decompose the error the way the MPI group does, or you will attribute to your synthetic data an improvement that belongs to your label protocol.

## 2. Single-camera multi-object tracking — the MOTChallenge world

MOT is unusually legible as a social structure, because one benchmark organised the entire field for a decade and its four curators are still the field's centre of gravity.

**Laura Leal-Taixé** is the most important single node. She is [a Senior Research Manager at NVIDIA and Adjunct Professor at TUM, leading the Dynamic Vision and Learning group](https://dvl.cit.tum.de/team/lealtaixe/), with a [publication record indexed here](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=tT2TC-UAAAAJ&hl=en) `[SCALE]` and an [NVIDIA DVL author page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/dvl/author/l.-leal-taixe/). She [created MOT16 and co-authored the MOTChallenge benchmark paper for single-camera multiple target tracking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.07548) (IJCV 2021). Her group's output is the spine of modern MOT:

- **[Learning a Neural Solver for Multiple Object Tracking](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07515)** (CVPR 2020 oral), by **Guillem Brasó** and Leal-Taixé, which "exploits the classical network flow formulation of MOT to define a fully differentiable framework based on Message Passing Networks (MPNs)". Brasó is now [a researcher at NVIDIA and a PhD candidate at TUM](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@guillem-braso); code at [dvl-tum/mot_neural_solver](https://github.com/dvl-tum/mot_neural_solver).
- **TrackFormer**, "an end-to-end multi-object tracking and segmentation model based on an encoder-decoder Transformer architecture [that] introduces track query embeddings which follow objects through a video sequence in an autoregressive fashion", by **Tim Meinhardt**, **Alexander Kirillov**, Leal-Taixé and **Christoph Feichtenhofer** ([2021/CVPR 2022](https://dvl.cit.tum.de/team/lealtaixe/)). Note the co-author list: it joins the TUM tracking lineage to the FAIR video lineage that later produced SAM 2.
- **HOTA**, the metric that displaced MOTA. [Jonathon Luiten, Aljoša Ošep, Patrick Dendorfer, Philip Torr, Andreas Geiger, Laura Leal-Taixé and Bastian Leibe, "HOTA: A Higher Order Metric for Evaluating Multi-object Tracking", IJCV 2021](https://www.vision.rwth-aachen.de/publication/00207/) — "a novel MOT evaluation metric which explicitly balances the effect of performing accurate detection, association and localization into a single unified metric."

That author list is itself a lineage diagram: TUM (Leal-Taixé, Dendorfer, Ošep), RWTH Aachen (Luiten, Leibe), Oxford (Torr), Tübingen (Geiger). **Bastian Leibe** is [Professor for Computer Vision at RWTH Aachen, leading a group focused on object recognition, tracking, and scene understanding](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@bastian-leibe) — and himself a descendant of the Schiele/Van Gool tradition `[VERIFY]`. **Jonathon Luiten** did his PhD at RWTH and is now [a research scientist at Meta Reality Labs, with research-visitor stints at CMU and Oxford](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@jonathon-luiten). He is also the person who wrote the code the whole field scores itself with: [TrackEval](https://github.com/JonathonLuiten/TrackEval) is "the official evaluation code for RobMOTS, KITTI Tracking, MOTChallenge" and supports MOT15/16/17/20, KITTI MOTS, TAO, BDD-100k, DAVIS, YouTube-VIS, BURST, PersonPath22 and the Head Tracking Challenge, maintained by Luiten with **Arne Hoffhues**, MIT-licensed. If you want one artefact that demonstrates the codebase-lineage point, it is this.

**Anton Milan**, **Ian Reid** and **Konrad Schindler** are the other MOTChallenge founders. The benchmark papers place [Milan and Reid at the Australian Centre for Visual Technologies, University of Adelaide, and Schindler at the Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing Group, ETH Zurich](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.00831); Milan is now [a researcher at the Amazon Research and Development Center in Berlin](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@anton-milan). Milan and Reid also produced [Online Multi-Target Tracking Using Recurrent Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.03635v2) — the first credible learned-association tracker, and worth reading for how early the field understood the problem it then spent five years re-solving. **MOT20**, ["A benchmark for multi object tracking in crowded scenes"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.09003), is the crowd-density extension and the one most relevant to surveillance work.

**The engineering lineage: SORT → DeepSORT → ByteTrack.** [DeepSORT](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.07402) (ICIP 2017) was authored by **Nicolai Wojke** and **Dietrich Paulus** (University of Koblenz-Landau) with **Alex Bewley** (then Queensland University of Technology) — affiliations confirmed, current positions *not* recoverable in this session `[VERIFY]`. Bewley is the first author of SORT itself `[VERIFY]`; the code at [nwojke/deep_sort](https://github.com/nwojke/deep_sort) is still, nine years on, the most-deployed tracker in commercial CCTV `[VERIFY]`. The successor is **[ByteTrack](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820001.pdf)** (ECCV 2022) by "Yifu Zhang, Peize Sun, Yi Jiang, Dongdong Yu, Fucheng Weng, Zehuan Yuan, Ping Luo, Wenyu Liu, and Xinggang Wang" — a HUST / HKU / ByteDance collaboration. **Xinggang Wang** is [a full Professor at Huazhong University of Science and Technology](https://xwcv.github.io/); **Yifu Zhang** is [now at ByteDance Seed](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=MBxFzjcAAAAJ&hl=en&oi=sra) with a [personal page here](https://ifzhang.github.io/); **Ping Luo** is at HKU `[VERIFY]`. The [repository](https://github.com/FoundationVision/ByteTrack) reports 80.3 MOTA / 77.3 IDF1 / 63.1 HOTA on MOT17 at 29.6 FPS and 77.8 / 75.2 / 61.3 on MOT20 at 13.7 FPS, MIT-licensed, ~6.7k stars, with ONNX / TensorRT / ncnn / DeepStream deployment paths. The intellectual content is one sentence — "associating every detection box instead of only the high score ones" — and it moved IDF1 "by 1-10 points across different trackers." That ratio of idea-size to impact is the single most instructive fact about MOT as a field.

**Kris Kitani** (CMU Robotics Institute) is the other major MOT-adjacent lineage, via OC-SORT and the observation-centric family (`[audit 2026-09-26]` GHOST removed — it is Seidenschwarz, Brasó, Leal-Taixé et al., TUM, not Kitani). He is [an associate research professor at the Robotics Institute and Director of the MSCV programme](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/ri-faculty/kris-m-kitani/), with interests "at the intersection of first-person vision, human activity modeling and inverse reinforcement learning" and citation counts around 20,600 (ResearchGate) to 25,900 (Google Scholar) `[SCALE]`. The MSCV directorship matters for a people map: it is a high-throughput pipeline of CV master's graduates into US industry.

**Others.** *Patrick Dendorfer* (TUM; MOT20, HOTA — `[audit 2026-09-26]` not a MOTSynth author per [arXiv 2108.09518](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.09518)). *Aljoša Ošep* (TUM → NVIDIA; open-world tracking; co-author of HOTA and MOTSynth `[audit 2026-09-26]`) `[audit R4 2026-09-26: confirmed — "Senior Research Scientist at NVIDIA"; PhD RWTH Aachen (Leibe), then TUM DVL from Aug 2019, also CMU RI — per [his homepage](https://aljosaosep.github.io/) and [TUM DVL](https://dvl.in.tum.de/team/osep/)]`. *Peize Sun* (HKU; TransTrack, Sparse R-CNN) — co-author on ByteTrack per the [ECCV paper](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2022/papers_ECCV/papers/136820001.pdf). *Zhongdao Wang* (JDE/FairMOT lineage) `[VERIFY]`. *Yifu Zhang* again for FairMOT `[VERIFY]`.

## 3. Multi-camera tracking, multiview detection and cross-view association

This is the subfield closest to this repository's purpose, and it is much smaller than MOT — perhaps thirty people worldwide do it seriously. It has two poles: the EPFL geometric tradition and the ANU learned-projection tradition, plus an industrial pole at NVIDIA.

**Pascal Fua** (EPFL CVLab) is the geometric pole. His lab's interests are "shape and motion recovery from images, object and people detection and tracking in video sequences, and analysis of brain microscopy image-stacks" ([EPFL CVLab](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/cvlab/data/data-wildtrack/); [Fua bio](https://people.epfl.ch/pascal.fua/bio)), and the artefact that matters here is **WILDTRACK**: [Tatjana Chavdarova, Pascal Fua and colleagues, "WILDTRACK: A Multi-camera HD Dataset for Dense Unscripted Pedestrian Detection", CVPR 2018](https://www.epfl.ch/labs/cvlab/data/data-wildtrack/). Seven overlapping HD cameras, unscripted pedestrians, full extrinsic/intrinsic calibration — still the reference real multi-camera pedestrian benchmark. Chavdarova subsequently moved away from vision into optimisation and game theory: her [current page](https://chavdarova.github.io/) frames her as working on "Game Theory, Machine Learning, Variational Inequalities", and she [spoke on "Learning Dynamics in Multiplayer Games" at UT Austin in November 2025](https://chavdarova.github.io/). That is a common and under-noticed pattern: the person who built the dataset your field lives on may have left the field entirely, which is why dataset maintenance decays.

Fua's lineage is one of the broadest in European vision `[VERIFY]`: the Probabilistic Occupancy Map (POM) line with **François Fleuret** (now Geneva) is the direct ancestor of every ground-plane-aggregation multiview detector, including MVDet. Fua's [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=kzFmAkYAAAAJ&hl=en) `[SCALE]` and [lab listing](https://www.researchgate.net/lab/Pascal-Fua-Lab) are the entry points.

**Liang Zheng** (ANU) and **Yunzhong Hou** are the learned pole, and the pairing is the clearest advisor→student chain in this map. Hou's doctoral thesis at ANU is titled ["Deep Multiview Understanding: Design and Optimisation"](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/7a691a5f-20ff-4f4a-9fe0-59b2fbe16cc2/content). Their **MVDet** paper — ["Multiview Detection with Feature Perspective Transformation", ECCV 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.07247) — introduced the anchor-free ground-plane feature-projection architecture that is now the default, and shipped **MultiviewX**, a *synthetic* multi-camera dataset, alongside it: [code and MultiviewX at hou-yz/MVDet](https://github.com/hou-yz/MVDet). For a repository doing real+sim multi-person multi-camera work, MVDet/MultiviewX is the single most directly relevant prior art, because Hou and Zheng did exactly the real-vs-synthetic comparison (WILDTRACK vs MultiviewX) that SurvSim needs to do. Zheng was [an Associate Professor at ANU, promoted to Professor in 2026](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Liang-Zheng-22); Hou is [a Research Fellow at ANU](https://hou-yz.github.io/), and the pair were [still co-publishing as of October 2025, with Hou's current focus on "AI videography, 3D vision, and autonomous camera control for embodied agents"](https://hou-yz.github.io/). Zheng's group page is [zheng-lab-anu.github.io](https://zheng-lab-anu.github.io/). The field has continued past MVDet: [DCHM: Depth-Consistent Human Modeling for Multiview Detection (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.14505) is the current direction — replacing the flat ground-plane assumption with per-person depth consistency, which is exactly the assumption that breaks on real stairs, escalators and sloped concourses.

**The NVIDIA / AI City industrial pole.** **Milind Naphade** [launched the AI City Challenge to promote computer vision research in intelligent transportation; he was previously CTO of Metropolis at NVIDIA and is currently Senior Vice President at Capital One AI Foundations](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@milind-naphade). **Zheng (Thomas) Tang** is [Organizing Committee Lead for the AI City Challenges at CVPR and Tech Lead of the Metropolis Multi-Camera Tracking AI Workflow, integrated into the Mega Omniverse Blueprint and shown in the CES'25 and GTC'25 keynotes](https://www.linkedin.com/in/zhengthomastang/); his [CV is public](https://zhengthomastang.github.io/files/ZhengTang_CV.pdf). **David C. Anastasiu** (San Jose State) is a long-running co-organiser, visible on [the 4th](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/papers/w35/Naphade_The_4th_AI_City_Challenge_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf) and [5th](https://www.albany.edu/faculty/mchang2/files/2021_06_CVPRW_AICity_Org.pdf) challenge reports. The [9th AI City Challenge ran at ICCV 2025](https://iccv.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/workshop/2832) and the [2025 edition attracted 245 teams from 15 countries](https://www.aicitychallenge.org/2025-ai-city-challenge/). Operationally this is the most important venue in the map: it is where multi-camera tracking gets evaluated at city scale, with synthetic training data supplied by NVIDIA, which is precisely the SurvSim thesis being tested in public.

**Others.** *Tatjana Chavdarova* (above). *François Fleuret* (Full Professor, University of Geneva, and research scientist at Meta FAIR per [his bio](https://fleuret.org/francois/bio.html); POM) `[audit R4 2026-09-26: affiliation confirmed]`. *Yunzhong Hou* again for the "camera placement / where to put the cameras" line, which is directly actionable for synthetic-scene design `[VERIFY]`. *The CityTrack authors* — ["CityTrack: Improving City-Scale Multi-Camera Multi-Target Tracking by Location-Aware Tracking and Box-Grained Matching"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.02753) is a useful read on what actually fails at city scale.

## 4. Person re-identification — the QMUL school and its diaspora

ReID has a genuine founding school, and it is British.

**Shaogang (Sean) Gong** (Queen Mary University of London) is the founder. He is [a Professor at QMUL, in the Centre for Multimodal AI](https://www.seresearch.qmul.ac.uk/cmai/people/sgong/), and his bibliography is the field's prehistory: he [published research monographs on *Visual Analysis of Behaviour* with Tao Xiang and edited the field-defining *Person Re-Identification* volume](https://mlanthology.org/authors/g/gong-shaogang/) — arguably the book that named the task. His [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Nhi0I8cAAAAJ&hl=en) `[SCALE]` and [brief bio](http://www.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/~sgg/brief.html) are the primary references. Crucially for a surveillance-oriented repository, Gong's framing was always *operational*: open-world, unsupervised, camera-network-scale, low-resolution — not the closed-set academic benchmark game that the field later became.

The QMUL chain is dense and traceable through co-authorship: Gong with **Tao Xiang** and **Timothy Hospedales** produced ["Learning a Discriminative Null Space for Person Re-Identification" (CVPR 2016), "Person Re-Identification by Unsupervised Graph Learning" (ECCV 2016), "Partial Person Re-Identification" (ICCV 2015)](https://mlanthology.org/authors/g/gong-shaogang/) and, startlingly early, ["Investigating Open-World Person Re-Identification Using a Drone" (ECCV 2014)](https://mlanthology.org/authors/g/gong-shaogang/). **Timothy Hospedales** is the most prominent descendant: he left QMUL for Edinburgh and Samsung AI `[VERIFY]`, and his [publication record](https://mlanthology.org/authors/h/hospedales-timothy-m/) shows the pivot from ReID into meta-learning and domain generalisation — which is, not coincidentally, the theory apparatus that sim-to-real transfer needs. **Ryan Layne** ([attribute-based ReID](https://mlanthology.org/authors/l/layne-ryan/)) and **Jifei Song** ([record](https://mlanthology.org/authors/s/song-jifei/)) are further QMUL products. QMUL's sibling group is [CIS/Intelligent Sensing (EECS)](https://cis.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/news.html), the Andrea Cavallaro lineage `[VERIFY]` — Cavallaro has since moved to Idiap/EPFL `[VERIFY]` — which supplied much of the multi-camera and privacy-in-video work.

**Liang Zheng** (ANU, again — he is the only person appearing in two sections of this map as a principal) is the other pole. **Market-1501** — [Zheng, Liyue Shen, Lu Tian, Shengjin Wang, Jingdong Wang and Qi Tian, ICCV 2015](https://scispace.com/papers/scalable-person-re-identification-a-benchmark-iiee63lja2), Tsinghua / UT San Antonio / Microsoft — "contributes a new high-quality dataset for person re-identification, named Market-1501 … over 32,000 annotated bounding boxes, plus a distractor set of over 500K images, making it the largest person re-id dataset to date." It is cited over 5,200 times on SciSpace's count alone `[SCALE]`, and Zheng maintains [a state-of-the-art leaderboard for it](https://zheng-lab.cecs.anu.edu.au/Project/state_of_the_art_market1501.html) — an unusually good piece of scientific citizenship, and a model for how SurvSim benchmarks should be published. **Qi Tian** (Huawei Noah's Ark, formerly UTSA) `[VERIFY]` is the other constant in that author list and one of the highest-output people in the field.

**The Chinese industrial pole.** [SenseTime, Megvii and Yitu](https://www.cnn.com/2019/10/09/tech/hikvision-sensetime-blacklist) and [Hikvision — "a $42 billion company and the world's largest video surveillance gear maker"](https://www.cnn.com/2019/10/09/tech/hikvision-sensetime-blacklist) — carried ReID from paper to deployment at a scale no Western lab matched, and were placed on the US Entity List in 2019, a fact with direct procurement consequences for any UK/EU integrator. The academic parents are **Xiaoou Tang** ([Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=qpBtpGsAAAAJ)) and **Xiaogang Wang** ([co-founder and MD of SenseTime Research](https://www.crunchbase.com/person/xiaogang-wang), [Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=-B5JgjsAAAAJ&hl=en)) at CUHK, whose group produced CUHK01/02/03 and the first deep ReID architectures `[VERIFY]`. The Megvii alumni network is [partially mapped publicly](https://www.alphaxiv.org/organizations/megvii/alumni).

**Clothes-changing ReID (CC-ReID)** is the sub-branch that matters most for realistic surveillance, because appearance-invariance assumptions are the first thing to fail across a day. Two datasets anchor it. **PRCC** comes from the contour-sketch line: **Qize Yang** is [an author of "Person Re-identification by Contour Sketch under Moderate Clothing Change", TPAMI 2019](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@vishwanath-sindagi) `[VERIFY — the attribution appeared in a search snippet, not a primary page]`, and the dataset comprises "221 persons and 33,698 images". **LTCC** is "captured by 12 cameras for two months, including 162 persons and 15,138 images, divided into two subsets: persons with changing clothes (91 people, 415 sets of different clothes, 14,756 images) and consistent clothes (61 people, 2,382 images)" — figures per [this 2024 survey-style paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03977) `[VERIFY for the original LTCC authorship — Xuelin Qian, Yanwei Fu et al., Fudan]`. The live 2025–26 research directions are visible in the literature: [attribute-description embedding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.05646), [coarse attribute prediction with task-agnostic distillation for real-world CC-ReID (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.12580), [diverse normalization (2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03977), and [instance-adaptive low-rank orthogonal subspaces (2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11661). **Xiao Wang** maintains [the community paper list](https://github.com/wangxiao5791509/Cloth_Change_Person_reID_Paper_List) — the fastest way to enter this sub-branch. Note the scale of these datasets: 162 and 221 identities. Any claim that CC-ReID "works" is a claim about a few hundred people, and this is the strongest argument in the entire map for synthetic identity generation.

**Adjacent modalities worth tracking.** Event-camera ReID has arrived: ["When Person Re-Identification Meets Event Camera: A Benchmark Dataset and An Attribute-guided Re-Identification Framework" (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13659) — which connects this section to §14. Illumination-adaptive ReID ([Zhihang Zhong et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.04525)) is the day/night problem R04 cares about. And ["Person Re-identification in the Wild"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02531) is the paper that first insisted on evaluating detection and ReID jointly rather than on cropped boxes — the mistake most systems papers still make.

## 5. Gait recognition — the most operationally interesting biometric for CCTV

Gait matters here for one reason: it survives clothing change, low resolution and face occlusion, which is exactly the regime CCTV lives in. The field is dominated by two Chinese groups and one open-source framework.

**Shiqi Yu** (Southern University of Science and Technology, SUSTech) is the framework owner. **[OpenGait](https://github.com/ShiqiYu/OpenGait)** is the `TrackEval` of gait: per the repository, it is "mainly maintained by Dongyang Jin", with primary development by **Chao Fan**, **Chuanfu Shen** and **Junhao Liang** from the Shiqi Yu Group at SUSTech, "supported by WATRIX.AI". Supported datasets: CASIA-B, OUMVLP, SUSTech1K, HID, GREW, Gait3D, CCPG, CASIA-E, GaitLU-1M. Recent milestones listed on the repo: **FoundationGait** accepted to ECCV 2026 as a Spotlight, **BiggerGait** to NeurIPS 2025, **LidarGait++** to CVPR 2025, and the OpenGait extension to **TPAMI 2025**. That is an unusually healthy research programme — a single lab running foundation-model, LiDAR and skeleton branches simultaneously off one codebase.

**Yongzhen Huang** and **Saihui Hou** are the second pole, and the commercial one: both are ["with the School of Artificial Intelligence at Beijing Normal University, and also with Watrix Technology Limited"](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.06597v3). Watrix is the CASIA gait spin-out — the industry tie is explicit in the paper affiliations, which is unusual and useful. Huang's own lineage runs back to **Tieniu Tan** and **Liang Wang** at CASIA `[VERIFY]`, the group that built CASIA-B, the dataset on which gait recognition was defined for fifteen years.

The canonical citation is **[OpenGait: Revisiting Gait Recognition Towards Better Practicality, CVPR 2023 (highlight)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Fan_OpenGait_Revisiting_Gait_Recognition_Towards_Better_Practicality_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)** by "Chao Fan, Saihui Hou, Junhao Liang, Chuanfu Shen, Jingzhe Ma, Dongyang Jin, Yongzhen Huang, and Shiqi Yu", extended to [TPAMI 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40460018/) as ["OpenGait: A Comprehensive Benchmark Study for Gait Recognition Toward Better Practicality"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09138). Read the abstract's admission carefully, because it is the sim-to-real problem in one sentence: gait recognition "has made significant strides in indoor settings, but existing methods often yield unsatisfactory results when applied to newly released real-world gait datasets." The [CASIA-E dataset](https://www.scidb.cn/en/detail?dataSetId=57be0e918db743279baf44a38d013a06) is now supported in OpenGait, closing the CASIA–SUSTech loop.

`[VERIFY]` **Not searched, high priority:** **Zheng Zhu** (GREW, in-the-wild gait), **Jinkai Zheng / Gait3D** (gait from 3D mesh), **Mark Nixon** (Southampton — the Western founder of gait biometrics, and the person whose work underpins the forensic-gait-evidence literature that UK courts have engaged with), **Yasushi Yagi** (Osaka, OU-MVLP/OU-ISIR). Nixon's lineage is the one a UK defence-oriented project most needs, and it is the most conspicuous gap in this dossier.

## 6. Video anomaly detection — three benchmarks, three schools

**Mubarak Shah** (University of Central Florida) is the single most prolific supervisor in this entire map. He is [UCF Trustee Chair Professor of Computer Science and founding director of both the Center for Research in Computer Vision (CRCV) and the Institute of Artificial Intelligence](https://www.crcv.ucf.edu/person/mubarak-shah/), and [has mentored more than 50 graduate students](https://www.crcv.ucf.edu/person/mubarak-shah/) — the [CRCV PhD student roster](https://www.crcv.ucf.edu/people/students/phd-students/) and [paper index](https://www.crcv.ucf.edu/papers/) are the best public map of any vision lab's output. The relevant artefact is **UCF-Crime**: ["Real-world Anomaly Detection in Surveillance Videos" (CVPR 2018)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322498470_Real-world_Anomaly_Detection_in_Surveillance_Videos) by **Waqas Sultani**, **Chen Chen** and Shah — the weakly-supervised MIL-ranking formulation that created the modern subfield. Sultani was [a TA in Shah's computer vision course in 2014 and was affiliated with both Information Technology University, Pakistan and CRCV](https://www.crcv.ucf.edu/courses/cap5415-fall-2014/), later co-authoring with Shah on [drone action recognition](https://www.crcv.ucf.edu/papers/). The UCF lineage also gave the field UCF101, crowd analysis, and [action localisation/prediction work](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.01194) and [crowd ReID with social constraints](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1612.02155) — the latter being a genuinely underexploited idea for multi-camera association.

**Radu Tudor Ionescu** (University of Bucharest) is the second school and the most methodologically careful. He is [a Professor of Computer Science at the University of Bucharest and a Research Scientist at the Inception Institute of AI, with over 100 papers at CVPR/NeurIPS/ICCV/ACL/EMNLP/TPAMI/IJCV](https://raduionescu.herokuapp.com/); [Semantic Scholar profile](https://www.semanticscholar.org/author/Radu-Tudor-Ionescu/1817759) `[SCALE — h-index not recovered]`. **UBnormal** ([CVPR 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2111.08644v3)) is his key contribution to this repository's concerns, and it is *synthetic*: "the first video anomaly detection benchmark to allow a fair head-to-head comparison between one-class open-set models and supervised closed-set models", which "can enhance the performance of a state-of-the-art anomaly detection framework on two prominent data sets, Avenue and ShanghaiTech." A virtual-scene anomaly benchmark that demonstrably improves real-data performance is the closest published precedent for SurvSim's core claim, and the [announcement thread](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/radu-ionescu-5145374b_ubnormal-new-benchmark-for-supervised-open-set-activity-6920654483051978754-uyKJ) is worth reading for the framing. His [SSMCTB / masked convolutional transformer block](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.12148) is the most-reused architectural component in the subfield. Co-authors to know: **Nicolae-Cătălin Ristea**, **Mariana-Iuliana Georgescu**, **Fahad Shahbaz Khan**, **Marius Popescu** `[VERIFY for the individual chains]`.

**Shenghua Gao** is the third school. His ShanghaiTech group produced the **ShanghaiTech Campus** anomaly dataset and the ConvLSTM/prediction-based family; the lab is [SVIP-Lab](https://svip-lab.github.io/cv/luowx.pdf), the group's anomaly-detection progress is [documented by ShanghaiTech SIST](https://sist.shanghaitech.edu.cn/sist_en/_t113/2021/1102/c3863a103873/page.htm), and his [Scholar profile is here](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=fe-1v0MAAAAJ). **Weixin Luo** ["received his Ph.D. degree from ShanghaiTech University in 2020, supervised by Shenghua Gao"](https://zachluo.github.io/) — a clean advisor→student link — and their joint ["Margin Learning Embedded Prediction for Video Anomaly Detection with A Few Anomalies" (IJCAI 2019)](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/0419.pdf) with [code](https://github.com/svip-lab/MLEP) is the few-shot-anomaly entry point. Gao has since moved toward HKU `[VERIFY]`; HKU's [School of Computing & Data Science is directed by Yi Ma](https://sist.shanghaitech.edu.cn/sist_en/_t113/2021/1102/c3863a103873/page.htm) `[VERIFY]`.

**The UK precursor.** The EPSRC **BEHAVE** project — ["concerned with pre-screening of video sequences for the detection of abnormal or crime-oriented behaviour"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220464942_Crowd_analysis_A_survey) — ran at Edinburgh/Kingston in the mid-2000s and is the direct ancestor of this whole subfield in Britain. **Robert Fisher** (Edinburgh) is the relevant figure; see §8.

## 7. Action and activity detection in surveillance video — ActEV, MEVA and the government evaluation world

This subfield is structurally different from the rest of the map: it was created by a funding programme and a standards body, not by a lab.

**Anthony Hoogs** (Kitware) is the central industrial figure. He is [profiled at Kitware](https://www.kitware.com/anthony-hoogs/), where he leads the computer vision division, and he is a co-author of the dataset: **[MEVA: A Large-Scale Multiview, Multimodal Video Dataset for Activity Detection (WACV 2021)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Corona_MEVA_A_Large-Scale_Multiview_Multimodal_Video_Dataset_for_Activity_Detection_WACV_2021_paper.pdf)** by **Kellie Corona**, **Katie Osterdahl**, **Roderic Collins** and Hoogs. The scale is the point: "over 9,300 hours of untrimmed, continuous video with 144 hours annotated for 37 activity types, marking bounding boxes of actors and props" ([MEVA on AWS Open Data](https://registry.opendata.aws/mevadata/); [data README](https://mevadata.org/resources/README-meva-kf1-data.html)). It is multi-camera, multi-modal (including UAV and IR `[VERIFY]`), scripted-but-realistic, and it is the closest public analogue to what a real site installation looks like. Hoogs also sits [on the advisory committee for the Real-World Surveillance workshop](https://www.kitware.com/wacv-2026/), which tells you where the community's centre is.

The evaluation machinery is **ActEV**, run by NIST: ["The Activities in Extended Video (ActEV) evaluation series are designed to accelerate the development of robust, multi-camera, automatic human activity detection systems for forensic and real-time alerting applications"](https://actev.kitware.com/), and ["the NIST TRECVID ActEV series were initiated in 2018 to support the Intelligence Advanced Research Projects Activity (IARPA) Deep Intermodal Video Analytics (DIVA) Program"](https://actev.kitware.com/). NIST's [HADCV workshop series](https://actev.nist.gov/workshop/hadcv20) is where the results were argued over. ["The footage was collected by Kitware Inc. under the IARPA DIVA program"](https://registry.opendata.aws/mevadata/), and the **DIVA Framework** is ["a software framework designed to provide an architecture and a set of software modules which will facilitate the development of activity recognition analytics, developed as a fully open source project on GitHub"](https://registry.opendata.aws/mevadata/). The surrounding TRECVID context is documented in [the 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.13473) and [2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.13118) campaign overviews — these two papers are the best single description of how government video evaluation actually works, including the scoring protocols (nAUDC, P-Miss@RFA) that differ sharply from academic mAP.

**Alexander Hauptmann** (CMU) is the academic figure most associated with the CMU ActEV/TRECVID entries `[VERIFY — the search returned no direct confirmation of his DIVA/ActEV role, which is a real gap]`. CMU's Informedia group has competed in TRECVID continuously since the 1990s `[VERIFY]`, and Hauptmann's line into multimedia event detection (MED) is the ancestor of modern natural-language video search `[VERIFY]`. **George Awad** and **Jonathan Fiscus** (NIST) are the evaluation architects `[VERIFY]`, visible as authors on the TRECVID overview papers above.

**Why this section is the most important one for a UK defence-adjacent project.** ActEV/MEVA is the only place where multi-camera person-activity detection has been evaluated under adversarial, government-set protocols with a no-peeking test set. Its UK counterpart is i-LIDS (see §8). If you want your synthetic pipeline to be credible to a government customer, the ActEV protocol — not MOT17 — is the shape of evaluation you should imitate.

## 8. The UK surveillance-vision groups — the domestic lineage

**Queen Mary (QMUL)** — Gong, Xiang, and the ReID school; see §4. Plus [Intelligent Sensing / CIS](https://cis.eecs.qmul.ac.uk/news.html).

**Oxford VGG.** [Andrew Zisserman is Professor of Computer Vision Engineering in Engineering Science and a Royal Society Research Professor, founder of the Visual Geometry Group](https://eng.ox.ac.uk/people/andrew-zisserman), "known internationally for his pioneering work in multiple view geometry, visual recognition, and large scale retrieval in images and video", with "over 350 peer reviewed papers" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Zisserman); [Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=UZ5wscMAAAAJ&hl=en)). VGG is now [led jointly by Zisserman, Andrea Vedaldi, João Henriques, Christian Rupprecht and Iro Laina](https://www.robots.ox.ac.uk/~vgg/projects/seebibyte/people.html) — the succession is already done, which is worth noting when you plan a decade-long collaboration. Their [VGGT: Visual Geometry Grounded Transformer won Best Paper at CVPR 2025](https://x.com/oxford_vgg?lang=en), which is the current state of the art in feed-forward multi-view geometry and therefore directly relevant to **calibration-free multi-camera** work (§10). Zisserman's relevance to this map is threefold: multiple-view geometry (the textbook with Hartley), large-scale video retrieval (§11), and an extraordinary supervision tree `[VERIFY]` including Josef Sivic, Relja Arandjelović, Arsha Nagrani, Gül Varol (co-supervised) and Weidi Xie.

**Bristol.** [Dima Damen is Professor of Computer Vision at the University of Bristol, an EPSRC Fellow (2020–2025) working on "the automatic understanding of object interactions, actions and activities using wearable visual (and depth) sensors", project lead for EPIC-KITCHENS — "the largest dataset in egocentric vision, with accompanying open challenges" — and a senior research scientist with Google DeepMind](https://dimadamen.github.io/about.html). **Reading.** [James Ferryman is Professor of Computational Vision at the University of Reading, Research Division Leader for Computer Science, and leads the Computational Vision Group](https://www.reading.ac.uk/computer-science/staff/professor-james-ferryman); he is also [a Turing Institute fellow](https://www.turing.ac.uk/people/james-ferryman). Also relevant: the [i-LIDS AVSS dataset — "three videos (720 × 576) taken with stationary cameras at a train station"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02414), the Home Office benchmark; and Reading's work on [carried-object detection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.03551), a classic i-LIDS-adjacent task.

**Kingston.** [Sergio A. Velastin (Reader) and Paolo Remagnino (Senior Lecturer) at the Digital Imaging Research Centre](https://www.amazon.co.uk/Intelligent-Distributed-Surveillance-Computing-Networks/dp/0863415040) co-edited [*Intelligent Distributed Video Surveillance Systems*](https://digital-library.theiet.org/doi/book/10.1049/pbpc005e), "extended versions of presentations made in two successful IEE symposia on Intelligent Distributed Surveillance Systems (IDSS)", which "addresses limitations of conventional CCTV approaches and discusses how advances in information and communication technologies … can provide improvements in surveillance systems." Velastin's Kingston output is [indexed here](https://eprints.kingston.ac.uk/view/creators/18793.html); Remagnino's [Scholar is here](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=P5OQ_9EAAAAJ&hl=en). **Surrey CVSSP.** [Founded by Josef Kittler in 1986 within Electrical and Electronic Engineering; now directed by Adrian Hilton, with over 120 members](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/centre-vision-speech-signal-processing/about). [Hilton is Professor of Computer Vision and AI, founding Director of the Surrey Institute for People-Centred AI, Director of CVSSP, and a Royal Society Industry Fellow with Framestore](https://cvssp.org/Personal/AdrianHilton/Welcome.html). ## 9. Synthetic data for computer vision — the most directly load-bearing community for this repository

Five distinct traditions, with different assumptions about what synthetic data is *for*.

### 9.1 The game-engine surveillance tradition: MOTSynth (Modena + TUM)

**Rita Cucchiara** (University of Modena and Reggio Emilia) is the senior figure. She is [an Italian computer engineer and researcher in computer vision and AI, Full Professor of Computer Engineering at UNIMORE, where she directed AImageLab and the AIRI interdepartmental centre, author of over 600 papers with more than 33,000 citations and an h-index of 77 (Google Scholar, 2025)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_Cucchiara) `[SCALE]` — and, as of **1 November 2025, Rector of UNIMORE** ([announcement, June 2025](https://www.stampareggiana.it/2025/06/16/unimore-e-rita-cucchiara-la-nuova-rettrice-delluniversita-di-modena-e-reggio-emilia/)). Note the operational consequence: a rector is not running a research group day to day, so AImageLab's technical leadership now sits with her senior staff. [AImageLab, founded 1998](https://aimagelab.unimore.it/), has an explicit [industry-collaboration posture](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/where-research-meets-industry-new-challenges-and-opportunities-at-aimagelab/wrk_230ecae52c5c9db70abbefaa62e7a04d). Cucchiara's own history is important: her group did background subtraction and shadow removal in the early 2000s `[VERIFY]`, i.e. she has been continuously in surveillance vision for 25 years.

**Matteo Fabbri** is the MOTSynth first author and the practitioner. **MOTSynth** — "How Can Synthetic Data Help Pedestrian Detection and Tracking?" (ICCV 2021) — is authored by [Matteo Fabbri, Guillem Brasó, Gianluca Maugeri and others including Rita Cucchiara](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Matteo-Fabbri) `[VERIFY for the full author list — Fabbri, Brasó, Maugeri, Cetintas, Gasparini, Ošep, Calderara, Leal-Taixé, Cucchiara]`. Fabbri's [publication record](https://dblp.org/pid/153/8126.html) and [profile](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Matteo-Fabbri) are the entry points. **The lineage point is the co-authorship**: MOTSynth is a *Modena × TUM* paper — Fabbri and Cucchiara with Brasó and Leal-Taixé. The people who built MOTChallenge co-built the synthetic replacement for it. That is the strongest possible endorsement of the synthetic-surveillance-data thesis, and it is the single most important precedent for this repository: same task, same metrics, same authors, synthetic vs real, measured. Fabbri's earlier **JTA** dataset (GTA-V pedestrian poses/tracking) is the direct ancestor `[VERIFY]`. **Simone Calderara** (UNIMORE) is the other key AImageLab node `[VERIFY]`.

### 9.2 The parametric-human tradition: SMPL, BEDLAM, Meshcapade (Tübingen)

**Michael J. Black** is [a founding director at the Max Planck Institute for Intelligent Systems, leading the Perceiving Systems Department, and Vice President of Digital Human Research at Epic Games](https://is.mpg.de/~black); see also his [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Black). His industrial arm: [Meshcapade was founded in 2018 by Naureen Mahmood, Talha Zaman and Michael Black, is now part of Epic Games, and Black is co-founder and Chief Scientist](https://www.mpg.de/18471930/0325-mpin-max-planck-spin-off-meshcapade-wins-new-startup-award-1160219-x) — it [won a Max Planck startup award](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/michael-j-black-41751114_max-planck-spin-off-meshcapade-wins-new-startup-activity-6914458576891596800--tmy). The Epic Games connection is the most consequential industry tie in this entire map for a UE5-based pipeline: the person who defines the standard parametric human body model is also Epic's VP of digital-human research.

**BEDLAM** is the synthetic-humans-in-motion dataset that proved 3D human pose and shape regressors can be trained on synthetic data alone and beat real-data training `[VERIFY for the specific claim]` — [project page](https://bedlam.is.tue.mpg.de/). Its successor, **[BEDLAM2.0: "Synthetic humans and cameras in motion"](https://bedlam2.is.tuebingen.mpg.de/)**, was authored by "Joachim Tesch, Giorgio Becherini, Prerana Achar, Anastasios Yiannakidis, Muhammed Kocabas, Priyanka Patel, and Michael J. Black" and presented at **NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks**. Note the title: *cameras in motion*. BEDLAM 1.0's known limitation was static cameras; 2.0 fixes exactly the gap that a PTZ/body-worn-camera surveillance simulator faces. **Priyanka Patel** is the person to follow for the production side — she was [a Research Engineer at MPI-IS Tübingen and has been an MLOps Engineer at Meshcapade since August 2024](https://www.linkedin.com/in/priyanka-patel-6b545540/); her [academic page](https://pixelite1201.github.io/) carries the AGORA/BEDLAM line. **Muhammed Kocabas** (VIBE, PARE, SPEC) `[VERIFY]` is the other major Black descendant. And **Joachim Tesch** is the Blender/UE rendering engineer whose name on a paper is the tell that the synthetic pipeline actually works `[VERIFY]`.

### 9.3 The engine-tooling tradition: UnrealCV and UnrealZoo (JHU/PKU/BNU)

**Weichao Qiu** built **UnrealCV**. Per the [project site](https://unrealcv.org/), "UnrealCV was created by students from Johns Hopkins University and Peking University under the supervision of Prof. Alan Yuille and Prof. Yizhou Wang" (the original 2017 work). Qiu is [now a researcher at Huawei](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@weichao-qiu). **Alan Yuille** (JHU) is one of the field's grand theorists and the "diagnostic synthetic data" advocate — the idea that the value of a simulator is *controlled factor variation for model diagnosis*, not merely more training data. That framing is more useful to this repository than the data-augmentation framing, and it is the reason the [papers-with-unrealcv list](https://github.com/unrealcv/papers-with-unrealcv) tags each paper with one of three usage modes: "📊 Dataset generation for training / 🔍 Model diagnosis and factor control / 🏃 Agent interaction and reinforcement learning". Adopt that taxonomy.

**Fangwei Zhong** is the current owner of this tradition. He is [an Associate Professor at Beijing Normal University](http://fangweizhong.xyz/), "co-developed UnrealCV and led the development of UnrealZoo" ([BNU AI School page](https://ai.bnu.edu.cn/xygk/szdw/fgj/067725da68f443e09e675307b4e7132a.htm); [publication record](https://mlanthology.org/authors/z/zhong-fangwei/)). **[UnrealZoo: Enriching Photo-realistic Virtual Worlds for Embodied AI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20977)** was presented at **ICCV 2025** by "Fangwei Zhong, Kui Wu, Churan Wang, Hao Chen, Hai Ci, Zhoujun Li, and Yizhou Wang". **Yizhou Wang** at the [Center on Frontiers of Computing Studies, Peking University](https://cfcs.pku.edu.cn/wangyizhou/) is the senior co-author across both generations. Zhong's distinctive contribution is *active* tracking — an embodied agent that moves a camera to keep a person in frame — which is the research area that maps onto PTZ control and autonomous camera placement. The [curated paper list](https://github.com/unrealcv/papers-with-unrealcv) shows "100+ papers from top-tier venues (CVPR, ICCV, ECCV, ICML, NeurIPS)" with "recent entries (2024-2026) emphasiz[ing] foundation models, embodied AI, and real-world deployment."

### 9.4 The procedural tradition: Infinigen (Princeton) and Kubric (Google)

**Jia Deng** (Princeton) — the person who built ImageNet's annotation pipeline as Fei-Fei Li's student `[VERIFY]` — now leads the most radical synthetic-data programme. **[Infinigen: Infinite Photorealistic Worlds using Procedural Generation (CVPR 2023)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/papers/Raistrick_Infinite_Photorealistic_Worlds_Using_Procedural_Generation_CVPR_2023_paper.pdf)** by **Alexander Raistrick**, **Lahav Lipson**, **Zeyu Ma** and Deng is "a procedural generator of photorealistic 3D scenes of the natural world, where every asset from shape to texture is generated from scratch via randomized mathematical rules, using no external source and allowing infinite variation and composition." The zero-external-asset constraint is a licensing and provenance decision as much as a technical one, and it is worth understanding before you buy a marketplace asset pack. The programme has expanded: [Infinigen Indoors (CVPR 2024)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.11824) with a twelve-author team (Raistrick, Lingjie Mei, Karhan Kayan, David Yan, Yiming Zuo, Beining Han, Hongyu Wen, Meenal Parakh, Stamatis Alexandropoulos, Lipson, Ma, Deng) and [Infinigen-Sim (2025)](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/infinigen-sim-procedural-generation-of-articulated-simulation-assets/wrk_7cc36c48f5cc99a403bd01ef72858e8c), "a toolkit for generating realistic, procedurally generated articulated assets for robotics simulation, including procedural generators for 12 common articulated object categories". Code: [princeton-vl/infinigen](https://github.com/princeton-vl/infinigen); site: [infinigen.org](https://infinigen.org/). *Infinigen Indoors is the branch relevant to indoor CCTV scene generation, and it is Blender-based, not UE-based — a real integration decision.*

**Klaus Greff** and **Andrea Tagliasacchi** built **[Kubric (CVPR 2022)](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Greff_Kubric_A_Scalable_Dataset_Generator_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)**, "an open-source Python framework that interfaces with PyBullet and Blender to generate photo-realistic scenes with rich annotations" — [Google Research entry](https://research.google/pubs/kubric-a-scalable-dataset-generator/), [code](https://github.com/google-research/kubric), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03570). Greff was a Research Scientist at Google Brain and [also co-developed PaLM-E](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@klaus-greff); Tagliasacchi was at Google with an SFU affiliation and has since moved `[VERIFY — his Scholar is at least partly ServiceNow/SFU-associated, see the ServiceNow AI Research listing of the Kubric paper](https://www.servicenow.com/research/publication/klaus-greff-kubr-cvpr2022.html)`. Kubric's lasting contribution is not people-centric — it is the *annotation-completeness* standard (instance masks, depth, optical flow, object-centric tracks) and the reproducible-pipeline discipline. Kubric begat the MOVi benchmarks and the whole TAP (tracking-any-point) line `[VERIFY]`, which is a better foundation for dense person-motion evaluation than box-based MOT metrics.

### 9.5 The corporate-toolkit tradition: PeopleSansPeople (Unity) and Cosmos (NVIDIA)

**[PeopleSansPeople](https://unity-technologies.github.io/PeopleSansPeople/)** is "a human-centric privacy-preserving synthetic data generator created by Salehe Erfanian Ebadi, You-Cyuan Jhang, Alex Zook, Saurav Dhakad, Adam Crespi, Pete Parisi, Steve Borkman, Jonathan Hogins, and Sujoy Ganguly from Unity Technologies" ([arXiv 2112.09290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.09290)). It "contains simulation-ready 3D human assets, a parameterized lighting and camera system, and generates 2D and 3D bounding box, instance and semantic segmentation, and COCO pose labels", with "template Unity environment, benchmark binaries, and source code … available on GitHub" ([repo](https://github.com/Unity-Technologies/PeopleSansPeople)). **Salehe Erfanian Ebadi** ([dblp](https://dblp.org/pid/172/9660.html), [GitHub](https://github.com/salehe-ee)) is the person to follow. The framing word is *privacy-preserving* — this is the argument that gets synthetic data past a DPIA, and it is the argument this repository should make explicitly. Note also the Unity Perception team's subsequent contraction `[VERIFY]`: the tooling is excellent and the corporate commitment has been uneven, which is an argument for owning your own generator.

**NVIDIA Cosmos** is the 2025–26 frontier: [the Cosmos World Foundation Model Platform for Physical AI (January 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03575) with **Ming-Yu Liu**, [VP of generative AI research at NVIDIA](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2025-01_cosmos-world-foundation-model-platform-physical-ai), as the senior author among a very large NVIDIA team. The claim is that ["world foundation models can generate synthetic data, providing a rich, varied dataset that enhances the training process"](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/world-foundation-models-advance-physical-ai/), and that ["Cosmos-Predict2.5 was trained on 200M curated video clips and refined with reinforcement learning-based post-training, enabling more reliable synthetic data generation, policy evaluation, and closed-loop simulation"](https://www.edge-ai-vision.com/2025/03/nvidia-announces-major-release-of-cosmos-world-foundation-models-and-physical-ai-data-tools/). The follow-up is ["World Simulation with Video Foundation Models for Physical AI" (September 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00062), plus [Cosmos-Predict1](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/cosmos-lab/cosmos-predict1/) and [Cosmos Policy (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.16163). Models are [openly available](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/cosmos-world-foundation-models/). **The strategic read for this repository:** the generative-video route and the engine-rendering route are now in direct competition as synthetic-data sources, and they have opposite properties. Engines give you *exact* labels and controllability with a rendering-realism gap; video WFMs give you realism with *approximate or absent* labels and no guarantee of physical consistency. The right answer for multi-person tracking with ground-truth identities is almost certainly engine-primary with WFM-based appearance transfer as a post-process `[VERIFY — this is an editorial judgement, not a sourced claim]`.

## 10. Crowd analysis and camera calibration in the wild

### 10.1 Crowd counting and density

**Vishal M. Patel** (Johns Hopkins) and his student **Vishwanath A. Sindagi** define the current benchmark practice. Their [survey, "A Survey of Recent Advances in CNN-based Single Image Crowd Counting and Density Estimation" (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01202) is still the standard entry point, and [JHU-CROWD++ — "Pushing the Frontiers of Unconstrained Crowd Counting: New Dataset and Benchmark Method"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12384) is the dataset that introduced weather and image-quality labels into crowd counting — directly relevant to R04's degraded-imaging concerns. Sindagi's record is [here](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Vishwanath-A-Sindagi-2220075584) and [here](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@vishwanath-sindagi).

**Antoni B. Chan** (City University of Hong Kong) is the other pole and the more theoretically careful one; he has "contributed work on loss functions for crowd counting and localization" `[VERIFY for specific papers]` and his lineage runs back to Nuno Vasconcelos at UCSD `[VERIFY]` — the dynamic-texture crowd-modelling origin of the field. Other names visible in the current literature: the [spatial-invariance critique of CNN counting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.05253), [TransCrowd (weakly-supervised transformer counting)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.09116), [hierarchical scale recalibration](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.03545), [single-column defences](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.06133), and the smart-city [scale-attentive aggregation line](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3653454). A genuinely new 2025 direction is **[Embodied Crowd Counting](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08367v1)** — move the camera to resolve the count — which joins this subfield to Zhong's active-tracking line (§9.3) and Hou's camera-control line (§3). If SurvSim can render crowds with exact head positions at any density, embodied counting is a natural showcase task. Also note **PANDA**, [a gigapixel-level human-centric video dataset](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.04852) `[VERIFY authors — Tsinghua, Xueyang Fu/Lu Fang et al.]`, which is the right benchmark for wide-area crowd work. And the [visual-counting survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.03033) for a broader map.

**The old British crowd literature** — [Remagnino, Velastin, Xu, *Crowd analysis: A survey*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220464942_Crowd_analysis_A_survey) — plus PETS2009's crowd challenge (§8) is where the *operational* definitions (flow, density thresholds, stampede precursors) were set, and they are better definitions than most modern papers use.

### 10.2 Camera calibration in the wild

This is the least-populated and most under-rated subfield in the map, and it is a hard dependency for multi-camera tracking: [as R02 argues, calibration error is the dominant cause of cross-view association failure] `[internal cross-reference]`.

**Xiaoming Liu** (Michigan State) and **Shengjie Zhu** produced the canonical work: **[Tame a Wild Camera: In-the-Wild Monocular Camera Calibration (NeurIPS 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10988)** with **Abhinav Kumar** and **Masa Hu**. Its contribution: "calibrat[ing] the complete 4 Degree-of-Freedom (DoF) intrinsic parameters" from monocular 3D priors — depth and surface normals — or from a novel **incidence field**, "defined as the incidence rays between points in 3D space and pixels in the 2D imaging plane", explicitly rejecting "specific 3D objects or strong geometry assumptions like checkerboards or Manhattan World". Code: [ShngJZ/WildCamera](https://github.com/ShngJZ/WildCamera); [project page](https://shngjz.github.io/WildCamera.github.io/); [ML Anthology entry](https://mlanthology.org/neurips/2023/zhu2023neurips-tame/). For a CCTV retrofit programme — where you will *never* get a checkerboard onto a live concourse and the installer's notes are wrong — this is the most immediately deployable research in the whole dossier.

The 2025–26 direction is unification into multimodal camera reasoning: ["Thinking with Camera: A Unified Multimodal Model for Camera-Centric Understanding and Generation" (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08673). And the Oxford VGG line (§8) intersects here: [VGGT won Best Paper at CVPR 2025](https://x.com/oxford_vgg?lang=en) by doing feed-forward multi-view geometry — camera poses and geometry from unposed images — which is the calibration-free route to multi-camera person association `[VERIFY for the specific applicability]`. Watch for the combination of VGGT-style pose estimation with MVDet-style ground-plane aggregation; that is the most likely shape of the next multiview-detection state of the art, and it removes the manual-calibration cost that kills MTMC deployments.

`[VERIFY]` **Perspective Fields** (Jin Sun / Linyi Jin et al., CVPR 2023) and **DeepCalib** were named in the brief but not confirmed by search in this session.

## 11. Video–language retrieval and natural-language video search

This subfield is the substrate for "find me the man in the red jacket near the barrier at 14:20", and it is dominated by a single European lineage plus two industrial labs.

**Cordelia Schmid** (INRIA / Google) is the matriarch. Her [CV](https://cordeliaschmid.github.io/data/cordelia_cv.pdf) and [bibliography](https://dblp.org/pid/s/CordeliaSchmid.html?view=bibtex&param=2) document a fifty-year-scale influence — and recall from §1 that she co-advised Navneet Dalal's HOG thesis, which means the founder of pedestrian detection and the founder of video–language retrieval are the same supervisory node. Her students and long-term collaborators form the field:

- **Ivan Laptev** (INRIA Paris WILLOW → Professor at [MBZUAI](https://mbzuai.ac.ae/study/faculty/ivan-laptev/) from 2023 `[audit R4 2026-09-26: confirmed]`; also a SURREAL co-author) — space-time interest points, the ancestor of all action recognition.
- **Josef Sivic** (INRIA → Professor at [CIIRC CTU Prague](https://www.ciirc.cvut.cz/en/josef-sivic-byl-jmenovan-profesorem-v-oboru-informatika-a-umela-inteligence/), ERC Advanced Grant 2022 `[audit R4 2026-09-26: confirmed]`) — video Google, HowTo100M.
- **Gül Varol** (École des Ponts ParisTech) — [publication record](https://dblp.org/pid/142/3066.html), [Scholar](https://scholar.google.co.uk/citations?user=ceSzF9YAAAAJ&hl=th). She co-authored, with Schmid and **Lucas Ventura**, ["Learning Text-to-Video Retrieval from Image Captioning" (IJCV 2025)](https://mlanthology.org/authors/s/schmid-cordelia/) and ["Chapter-Llama: Efficient Chaptering in Hour-Long Videos with LLMs" (CVPR 2025)](https://mlanthology.org/authors/s/schmid-cordelia/). Chapter-Llama is the right prior art for long-archive video search: hour-long inputs, LLM-driven temporal segmentation.
- **Antoine Yang** ([record](https://mlanthology.org/authors/y/yang-antoine/)) — [Vid2Seq (CVPR 2023)](https://mlanthology.org/authors/s/schmid-cordelia/) dense video captioning and [VidChapters-7M (NeurIPS 2023)](https://mlanthology.org/authors/s/schmid-cordelia/).
- **Arsha Nagrani** (Oxford VGG under Zisserman → Google) — [record](https://mlanthology.org/authors/n/nagrani-arsha/); the VGG→Google pipeline for video-text.
- **CoVR: Learning Composed Video Retrieval from Web Video Captions (AAAI 2024)** — [same lineage](https://mlanthology.org/authors/s/schmid-cordelia/) — composed retrieval ("this person, but running") is the exact query form a surveillance operator wants.

**The current state of the art is industrial.** **[VideoPrism (ICML 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13217)** from Google/DeepMind is "a general-purpose video encoder designed to handle a wide spectrum of video understanding tasks, including classification, retrieval, localization, captioning, and question answering", by "Long Zhao, Nitesh B. Gundavarapu, Liangzhe Yuan, Hao Zhou, Shen Yan, Jennifer J. Sun, Luke Friedman, Rui Qian, Tobias Weyand, Yue Zhao, Rachel Hornung, Florian Schroff, Ming-Hsuan Yang, David A. Ross, Huisheng Wang, Hartwig Adam, Mikhail Sirotenko, Ting Liu, and Boqing Gong". Training scale: "1 billion image-text pairs from WebLI, 36 million high-quality video-text pairs, and 582 million video clips with noisy or machine-generated parallel text", achieving "state-of-the-art performance on 31 out of 33 public video understanding benchmarks using a single frozen model" ([Google blog](https://research.google/blog/videoprism-a-foundational-visual-encoder-for-video-understanding/), [code](https://github.com/google-deepmind/videoprism), [weights on HuggingFace](https://huggingface.co/google/videoprism-base-f16r288/blob/7288dcfb37819842dce2e6dc3bcd56e78161b937/README.md)). **Long Zhao** has since [moved to xAI](https://garyzhao.github.io/) — worth knowing, because it means the VideoPrism team is dispersing. **Ming-Hsuan Yang** (UC Merced/Google) and **Ting Liu** and **Boqing Gong** are the senior anchors `[VERIFY for current roles]`.

**Shanghai AI Laboratory** is the open-source counterweight. **Yu Qiao** is [a Professor at Shanghai AI Laboratory](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@yu-qiao) whose "work on foundation models includes InternVL and InternImage, as well as the video generative benchmarks VBench and MVBench". The **InternVideo** series is the most important openly-released video-foundation line: [InternVid (a large-scale video-text dataset)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.06942v2), [InternVideo2 — "a new family of video foundation models (ViFM) that achieve the state-of-the-art results in video recognition, video-text tasks, and video-centric dialogue"](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15377v3), [VideoChat (chat-centric video understanding)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11432-024-4321-9), and [InternVideo2.5, open-sourced February 2025 by Shanghai AI Lab with Nanjing University and SIAT, whose "temporal span and fine granularity … expand[ed] its capacity sixfold compared to the previous model"](https://cs.nju.edu.cn/lm/en/post/2025-02-11-internvideo-25-release/index.html) — framed as finding "the needle in a haystack in tens of thousands of frames, with fine-grained spatiotemporal perception", which is verbatim the archive-search problem.

**Evaluation caution.** The subfield's own literature is now sceptical of its benchmarks: ["Breaking Down Video LLM Benchmarks: Knowledge, Spatial Perception, or True Temporal Understanding?" (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.14321) argues much apparent video understanding is static-frame knowledge. See also the [auxiliary-information review for text-to-video retrieval (2025)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23952), [long-video narrative structure work](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.19706), [generative-recall retrieval (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.21193), the [unified-embedding retrieval line (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.12193), and [the AI-generated-video evaluation survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.19884). For person-centric text retrieval specifically, the [review of person retrieval in surveillance using textual query](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.02414) is the domain-specific entry point.

**Meta's contribution** is segmentation rather than retrieval, but it is the most-deployed video model of the period: **[SAM 2: Segment Anything in Images and Videos (ICLR 2025 oral)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.00714)**, by "Nikhila Ravi, Valentin Gabeur, Yuan-Ting Hu, Ronghang Hu, Chaitanya Ryali, Tengyu Ma, Haitham Khedr, Roman Rädle, Chloe Rolland, Laura Gustafson, Eric Mintun, Junting Pan, Kalyan Vasudev Alwala, Nicolas Carion, Chao-Yuan Wu, Ross Girshick, Piotr Dollár, and Christoph Feichtenhofer". The authors "built a data engine to collect the largest video segmentation dataset to date", using "a simple transformer architecture with streaming memory for real-time video processing", achieving "better accuracy using 3× fewer interactions than prior approaches" in video and "6× faster" than SAM in images ([Meta page](https://ai.meta.com/research/publications/sam-2-segment-anything-in-images-and-videos/), [ICLR oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/oral/31878), [code](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam2)). **Christoph Feichtenhofer** is the node that connects this to §2 — he co-authored TrackFormer with Leal-Taixé — and **Nikhila Ravi** is the lead to follow. The practical use for a synthetic-data project is *auto-annotation of real video* to build evaluation sets cheaply, and mask-level supervision that engines give you for free.

## 12. Surveillance human factors — the smallest community, the largest operational effect

This is the literature that decides whether any of the above matters, and it is almost entirely disjoint from the computer-vision community: different venues (*Applied Ergonomics*, *Security Journal*, *Human Factors*), different authors, near-zero cross-citation.

The core empirical finding is bleak and consistent: **operators miss roughly half of what they are looking for**. In controlled CCTV-monitoring studies, ["operators detected 50% of target behaviors with high false alarm rates, indicating significant room for improvement"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289672475_Vigilance_decrements_in_closed_circuit_television_CCTV_surveillance). The vigilance-decrement literature is more nuanced than the folk version: [*Work exposure and vigilance decrements in closed circuit television surveillance* (Applied Ergonomics, 2014)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0003687014001847) identified "two types of CCTV surveillance functions through observation and discussion with operators and managers, resulting in the identification of 'generalist' and 'specialist' surveillance", and found that "vigilance decrements were found for novices and generalists, but specialists maintained their performance for the first hour and then increased it" ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25479991/), [full text](https://www.academia.edu/20740106/Work_exposure_and_vigilance_decrements_in_closed_circuit_television_surveillance)). That is a training-and-role-design finding, not a technology finding, and it should govern how an alerting system is specified: a specialist with a defined target maintains performance; a generalist watching everything degrades.

The second key mechanism is **inattentional blindness**, not fatigue: [*Pirate Stealth or Inattentional Blindness? The Effects of Target Relevance and Sustained Attention on Security Monitoring for Experienced and Naïve Operators*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3897661/) shows that target *relevance* — whether the operator expects this kind of event — dominates. An analytic that fires on a class the operator does not believe in will be ignored.

**Key people and sources `[VERIFY for individual attributions — these came from search snippets, and the human-factors names were not individually confirmed in this session]`:** the **Tickner** studies of the 1970s Home Office era are the foundational British work referenced as ["foundational work by Tickner and colleagues on CCTV monitoring"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/289672475_Vigilance_decrements_in_closed_circuit_television_CCTV_surveillance). **Craig Donald** (South Africa) is the practitioner-author of the specialist/generalist distinction `[VERIFY]`. The review literature to read: [*See No Evil: Cognitive Challenges of Security Surveillance and Monitoring* (Journal of Applied Research in Memory and Cognition, 2017)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2211368117300207) and [*Is CCTV Surveillance as Effective as Popular Television Crime Series Suggest? Cognitive Challenges* (2018)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-319-96077-7_44). On the training side, [*CCTV: A challenging case for threat image projection implementation* (Security Journal, 2012)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/sj.2012.49) asks whether the aviation-security TIP method (inserting synthetic threats into live imagery to keep screeners calibrated) can transfer to CCTV. **That paper is the single most important human-factors reference for this repository**, because TIP-for-CCTV *is* a synthetic-imagery product: if SurvSim can composite plausible synthetic persons and events into live or recorded operational feeds, it is a training and operator-calibration tool, not only a model-training tool. That is a materially different and possibly better business case than "more training data".

Finally, the one CV paper in this cluster: [*Automated Detection of Firearms and Knives in a CCTV Image*](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4732080/) (Grega et al.) — a useful reality check on what object detection achieves on real CCTV resolution for small, concealed, safety-critical objects.

`[VERIFY]` **The observation to carry forward.** The human-factors community has measured what a human operator can do. The CV community has measured what a model can do. Almost nobody has measured the *joint* system — human plus alerting analytic — under realistic base rates. That gap is where a defensible product claim lives, and it needs an experiment, not a benchmark.

## 13. OpenCV, autonomy and the broader industrial nodes

**Gary Bradski** created OpenCV at Intel and remains the field's most consequential infrastructure builder. He is [currently Senior Fellow at Gauss Surgical and CEO of OpenCV.ai](https://www.linkedin.com/in/garybradski/); see his [IEEE-RAS roboticist profile](https://www.ieee-ras.org/roboticshistory/roboticist-detail/roboticistprofile.html?profileid=110) and [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Bradski). **Alex Kendall** is the exemplar of the Cambridge-to-unicorn path. ## 14. Neuromorphic and event-based vision

The relevance here is specific: event cameras give microsecond temporal resolution, 120 dB+ dynamic range and very low power, which addresses precisely the CCTV failure modes R04 documents — motion blur at night, backlit doorways, and power/bandwidth budgets on pole-mounted cameras.

**The Zurich institutional core.** [Giacomo Indiveri is a Professor at the Faculty of Science of the University of Zurich and director of the Institute of Neuroinformatics (INI)](https://cmc-dresden.org/members/giacomo-indiveri/) ([Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=kdHjCAMAAAAJ&hl=en)). [Tobi Delbruck earned his PhD from Caltech in 1993 and since 1998 has been a Professor of Physics and Electrical Engineering at ETH Zurich, leading a group focused on neuromorphic sensors, neural hardware, machine vision, and neural robotic control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Delbruck) ([home page](https://tilde.ini.uzh.ch/~tobi/)). [Indiveri and Delbruck, with Shih-Chii Liu, form the "hardware groups" at INI](https://tilde.ini.uzh.ch/~tobi/) — the University of Zurich / ETH joint institute. Delbruck [has founded three spin-off companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_Delbruck) `[VERIFY names — iniVation and Insightness are the widely cited two]`, and the DVS/DAVIS sensor line he co-created is the hardware every event-vision paper uses. **Ning Qiao** is [CEO of SynSense](https://tilde.ini.uzh.ch/~tobi/), the INI-descended neuromorphic-processor company — the commercialisation node of the Indiveri line. **Shih-Chii Liu** is the third INI hardware PI, and the silicon-cochlea/audio-sensing branch is directly relevant to the audio-analytics thread in R05 `[VERIFY]`. The academic ancestor above all of them is **Carver Mead** at Caltech, whose students Delbruck and Misha Mahowald created the field `[VERIFY]`.

**Davide Scaramuzza** (University of Zurich) is the person who made event cameras *useful*. He is [Professor of Robotics and Perception at UZH, whose research focuses on autonomous, agile navigation of mobile robots using standard and event-based cameras](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/people_scaramuzza.html) ([group site](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/), [event-vision page](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/research_dvs.html), [publications](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/publications.html), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Scaramuzza)). He delivered [an IROS 2025 keynote on "current development trends and application opportunities for event cameras … ranging from robotics to virtual reality and smartphones, while also exploring current challenges and future development directions"](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/) and [a Swiss Robotics Day 2025 keynote on the Nature drone-racing work](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/) — those two talks are the best available single-sitting orientation to the field's state in 2025. His [student-projects page](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/student_projects.php) currently advertises "event-based cameras for spacecraft navigation … [because] event cameras offer significant benefits in difficult robotic scenarios characterized by high-dynamic range and rapid motion". **Daniel Gehrig** is the most visible RPG descendant on event-based learning ([page](https://danielgehrig18.github.io/)); **Guillermo Gallego** (now TU Berlin) is the other, and author of the standard event-vision survey `[VERIFY]`. RPG's earliest event-tracking work — [*Event-based, 6-DOF Camera Tracking from Photometric Depth Maps*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.03468) — is still instructive on what the modality gives you.

**The large-scale hardware programmes.** **Mike Davies** is [director of the Neuromorphic Computing Lab at Intel Labs](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai), which built **Hala Point**, initially deployed at Sandia: it ["uses 1,152 of Intel's Loihi 2 processors, stitched together in a three-dimensional grid for a total of 1.15 billion neurons, 128 billion synapses, 140,544 processing cores, and 2,300 embedded x86 cores", achieving "over 10 times more neuron capacity and up to 12 times higher performance" than Pohoiki Springs](https://download.intel.com/newsroom/archive/2025/en-us-2024-04-17-intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai.pdf). The headline efficiency claim: ["Loihi-based systems can perform AI inference and solve optimization problems using 100 times less energy at speeds as much as 50 times faster than conventional CPU and GPU architectures"](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai) — read that as a claim about *sparse, event-driven* workloads specifically, not dense CNN inference. Coverage: [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2024/04/17/intel_hala_point_neuromorphic_owl/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/intel-unveils-hala-point-its-second-generation-neuromorphic-computing-system), [Hackster](https://www.hackster.io/news/intel-aims-for-efficient-brain-scale-neuromorphic-ai-research-with-the-loihi-2-powered-hala-point-5706be254be6), [Sandia](https://newsreleases.sandia.gov/artificial_neuron/), [investor release](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1691/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to).

**Steve Furber** (University of Manchester) is the UK's neuromorphic founder and the most distinguished computer architect in this map: [he is "the principal designer of the ARM microprocessor and the creator of the SpiNNaker neuromorphic computer architecture"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpiNNaker), and the SpiNNaker project, ["20 years in conception and 15 in construction, has delivered the world's largest neuromorphic computing platform incorporating over a million ARM mobile phone processors and capable of modelling spiking neural networks of the scale of a mouse brain in biological real time"](https://library.oapen.org/handle/20.500.12657/47874) ([his profile](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Steve-Furber); [his book](https://www.barnesandnoble.com/w/spinnaker-a-spiking-neural-network-architecture-steve-furber/1136624563); [HBP interview](https://www.humanbrainproject.eu/en/follow-hbp/news/2023/09/01/neuromorphic-computing-and-human-brain-project-interview-prof-steve-furber-spinnaker-and-cross-disciplinary-brain-research/)). The successor, [SpiNNaker2 — "a large-scale neuromorphic system for event-based and asynchronous machine learning"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.04491) — is a Dresden (TU Dresden / SpiNNcloud) continuation under Christian Mayr `[VERIFY]`. The community's own sober self-assessment is worth reading: ["Demonstrating Advantages of Neuromorphic Computation: A Pilot Study"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.03618) and [IEEE Spectrum's "Neuromorphic Computing Is Ready for the Big Time"](https://spectrum.ieee.org/neuromorphic-computing-2671121824).

**The learning-algorithm side.** **Emre Neftci** is [affiliated with Forschungszentrum Jülich and is a Professor at RWTH Aachen](https://www.researchgate.net/scientific-contributions/Emre-O-Neftci-2144535340), with 2025 output including ["Unsupervised online learning of complex sequences in spiking neuronal networks" (CVPR 2025), with Younes Bouhadjar, Melissa Lober, Markus Diesmann and Tom Tetzlaff](https://dblp.org/pid/62/5283.html) and AICAS 2025 work "on event-driven accelerators for SNN inference and efficient event-based object detection with hybrid neural networks" — that last item is the one this repository cares about: *hybrid* ANN/SNN detectors are the realistic near-term deployment shape. His surrogate-gradient line with **Friedemann Zenke** (whose [lab tracks SNN work publicly](https://zenkelab.org/tag/spiking-neural-networks/)) and Hesham Mostafa is what made deep SNNs trainable `[VERIFY]`. **Wolfgang Maass** (TU Graz) is the theorist — liquid state machines, and a [2025 Nature Communications paper on "a simple model for behavioral time scale synaptic plasticity (BTSP) [providing] content addressable memory with binary synapses and one-shot learning"](https://igi-web.tugraz.at/people/maass/publications.html). Current methodological debates visible in the 2025–26 literature: [SNNs needing high-frequency information](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.18608), [multiplication-free parallelizable spiking neurons](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14490), [scalable hybrid training for recurrent SNNs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14464), [stable learning with affine encoders/decoders](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.04549), [mapping SNNs to hardware](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.01843), and the broad [NeuroAI and Beyond position paper (2026)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.19955). The [SNN Workshop](https://sites.google.com/view/snn-workshop/) series is the community's meeting point.

**Why this matters to SurvSim.** Event data is *exactly* the modality where synthetic generation is most defensible, because the sensor model is simple (log-intensity threshold crossings) and simulators (ESIM/v2e — the latter by Yuhuang Hu, Shih-Chii Liu and Tobi Delbruck at INI Zurich, CVPRW 2021, per the [v2e README](https://github.com/SensorsINI/v2e) `[audit R4 2026-09-26: confirmed]`) are well validated. If the repository wants a subfield where synthetic-to-real transfer is least contested, event-based person detection is it. The counterweight is that [event-based person ReID is only now getting benchmarks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.13659), so there is little to compare against.

## 16. Conferences, workshops and venues — the 2026 calendar and what each is for

**Tier-1 general vision (where the field's canon is set).**

- **CVPR** — June, US. The main venue for everything in this dossier. Deadline typically November. [CVPR 2025 hosted the VGGT best paper](https://x.com/oxford_vgg?lang=en). Historically the home of the AI City Challenge workshop.
- **ICCV** — odd years, October. [ICCV 2025 hosted the 9th AI City Challenge](https://iccv.thecvf.com/virtual/2025/workshop/2832) and [UnrealZoo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.20977). Deadline typically March.
- **ECCV** — even years, autumn. ByteTrack (2022) and MVDet (2020) were ECCV papers; [FoundationGait is an ECCV 2026 Spotlight](https://github.com/ShiqiYu/OpenGait). Deadline typically March.
- **NeurIPS** — December. Now the primary venue for *datasets*: [BEDLAM2.0 at the 2025 Datasets & Benchmarks track](https://bedlam2.is.tuebingen.mpg.de/), [WildCamera at NeurIPS 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10988), [BiggerGait at NeurIPS 2025](https://github.com/ShiqiYu/OpenGait). If you release a synthetic dataset, this is the target.
- **ICLR** — spring. [SAM 2 was an ICLR 2025 oral](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2025/oral/31878). Increasingly where foundation-model video work lands.
- **ICML** — July. [VideoPrism, ICML 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13217).
- **WACV** — winter/early spring, applications-focused. The most welcoming tier-1-adjacent venue for surveillance work: [MEVA appeared at WACV 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2021/papers/Corona_MEVA_A_Large-Scale_Multiview_Multimodal_Video_Dataset_for_Activity_Detection_WACV_2021_paper.pdf), and [WACV 2026's workshop list](https://wacv.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/Workshops), [organisers](https://wacv.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/Organizers) and [call for workshops](https://wacv.thecvf.com/Conferences/2026/CallForWorkshops) are public. **This is the right first venue for a SurvSim paper.**

- **AVSS — IEEE International Conference on Advanced Video and Signal-Based Surveillance.** The field's home conference. **Practical calendar advice.** The realistic annual rhythm for a project like this is: submit the dataset/benchmark to **WACV RWS** (autumn deadline) for community contact, the methodological result to **AVSS** (spring deadline, Lecce in 2026) for the surveillance audience, the full dataset to **NeurIPS D&B** (May–June deadline) for citation volume, and enter **AI City Challenge** (registration typically January–February) for a credibility-conferring leaderboard placement. Do not aim first at CVPR; the surveillance-application framing is systematically disadvantaged there `[VERIFY — editorial judgement]`.

**Chain 1 — MOTChallenge → NVIDIA (8 hops).**
[MOT15/MOT16 benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.01942) → authors Leal-Taixé, Milan, Reid, Roth, Schindler → Leal-Taixé founds [TUM Dynamic Vision and Learning](https://dvl.cit.tum.de/team/lealtaixe/) → her student [Guillem Brasó](https://www.alphaxiv.org/@guillem-braso) writes [the neural solver](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07515) → Brasó co-authors [MOTSynth](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Matteo-Fabbri) with Modena's Fabbri and Cucchiara → Leal-Taixé becomes [Senior Research Manager at NVIDIA](https://dvl.cit.tum.de/team/lealtaixe/) and Brasó joins NVIDIA → NVIDIA's other arm runs [the AI City Challenge under Zheng Tang](https://www.linkedin.com/in/zhengthomastang/) with the [Metropolis multi-camera workflow shipped at CES'25/GTC'25](https://www.linkedin.com/in/zhengthomastang/) → and NVIDIA ships [Cosmos world foundation models for synthetic physical-AI data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03575). *Net: the people who defined how MOT is measured now work for the company selling both the synthetic data and the multi-camera tracking product.*
