## 1. History: from Hodgkin–Huxley to Hala Point

### 1.1 The biophysics layer (1952–1990)

The lineage starts with **Hodgkin and Huxley (1952)**, whose four-variable conductance model of the squid giant axon — membrane capacitance in parallel with voltage-gated sodium and potassium conductances and a leak — earned the 1963 Nobel Prize and remains the reference against which every simplification is judged. **[lore]** Its cost is the point: four coupled nonlinear ODEs per neuron, sub-microsecond integration steps near spike onset. Nobody builds a perception system out of it. Intermediate reductions matter historically — **FitzHugh–Nagumo** (two-variable phase-plane caricature), **Morris–Lecar**, **Hindmarsh–Rose**, and **Izhikevich (2003)**, whose two-variable quadratic model reproduces about twenty distinct cortical firing patterns at roughly 13 floating-point operations per neuron per millisecond, making it the historical sweet spot for *neuroscience* simulation. **[VERIFY]** the exact FLOP figure, which is from Izhikevich's own comparison table.

The model that actually underpins the engineering field is much older and much cruder: the **leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF)** neuron, whose integrate-and-fire ancestor is **Lapicque (1907)**. Membrane potential leaks toward rest, accumulates weighted input, emits a spike and resets when it crosses threshold. In discrete time:

```
V[t] = β·V[t-1] + Σ_i w_i · s_i[t]        (β = exp(-Δt/τ_m), the leak)
s[t] = Θ(V[t] - V_th)                      (Heaviside spike)
V[t] ← V[t] - V_th · s[t]                  (soft reset)   or   V[t] ← V_reset · (1-s[t])   (hard reset)
```

**[INF] Why this matters for reading [vendor product].** Essentially every commercial neuromorphic accelerator in 2026 implements LIF or a close cousin (adaptive LIF, current-based LIF, or a programmable microcode neuron that can express them). SynSense's Xylo is described explicitly as a chip "*designed to efficiently simulate leaky integrate-and-fire (LIF) neurons to implement deep spiking neural networks for edge processing applications*" ([SynSense Xylo](https://www.synsense.ai/products/xylo/)). So when a vendor says "spiking neural network", the safe default assumption is *LIF units, discrete timesteps, surrogate-gradient training* — not Hodgkin–Huxley biophysics. A good, non-hostile question to ask the vendor is simply: **"what neuron model, and how many timesteps at inference?"** The answer tells you nearly everything about the real architecture, and it is a question an insider asks.

### 1.2 Maass 1997 and the "third generation" frame

The framing device the whole field still uses comes from Wolfgang Maass: **"Networks of spiking neurons: The third generation of neural network models"**, *Neural Networks* 10(9):1659–1671, 1997 ([dblp record](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/nn/Maass97.html)). First generation = McCulloch–Pitts threshold units; second = sigmoidal/rate-coded units trained by backprop (today's ANNs); third = units communicating with *timed binary events*. As the modern literature restates it, "*spiking neural networks (SNNs) are considered as the third generation of neural network models. The biology-inspired binary spike transmitted mechanism brings SNNs multiple advantages and high potential for fast inference and low computation cost on neuromorphic architectures*" ([Research Advances and New Paradigms for Biology-inspired SNNs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.13996)).

**[INF] Note what the marketing does with this.** "Second generation SNN", the vendor's phrasing for [vendor product], is *not* Maass's generation numbering — Maass's third generation *is* SNNs. the vendor means "our second product generation". Do not confuse them in conversation, and do not correct anyone about it either; just be precise in your own use.

Maass's substantive contributions beyond the taxonomy: proofs that spiking neurons with temporal coding can be *computationally more powerful per unit* than rate-coded sigmoidal units for certain functions, and the observation that a single well-timed spike can carry information that a rate code needs a whole window to convey. That result is the theoretical seed of **latency/time-to-first-spike coding**, which is where the genuine latency and energy wins live (§4.1).

### 1.3 Plasticity: STDP and the local-learning dream

**Spike-timing-dependent plasticity** — Markram et al. (1997), Bi & Poo (1998) — is the empirical finding that synaptic change depends on the *relative timing* of pre- and post-synaptic spikes on a tens-of-milliseconds scale: pre-before-post potentiates, post-before-pre depresses. **[lore]** Its appeal to engineers is that it is *local*: a synapse needs only its own two spike trains, no global error signal, no backward pass, no stored activations. That is exactly what on-chip learning hardware can implement.

Its problem, equally, is well established by 2026: **unsupervised STDP does not scale to deep networks or hard discriminative tasks.** Diehl & Cook's 2015 STDP MNIST network reached roughly 95% — respectable in 2015, uncompetitive now — and attempts to stack STDP layers to ImageNet-scale accuracy have not produced anything close to gradient-trained SNNs. [audit 2026-09-26: 95% with 6,400 excitatory neurons confirmed in the Frontiers in Comput. Neurosci. 2015 abstract] Modern work has largely moved to gradient methods (§2), with local rules surviving in three niches: **(i)** on-chip few-shot adaptation on top of a frozen gradient-trained backbone; **(ii)** biologically-motivated research; **(iii)** unsupervised feature learning where labels are absent. A 2026 survey benchmarking this space exists — "*NeuroTrain: Surveying Local Learning Rules for Spiking Neural Networks with an Open Benchmarking Framework*" ([arXiv 2605.15058](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15058)) — and is the right entry point if you need to argue the case either way. **[VERIFY]** its contents; I have only its title and abstract framing from the index.

**[INF] This is the single most important piece of context for [vendor product]'s "low shot learning" claim.** Niche (i) is a real, defensible, *deployable* capability: pre-train a spiking backbone on a large corpus, then adapt the readout on-device from a handful of examples using a local rule or a tiny gradient step. It is also precisely the pattern that makes a synthetic-data generator valuable, because the pre-training corpus is the expensive part and synthetic data is how you get it. If the vendor's low-shot claim is niche (i), our repository is directly on the critical path. If the claim is "STDP learns new target classes from three examples with no pre-training", that would be an extraordinary claim requiring extraordinary evidence. **Ask which.**

### 1.4 Reservoirs: liquid state machines

Maass, Natschläger & Markram (2002) proposed the **liquid state machine (LSM)**: a fixed, randomly-connected recurrent pool of spiking neurons whose high-dimensional transient dynamics act as a temporal kernel, with only a simple linear readout trained. Echo State Networks (Jaeger, 2001) are the rate-coded twin; together they are "reservoir computing".

**[INF] Do not skip this as history.** LSMs are *disproportionately* represented in exactly the application the vendor advertises. The radar-SNN literature retrieved for §3.4 describes an approach where "*spike trains are fed into spiking recurrent neural networks (liquid state machines), with readout signals used for various classifiers including logistic regression, random forest, and SVM*" ([search synthesis over radar SNN literature](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352328411_Radar-Based_Hand_Gesture_Recognition_Using_Spiking_Neural_Networks)). The reason is structural: for a *low-dimensional, strongly temporal* signal — a radar Doppler bin sequence, an RF pulse train, an audio envelope — a fixed reservoir plus a trained linear readout is cheap, needs few labelled examples, trains in seconds, and maps naturally onto fixed neuromorphic fabric. **A reservoir with a retrained readout is also the most plausible concrete mechanism behind a "data-agnostic, low-shot" multi-sensor engine** (§4.4). That is an inference, not a finding, but it is the one I would bet on.

### 1.5 Carver Mead and the birth of "neuromorphic"

Carver Mead published **"Neuromorphic electronic systems"** in *Proceedings of the IEEE*, 1990, and "*around 1989–1990, Mead formalized this approach by coining the term 'neuromorphic', which denoted electronic systems whose architecture and operational principles were directly inspired by biological nervous systems*" ([search synthesis; see Caltech's Mead bibliography](https://feeds.library.caltech.edu/people/Mead-C-A/combined.html)). The founding artefact is the **silicon retina**: Mead and his student **Misha Mahowald** described the first analog silicon retina in "A Silicon Model of Early Visual Processing", *Neural Networks* 1 (1988), followed by "The Silicon Retina" in *Scientific American* (1991). It "*used analog electrical circuits to mimic the biological functions of rod cells, cone cells, and other non-photoreceptive cells in the retina of the eye*". Mead himself is on record that "*the silicon retina was Misha's idea, and she basically dragged me into neurobiology*" ([Misha Mahowald — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misha_Mahowald); see also [Neuromorphic Engineering: In Memory of Misha Mahowald](https://direct.mit.edu/neco/article/35/3/343/113812/Neuromorphic-Engineering-In-Memory-of-Misha), *Neural Computation* 35(3), 2023). Mead received a lifetime contribution award for neuromorphic engineering ([Caltech](https://www.caltech.edu/about/news/carver-mead-earns-lifetime-contribution-award-for-neuromorphic-engineering)).

Mahowald died in 1996. **[INF]** Mention her, not just Mead, if the topic comes up: it signals you have read the field's history rather than its press releases, and the 2023 *Neural Computation* memorial issue is a credible thing to have read.

The direct engineering descendant of the silicon retina is the **DVS** (Dynamic Vision Sensor) of Lichtsteiner, Posch and Delbrück (2008) — the first practically usable event camera, and the ancestor of everything in §1.7.

### 1.6 The digital neuromorphic chips

**SpiNNaker (Manchester, Steve Furber).** A deliberately un-clever design: don't build neuron circuits, build a very large number of small ARM cores with a packet-switched fabric optimised for tiny spike packets, and simulate neurons in software. SpiNNaker 1 reached a million ARM968 cores. **SpiNNaker 2**, developed at TU Dresden under Christian Mayr and commercialised by the 2021 spin-out **SpiNNcloud**, is where the line is in 2026: the TU Dresden machine "*comprises 35,000 chips and over five million processor cores*", and "*SpiNNcloud is now offering a five-billion neuron supercomputer in the cloud*" ([TU Dresden](https://tu-dresden.de/tu-dresden/newsportal/news/milestone-for-energy-efficient-ai-systems-tud-launches-spinncloud-supercomputer); [EE Times](https://www.eetimes.com/spinnaker-based-neuromorphic-supercomputer-launches-in-dresden/)). Furber is "*the designer of the original ARM architecture*" ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/spinncloud-to-deploy-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-supercomputer-at-leipzig-university/)) — a useful fact, because it explains the design philosophy: a systems architect's answer to neuromorphic computing, not a device physicist's.

Deployment matters more than peak specs for our purposes. In June 2025 "*Sandia National Laboratories deployed a SpiNNaker2-based neuromorphic computing system to explore energy-efficient architectures for artificial intelligence and national security applications*" ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/sandia-deploys-spinnaker2-neuromorphic-system-from-spinncloud/); [The Next Platform](https://www.nextplatform.com/2025/06/16/sandia-deploys-spinnaker2-neuromorphic-system/)). In November 2025 SpiNNaker2 was selected for the NSF-funded **Neuromorphic Commons (THOR)** project, "*with capacity to simulate more than 393 million neurons, making it one of the four largest neuromorphic supercomputers in the world*" ([Silicon Saxony](https://silicon-saxony.de/en/spinncloud-us-research-initiative-thor-equipped-with-neuromorphic-supercomputer-milestone-for-brain-inspired-computing/)). **[INF]** "National security applications" appearing verbatim in a Sandia press release is the clearest public signal that defence interest in neuromorphic compute is programmatic, not speculative — worth having at hand.

**IBM TrueNorth (2014).** Merolla, Arthur, Alvarez-Icaza et al., "A million spiking-neuron integrated circuit with a scalable communication network and interface", *Science* 345:668, 8 Aug 2014, [DOI 10.1126/science.1254642](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25104385/). The chip "*contains 4096 neurosynaptic cores interconnected via an intrachip network that integrates 1 million programmable spiking neurons and 256 million configurable synapses*" and, per the *Science* abstract, runs 400x240 video at 30 fps on **63 mW** (the "70 mW" figure is press/Wikipedia, not the paper) `[audit R3 2026-09-26]`; the companion TCAD paper is titled "TrueNorth: Design and Tool Flow of a **65 mW** 1 Million Neuron Programmable Neurosynaptic Chip" ([IBM Research](https://research.ibm.com/publications/truenorth-design-and-tool-flow-of-a-65-mw-1-million-neuron-programmable-neurosynaptic-chip)). **[INF] The 63/65 mW discrepancy (and the 70 mW press figure) is not an error — it is the lesson.** Different papers on the same silicon quote different power because the workload, spike rate and measurement boundary differ. This is the *first* thing to remember whenever anyone quotes a neuromorphic power number, including us in §5.5.

TrueNorth's historical significance is twofold: it proved million-neuron digital integration at sub-100 mW, and it demonstrated the field's characteristic failure mode — it had **no on-chip learning** and a constrained, unusual programming model (binary/ternary synapses, stochastic rounding, IBM's Corelet toolchain). Networks had to be trained offline and mapped in, and the mapping was painful enough that the ecosystem never broadened. **Keep that in mind as the benchmark for what "engineering grade" has to mean** (§4.5): the technical achievement was real and the deployment ecosystem still did not materialise.

**Intel Loihi 1 (2018) and Loihi 2 (2021), Mike Davies' group.** Loihi 1: 128 neuromorphic cores (+3 x86), ~130k neurons, ~130M synapses, 14 nm [**secondary only** — Open Neuromorphic; the Davies 2018 IEEE Micro primary was not reachable `[audit R3 2026-09-26]`], and crucially **programmable on-chip learning rules** including STDP variants and three-factor rules. Loihi 2 moved to Intel 4, added *programmable neuron models* (microcode rather than fixed LIF) and **graded spikes** — spikes carrying a small integer payload rather than a pure binary event. **[INF] Graded spikes are an under-appreciated tell.** They are an admission that pure binary spiking costs too much accuracy or too many timesteps for real workloads, and they are the hardware-side mirror of the algorithmic trend in §2.4 (SpikeYOLO's integer-valued I-LIF). A single Loihi 2 chip "*supports up to 1 million neurons and 120 million synapses*" ([search synthesis over Intel and Open Neuromorphic material](https://open-neuromorphic.org/neuromorphic-computing/hardware/loihi-2-intel/)).

**Hala Point (April 2024)** is the system-level statement. It "*contains 1.15 billion neurons and packages 1,152 Loihi 2 processors produced on Intel 4 process node in a six-rack-unit data center chassis the size of a microwave oven*", "*supports up to 128 billion synapses distributed over 140,544 neuromorphic processing cores, consuming a maximum of 2,600 watts of power*", is "*capable of supporting up to 20 quadrillion operations per second, or 20 petaops*", "*achieves up to 12 times higher performance compared to Intel's initial neuromorphic system, Pohoiki Springs*", reaches "*15 TOPS/W at INT8*", includes "*over 2,300 embedded x86 processors for ancillary computations*", and was "*initially deployed at Sandia National Laboratories*" ([Intel newsroom](https://newsroom.intel.com/artificial-intelligence/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to-enable-more-sustainable-ai); [Intel IR](https://www.intc.com/news-events/press-releases/detail/1691/intel-builds-worlds-largest-neuromorphic-system-to); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/intel-unveils-115bn-neuron-neuromorphic-system-hala-point/)).

**[INF] Read those numbers adversarially, because they are the single most-quoted set in the field.** 1.15 billion neurons at 2.6 kW is roughly 2.3 µW per neuron and is genuinely remarkable *for spiking workloads with low activity*. But "1.15 billion neurons" is a capacity figure, not a utilisation figure; "20 petaops" is a peak; and **15 TOPS/W at INT8 is not an extraordinary number in 2026** — commodity edge inference accelerators are in the same order of magnitude, and datacentre parts on newer nodes compete on that metric directly. The defensible version of Intel's claim is not "neuromorphic beats GPUs at dense matrix multiply" (it does not) but "for sparse, recurrent, temporally-structured, low-activity workloads there is a large efficiency gap". That is a much narrower and much more honest claim, and it is the one to make in a room with an experienced CTO.

**BrainScaleS (Heidelberg, Karlheinz Meier's legacy group).** The analog counter-position: build actual physical neuron circuits whose dynamics run **~1000× faster than biology** rather than in real time. **BrainScaleS-2** is "*an accelerated spiking neuromorphic system-on-chip integrating 512 adaptive integrate-and-fire neurons, 131k plastic synapses, embedded processors, and event routing*", and "*the 1000× acceleration factor means a 100-second biological training session is completed in just 100 milliseconds, enabling rapid parameter sweeps and large-scale statistical studies*" ([Open Neuromorphic](https://open-neuromorphic.org/neuromorphic-computing/hardware/brainscales-2-universitat-heidelberg/); [Frontiers in Neuroscience 2024, "Scalable network emulation on analog neuromorphic hardware"](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnins.2024.1523331/full)). 2024 work "*extended the system with a configurable realtime event interface that enables a tight coupling of its distinct analog network core to external sensors and actuators*", aimed at "*high-speed robotic applications that require precise timing on a microsecond scale*" ([EBRAINS](https://www.ebrains.eu/news-and-events/ebrains-neuromorphic-platform-brainscales-2-adds-new-interface-for-high-speed-robotics)).

**[INF]** 512 neurons is the number to hold on to. Analog neuromorphic is scientifically the most interesting branch and the *least* relevant to a 2026 ISR product: device mismatch, calibration burden and tiny neuron counts make it a research instrument. If [vendor product] is analog, that would be a genuine surprise and a genuine risk. Almost certainly it is not.

**Tianjic (Tsinghua, Luping Shi; Nature cover, 1 Aug 2019).** "Towards artificial general intelligence with hybrid Tianjic chip architecture" ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1424-8)). "*Each chip contains 156 computational function cores, which is about 40,000 neurons and tens of millions of synapses*" in "*3.8 × 3.8 square millimetres*" [cores/neurons **secondary only** (Frontiers 2022 review: "156 neural cores, simulating 40,000 neurons and 10,000,000 synapses"); the Nature abstract gives no counts; die area is **snippet-only** `[audit R3 2026-09-26]`]; it "*adopts a many-core architecture, reconfigurable building blocks and a streamlined dataflow with hybrid coding schemes, and can not only accommodate computer-science-based machine-learning algorithms, but also easily implement brain-inspired circuits and several coding schemes*"; the demo was "*a self-driving bicycle … equipped with a camera, gyroscope, speedometer, driving and steering motors and a Tianjic chip*" ([Tsinghua](https://www.tsinghua.edu.cn/en/info/1244/3005.htm)).

**[INF] Tianjic is the most strategically relevant historical chip for [vendor product],** because it is explicitly *hybrid*: one fabric running both ANN and SNN paradigms with mixed coding. A pragmatic 2026 "engineering grade" product that must handle EO/IR/HSI/RADAR/RF is far more likely to be a hybrid ANN+SNN pipeline than a purist all-spiking stack. §4.5 argues this is in fact the most probable reading of [vendor product].

**SynSense (Zurich/Chengdu), Speck and Xylo.** Speck is "*a fully event-driven neuromorphic vision SoC that can support large-scale spiking convolutional neural networks (sCNN) with a fully asynchronous chip architecture and is fully configurable with 320K spiking neurons*"; critically it "*combines a Dynamic Vision Sensor and a Spiking Neural Network processor on a single chip*". **[INF] Speck is the most important single device in this document for surveillance.** Sensor and spiking processor on one die means there is no frame buffer, no MIPI link running at frame rate, no host SoC awake. That is the architecture in which the energy claim survives contact with reality (§4.2), because the I/O that usually dominates the budget has been designed out. Note also the *honest* power spread in SynSense's own numbers: **<1 mW quiescent-ish versus 8.74 mW during active gesture interaction** — nearly an order of magnitude, driven entirely by scene activity. Any perimeter-sensor power budget we compute in §5.5 must be a *distribution over scene activity*, never a single number. A windy hedgerow at dusk is not an empty car park.

The industry has converged on the pragmatic answer: spiking for the always-on, temporally-sparse, cheap part; conventional DSP and CNN for the parts where spiking loses. "Presence detection" and "people counting" in the target list are our application domain exactly. And note the "500× lower energy" claim has no stated baseline — that is the genre convention, and it is why §4.2 exists.

**GrAI Matter Labs** built GrAI VIP / NeuronFlow around sparsity-exploiting event-driven dataflow; its assets were acquired by Snap in 2023 and it no longer exists as an independent supplier. [audit 2026-09-26: Snap acquired GrAI Matter Labs SAS on 31 Oct 2023; made public in early 2024 (EE Times, Bits&Chips)] **[INF] Include it anyway** — a named neuromorphic startup that did not make it is a necessary corrective to a list that otherwise reads like an unbroken success story, and supplier viability is a genuine procurement risk for any low-SWaP payload programme.

### 1.8 Event cameras: the sensors

## 2. Training SNNs: what actually works in 2026

### 2.1 The core difficulty, stated precisely

The spike function is a Heaviside step. Its derivative is zero everywhere and undefined at threshold. Backpropagation therefore has nothing to propagate. Every training method in the field is a way around that single fact, and there are exactly three families: **convert an ANN**, **fake the gradient**, or **learn locally without gradients** (§1.3). A fourth, **evolutionary/neuroevolution search**, exists — e.g. [SPENSER: Towards a NeuroEvolutionary Approach for Convolutional Spiking Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.10987) — and is a research curiosity at our scale.

Compounding the difficulty: an SNN is a *recurrent* network even when its topology is feedforward, because membrane state carries across timesteps. Training therefore means backpropagation through time over `T` timesteps, and **activation memory scales with T**. This is the field's dirty secret on the training-cost side and is quantified in §4.3.

### 2.2 ANN-to-SNN conversion

Train a conventional ReLU CNN, then replace each ReLU with a spiking neuron whose *firing rate* approximates the ReLU activation, rescaling thresholds/weights layer-by-layer so the rate mapping holds. Historical milestones: Cao et al. (2015), Diehl et al. (2015) with threshold balancing, Rueckauer et al. (2017) with robust percentile-based normalisation, Sengupta et al.'s Spike-NORM (2019) for VGG/ResNet-scale conversion, and the RMP/soft-reset family that cut latency substantially.

**Advantages.** You inherit the entire ANN toolchain and all of its accuracy. Training cost is ordinary ANN training cost. It is the lowest-risk path to a working spiking model, and **[INF]** it is what I would expect a commercial "engineering grade" product to lean on for its vision path, because it is the only route whose accuracy is predictable in advance.

**The fatal caveat.** Rate coding needs *many* timesteps for the rate to be well estimated. Early conversions needed hundreds to thousands of timesteps per inference to match the source ANN. The entire energy and latency argument for SNNs collapses if you need 1000 timesteps: you have turned one dense forward pass into a thousand sparse ones. Modern calibration methods have pushed conversion into the tens of timesteps, and the direct-training literature reports high accuracy at `T = 4` to `T = 6`; one hyperspectral result notes networks that "*feed analog pixel values directly into the first layer, allowing networks to achieve high accuracy in just 5 time steps*" ([search synthesis, HSI-SNN literature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11619)). **The timestep count `T` is the single most important number to interrogate in any SNN claim.** It multiplies latency, energy and training memory. A clean [vendor product] question: *"what is T at inference, and is it fixed or input-adaptive?"*

Conversion also handles *temporal* tasks badly, by construction: the source ANN had no notion of time, so the converted SNN's dynamics encode magnitude, not timing. For event-camera or radar work — i.e. for the tasks where SNNs have a real edge — conversion is the wrong tool.

### 2.3 Surrogate gradients — the mainstream

Replace the spike derivative in the *backward* pass only with a smooth surrogate (a box/rectangular window, a fast sigmoid derivative, an arctan derivative, a triangular kernel), keep the exact Heaviside in the forward pass, and run BPTT. The canonical reference is **Neftci, Mostafa & Zenke, "Surrogate Gradient Learning in Spiking Neural Networks: Bringing the Power of Gradient-Based Optimization to Spiking Neural Networks"**, *IEEE Signal Processing Magazine* 36(6):51–63, 2019 ([arXiv 1901.09948](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09948)). Its framing: spiking networks are "*nature's versatile solution to fault-tolerant and energy efficient signal processing*", whose "*training requires overcoming challenges linked to their binary and dynamical nature*", with surrogate gradients presented as "*a particularly flexible and efficient approach to overcome these challenges*".

**SLAYER** (Spike Layer Error Reassignment in Time), Shrestha & Orchard, *NeurIPS* 2018, is the other foundational method: it "*adopts a Spike Response Model viewpoint and redistributes error through temporal response kernels so that credit assignment aligns with delayed postsynaptic effects, naturally supporting learning not only synaptic weights but also axonal delays on event-driven tasks*" (synthesis over the SLAYER literature; see the [surrogate-gradient review](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.09948) for the family context). **[INF] Learnable axonal delays are worth flagging** — delays are free parameters that only exist in spiking networks, and they are the clearest example of a spiking model having *more* expressive machinery than its ANN counterpart rather than less.

Practical facts that matter when you implement this (§5.3):
- The surrogate's *shape* matters less than its *width*; too wide and gradients flow through neurons nowhere near threshold, too narrow and nothing learns. Learnable widths are now standard — see [Learnable Surrogate Gradient for Direct Training Spiking Neural Networks](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2023/0335.pdf), IJCAI 2023.
- Batch-norm has to be adapted for the temporal dimension (tdBN / BNTT variants) or deep SNNs do not train.
- Gradients through the *reset* path are usually detached; including them tends to destabilise training. **[lore]**
- Memory is `O(T × activations)`. `T=8` on an 8-layer network is ~8× the activation memory of the equivalent ANN.
- Regularising the spike rate (an L1/L2 penalty on firing) is *mandatory* if you intend to claim energy benefits, because the untrained-for default is a dense, high-rate network with no sparsity advantage at all. A 2026 line of work adds temporal regularisation — [Enhancing Generalization of Spiking Neural Networks Through Temporal Regularization](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19256v3) **[VERIFY]**.

### 2.4 The integer-spike compromise, and why it is the real 2026 state of the art

The decisive recent trend is abandoning strictly binary spikes during *training* while retaining spike-driven *inference*. The flagship result is **SpikeYOLO**: "Integer-Valued Training and Spike-Driven Inference Spiking Neural Network for High-performance and Energy-efficient Object Detection", ECCV 2024 **Oral and Best Paper Candidate** ([arXiv 2407.20708](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.20708); [ECVA PDF](https://www.ecva.net/papers/eccv_2024/papers_ECCV/papers/04704.pdf); [code](https://github.com/BICLab/SpikeYOLO)).

Its two moves: simplify YOLO into "*meta SNN blocks*" to defeat the spike-degradation problem in deep spiking backbones, and introduce the **I-LIF** neuron, which "*combines integer-valued training with spike-driven inference, with the former used to reduce quantization errors in spiking neurons*". An integer activation of value `k` at training time is expanded into `k` binary spikes over `k` virtual timesteps at inference — so the deployed network is still additive and spike-driven, while the trained network had a usable gradient signal.

Reported numbers, which are the ones to memorise:

| Dataset | SpikeYOLO | Comparison |
|---|---|---|
| COCO | **66.2% mAP@50, 48.9% mAP@50:95** | "*+15.0% and +18.7% higher than the prior state-of-the-art SNN*" |
| Gen1 (event) | **67.2% mAP@50** | "*+2.5% greater than the ANN with equivalent architecture*", "*energy efficiency … improved by 5.7×*" |

**[INF] Read that table carefully, because it is the most honest summary of the field's position available.** On **frame** data (COCO), the best spiking detector beats the previous best *spiking* detector by a large margin — and is still well behind contemporary ANN detectors on the same benchmark. On **event** data (Gen1), the spiking model *beats the equivalent ANN* and is 5.7× more energy-efficient. That is the whole thesis of this document in one row of a table: **SNNs win on event-native, temporally sparse data, and lose on frames.** It is also the strongest argument for why a *simulator that can emit event data* is more valuable to an SNN company than one that emits video only.

Other detection results worth carrying:
- **Spiking-YOLO** (Kim et al., AAAI 2020) — the first deep SNN detector, via conversion with channel-wise normalisation and signed neurons; needed on the order of thousands of timesteps. The historical baseline. **[VERIFY]** the timestep count.
- **EMS-YOLO** (ICCV 2023) — a directly-trained, full-spike residual detector, notable for removing the conversion dependency.
- **SFOD: Spiking Fusion Object Detector**, CVPR 2024 ([PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024/papers/Fan_SFOD_Spiking_Fusion_Object_Detector_CVPR_2024_paper.pdf); [code](https://github.com/yimeng-fan/SFOD)) — "*the first-time fusion of feature maps from different scales in SNNs applied to event cameras*", Spiking DenseNet + SSD head, reporting "*93.7% accuracy on the NCAR dataset*" and "*a state-of-the-art mAP of 32.1%*" on Gen1 among SNN methods. Its ablation finding is practically useful: "*combining Spiking Rate Decoding with MSE loss function produces the best classification performance*".
- **[INF] Note the internal inconsistency across the SNN-detection literature**: SFOD reports 32.1% mAP on Gen1 as SNN state of the art (mAP@50:95-style protocol) while SpikeYOLO reports 67.2% mAP@50 on the same dataset. These are *different metrics and different event-representation windows*. This is the field's reproducibility problem in miniature, and it is why [STEP: A Unified Spiking Transformer Evaluation Platform for Fair and Reproducible Benchmarking](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.11151) exists **[VERIFY]**. **Never compare two SNN papers' numbers without checking the representation window and the mAP variant.** Saying this out loud in an interview is a strong signal.

For scale context on the ANN side of event detection: **RVT** (Recurrent Vision Transformer, CVPR 2023) is the standard non-spiking baseline, at roughly **47.2 mAP on Gen1**, with 2025 work such as EMF reporting "*49.1 mAP, outperforming the previous state-of-the-art (RVT-B) by 1.9 mAP*" ([EMF](https://lacuna.tiptreesystems.com/work/emf-event-meta-formers-for-event-based-real-time-traffic-object-detection/wrk_ea085920418aaba52110ce6da9b7ab30); [EvRT-DETR](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.02890)). **[VERIFY]** the RVT-B figure, which I have only by subtraction from the EMF claim. **[INF] The gap between ~47–49 mAP (ANN on events) and ~32 mAP (SNN on events, SFOD protocol) is the price of going fully spiking on this benchmark today.** Know that number; it is the answer to "so why not just use SNNs for everything?"

### 2.5 Spiking transformers

The 2023–2026 arc. **Spikformer** (ICLR 2023) introduced Spiking Self-Attention, removing softmax and computing attention over binary spike matrices so the operation stays additive. **Spike-driven Transformer** (NeurIPS 2023) pushed to fully event-driven, multiplication-free attention (Spike-Driven Self-Attention using masking and sparse addition). **Spike-driven Transformer V2 / Meta-SpikeFormer** generalised the architecture. **Spike-driven Transformer V3**, "Scaling Spike-driven Transformer with Efficient Spike Firing Approximation Training", *IEEE T-PAMI* 2025 ([code](https://github.com/biclab/spike-driven-transformer-v3)), reports "*state-of-the-art top-1 accuracy of 78.5%, 79.8%, 84.0%, and 86.2% with models containing 10M, 19M, 83M, and 173M parameters*" on ImageNet, with the 10M model "*outperform[ing] the best existing SNN by 7.2% … with training time acceleration and inference energy efficiency improved by 4.5× and 3.9×*". The 173M model is quoted as "*86.2% accuracy … requiring 1384 MB of memory, and performing 28.4 billion synaptic operations per second during inference*".

**SpikingResformer** (CVPR 2024, [arXiv 2403.14302](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.14302)) bridges ResNet and ViT in the spiking domain: "*SpikingResformer-Ti achieves 74.34% accuracy with only 11.14M parameters and 2.73G SOPs (2.46 mJ), outperforming Spike-driven Transformer-8-384 by 2.06%, saving 5.67M parameters and 1.44 mJ energy*", and "*SpikingResformer-L achieves up to 79.40% accuracy when the input size is enlarged to 288×288*". Also in the line: **Spikingformer** (AAAI 2026, [code](https://github.com/TheBrainLab/Spikingformer/)), **MSVIT** (IJCAI 2025, [PDF](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2025/0601.pdf)), **Quantized Spike-driven Transformer** ([OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=5J9B7Sb8rO)), and token-pruning work ([TP-Spikformer](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.00527v1)). **[VERIFY]** the 2026-dated items.

**[INF] Honest reading.** 86.2% top-1 on ImageNet from a spiking model is a genuine, substantial achievement and kills the old objection that SNNs cannot scale. But: it takes 173M parameters and 1.4 GB of memory, which is not a low-SWaP artefact; a conventional ViT/ConvNeXt of that size exceeds it; and the "2.46 mJ" style figures are **analytically computed from SOP counts times an assumed pJ/SOP on assumed hardware**, not measured on silicon. Every energy number in this subsection is of that kind. Say so when you quote them. The *useful* number in the set is SpikingResformer-Ti's 11M parameters at 74.3% — that is a plausible edge-scale model.

**SpikeGPT** (Zhu, Eshraghian et al., 2023) was the first generative spiking language model, ~216M/260M parameters, built on an RWKV-style linear-attention recurrence so that the model can run in an event-driven streaming mode, with the authors claiming roughly an order-of-magnitude operation reduction versus a comparable transformer. **[VERIFY] every figure in this sentence** — my retrieval attempt for the SpikeGPT paper was blocked by the egress proxy and this is from background knowledge, not from a source read in this session. **[INF]** Its relevance to us is oblique but real: if an ISR product ever wants natural-language query over sensor streams (see R05 Part B) on a low-SWaP platform, this is the only spiking precedent, and it is early-stage.

## 3. Event cameras for surveillance and ISR: the real properties

### 3.1 What you genuinely gain

**Dynamic range.** 120 dB (IMX636, per Prophesee) to 140 dB (per the TPAMI survey's comparison) against roughly 60 dB for a conventional CMOS frame sensor. **[INF] This is the property with the most direct operational value in our domain** and the one to lead with. Look at `docs/06_REALISM_AND_SENSOR_MODEL.md` §3.2: our own CCTV sensor model explicitly simulates "*EV from scene lux; auto-exposure lag (1st-order, 300–800 ms), WDR tone curve, clipping*". Every one of those failure modes — the doorway blown out while the corridor is black, the figure lost for half a second while AGC hunts after a car's headlights sweep past, the face crushed to white by an IR illuminator at 3 m — is a *dynamic-range and exposure-control* failure. An event pixel has no global exposure, no AGC, and a per-pixel logarithmic response, so it does not have those failures. If you want one sentence for a customer: **an event camera does not have an exposure setting to get wrong.**

**Temporal resolution and latency.** "*100 µs or less at 1000 lux*" (vendor) to 220 µs (measured), against 33 ms for 30 fps video plus pipeline latency. No motion blur, because there is no exposure interval. **[INF]** For our domain the honest framing is that microsecond latency is mostly *wasted* on walking humans at 1.5 m/s — a person moves 1.5 µm in a microsecond. The temporal resolution pays off for (a) fast events: thrown objects, vehicle wheels, rotor blades, muzzle flash, shattering glass; (b) *avoiding blur* on a PTZ camera mid-slew or a camera on a mast in wind; (c) reducing end-to-end alert latency in a wake-up architecture. Do not oversell microseconds for pedestrians; **do** sell them for drones (§3.2).

**Low light.** The log-response pixel keeps producing contrast events at illuminance where a frame sensor is noise-limited or has dropped to a 1/8 s exposure and smeared everything. **[VERIFY] the quantitative limit** — event sensors are *not* magic at low light; their bandwidth drops and their noise rate rises, which is exactly the effect v2e models (§5.1) and one of the reasons v2e exists: it is "*currently the only tool that can realistically model DVS under low illumination conditions*" ([v2e, CVPRW 2021](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021W/EventVision/papers/Hu_v2e_From_Video_Frames_to_Realistic_DVS_Events_CVPRW_2021_paper.pdf)).

**Bandwidth and power at the sensor.** Output is proportional to scene activity, with "*focal-plane data compression*" (Prophesee's phrasing). An empty corridor costs almost nothing. **[INF]** This is what makes fence-line and perimeter sensing interesting: the marginal cost of *watching nothing happen* approaches zero, which is the opposite of a frame camera's cost structure and is the correct economic frame for an always-on sensor at the end of a solar panel and a battery.

**Privacy-adjacent properties.** Events encode temporal contrast, not absolute intensity or texture. A static face produces no events; a moving one produces edge-like structure. **[INF] Be careful here, and be honest, because this is where the field oversells hardest.** "Privacy-preserving" is a marketing claim, not a mathematical guarantee: event-to-video reconstruction networks (E2VID and successors) recover recognisable imagery from event streams, and gait is itself a biometric that survives the loss of texture. The defensible claim is **"no photographic image of a person is ever recorded or transmitted, and reconstruction requires a deliberate additional model"** — which is a real and useful data-protection argument (a plausible DPIA advantage under UK GDPR) but not anonymity. **[VERIFY]** before writing any of this into a compliance document; the ICO's position on whether event data constitutes personal data is not something I can source here, and **[INF]** my expectation is that it plainly *is* personal data when it identifies an individual, whatever its format. See `docs/09_GLOSSARY_FAQ_ETHICS.md` for the repository's line on this.

### 3.2 What you genuinely lose — and the one that will sink a naive deployment

**Static objects are invisible. This is the whole game for surveillance.** From the literature: "*event generation depends on motion, and in static scenes, or when edges align with the direction of camera motion, events are not triggered*"; "*event cameras are blind to the stationary components of a scene, which induce no brightness changes over time*"; they "*respond to relative motion in the scene and thus require either the object or the camera to be moving, making them unlikely to be a strong choice for recognizing static or slow moving objects*"; "*a typical issue is the relative static object — when both the front car and ego-vehicle are stopped, the event camera barely perceives the front car*"; and "*event sparsity, loss of spatial information, and perceptual fading undermine long-term feature tracking, edge detection, and high-quality image reconstruction, causing the performance of event-based systems to degrade significantly without sufficient camera or scene motion*" ([Event-based Vision: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.08405); [Emergent Visual Sensors for Autonomous Vehicles](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.09383)). Prophesee's own Gen1 dataset description names "*stationary objects*" as one of the benchmark's challenges.

**[INF] Now apply that to our actual use cases.** `docs/03_BEHAVIOUR_CATALOG.md` includes loitering. **A loiterer who stops moving ceases to exist for an event camera.** Someone standing still against a wall watching a gate for ten minutes — the highest-value behaviour in perimeter security — emits essentially nothing beyond noise. Nor is this fixable by better algorithms; it is physics at the pixel. **This is the single most important honest statement in this document, and delivering it well is how an intern demonstrates judgement rather than enthusiasm.** The mitigations, in descending order of practicality:

1. **Persistent state in the model.** Track and *remember*: the person was detected while walking into the alcove, and no exit event was seen, therefore they are still there. This converts the static-object problem from a perception problem into a tracking-and-occupancy problem. It requires a stateful model — which is, conveniently, what a recurrent SNN is — and it requires evaluation metrics that reward *persistence*, not per-frame detection. **[INF] This is our Experiment 1 (§5.6) and it is a genuinely publishable question.**
2. **RGB/thermal + event fusion.** The frame camera holds the static scene; the event camera provides latency, dynamic range and motion. The hybrid literature is the largest branch of applied event vision — e.g. [Neuromorphic Drone Detection: An Event-RGB Multimodal Approach](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-92460-6_16), [Efficient Event-Based Semantic Segmentation via Exploiting Frame-Event Fusion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03765), and the hybrid tracker [EBBINNOT: A Hardware Efficient Hybrid Event-Frame Tracker for Stationary Dynamic Vision Sensors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00422) — whose very title ("*for Stationary DVS*") tells you the community identified this exact problem for fixed surveillance cameras years ago. **[INF] EBBINNOT is the most directly relevant prior art in this document for a fixed-camera event surveillance system. Read it first.**
3. **Deliberately move the sensor.** Induced dither or a slow scan re-renders the static scene as events. See [VibES: Induced Vibration for Persistent Event-Based Sensing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.19094) **[VERIFY]**. This is a real engineering answer (a piezo and a known waveform) with a real cost: the whole scene now produces events, destroying the sparsity that justified the sensor.
4. **Exploit the noise.** [Noise2Image: noise-enabled static scene recovery for event cameras](https://m.researching.cn/newsdetails/a854ad1c-1d93-430e-a005-ae89fed26103) uses the fact that the noise rate is *illumination-dependent*, so the noise itself carries a static intensity image. Elegant, slow, low-SNR. **[VERIFY]**. Also relevant: [Image reconstruction of static scenes from a static event camera](https://dergipark.org.tr/en/download/article-file/4549109) **[VERIFY]**.

**Other real losses.** **Noise**: background-activity events from junction leakage and photon shot noise, rising steeply at low illumination and at high temperature, with hot pixels that fire continuously; denoising is a mandatory, tuned pipeline stage, not an option — see [EDnCNN / Event Probability Mask](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.08282). **Flicker**: mains-frequency artificial lighting (100/120 Hz) produces enormous global event storms; a 50 Hz-lit car park at night is a pathological case, and any night-time perimeter claim must be tested against it. **[lore]** **No absolute intensity**: you cannot read a number plate, a face, a uniform colour or a badge from events alone, which removes most of the *identification* half of ISR. **Spatial resolution and pixel pitch**: 0.92 MP at 4.86 µm, against 4–8 MP at 1.5–2.9 µm for a modern CCTV sensor — a significant loss at range. **Tooling and talent**: the representation choice (voxel grid, time surface, 2D histogram, graph, raw stream) is unsettled and strongly affects results; "*the well-established stacked 2D Histogram approach from RVT protocols constructs event frames from each 50 ms time window*" is the de facto standard, and note the irony — **the dominant "event-based" detection pipeline converts events back into 50 ms frames.** **[INF] Say that sentence out loud in an interview. It is the field's most revealing fact:** if the winning method bins events into 50 ms frames, the asynchronous, microsecond-latency argument has already been given up for accuracy, and the remaining honest advantages are dynamic range, blur immunity and sensor-side bandwidth.

## 4. Honest assessment: where SNNs win, where they lose, and what "engineering grade" would have to mean

### 4.1 Where SNNs genuinely win

**1. Always-on, ultra-low-power wake-up.** The clearest, least contested win. A sub-milliwatt spiking classifier sitting behind an event sensor, whose job is a binary "something worth waking the real system for", running for months on a battery. SynSense's own numbers — "*<1 mW*" for a DVS face-recognition demo, "*219 µW idle / 93 µW dynamic*" for Xylo on audio — are in a regime no conventional accelerator reaches, and the Speck architecture (sensor + SNN on one die) removes the I/O that would otherwise dominate. Innatera targets the same regime commercially: "*presence detection, people counting*" at "*microwatt levels*". **This is the win. It is a real product category, it is shipping, and it is exactly what "low SWaP austere environments" describes.**

**2. Event-native data.** The SpikeYOLO Gen1 result is the evidence: "*67.2% mAP@50, +2.5% greater than the ANN with equivalent architecture, and the energy efficiency is improved by 5.7×*". When the input is already an asynchronous sparse spatiotemporal point cloud, a spiking network is the *natural* function class, and it beats the ANN on both axes simultaneously. Same story for ESM pulse trains (§3.4).

**3. Temporal sparsity and low-duty-cycle signals.** Anything where the informative content is a rare, timed event in a long quiet stream: acoustic gunshot detection, RF pulse arrival, vibration anomaly, tripwire crossing. The computation cost tracks the events, not the clock.

**4. Latency on genuinely fast phenomena.** Rotor blades, projectiles, muzzle flash, high-speed vehicles, glass breaking. **[INF]** Not pedestrians.

**5. Learnable delays and native recurrence.** Machinery (SLAYER-style axonal delays, membrane state) that gives spiking models temporal inductive biases an ANN has to learn or be architected into. Under-exploited; this is where the research upside is.

**6. On-device incremental adaptation.** Local plasticity or a last-layer update on frozen features: add a class in the field, from a few examples, without a training cluster or a data-return pipeline. **[INF] For a defence customer who cannot exfiltrate data from a deployed platform, this is a genuinely differentiating capability, and it is the most likely literal content of "low shot learning".**

### 4.2 The energy question, properly

This deserves its own subsection because it is where the field's credibility is decided, and because 2024–2026 produced a serious internal critique.

**The critique.** "Reconsidering the Energy Efficiency of Spiking Neural Networks Inference from Analytical Perspectives" ([arXiv 2409.08290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08290)) finds that "*prevailing energy evaluations often oversimplify, focusing on computational aspects while neglecting critical overheads like comprehensive data movement and memory access, which can lead to misleading conclusions regarding the true energy benefits of SNNs*". Specifically, "*SNNs' energy benefits are frequently overstated by ignoring the 'memory wall' — the energy cost of data movement*". The quantitative conclusion is the part to memorise: "*SNNs are only more efficient under very strict conditions, such as spike rates below 6.4% and short time windows of T=5–10. Otherwise, optimized QNNs consume less energy due to reduced data movement overheads from dense and static computation patterns, as well as more predictable memory access patterns enabling better hardware utilization*" ([review summary](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/reconsidering-the-energy-efficiency-of-spiking-neural-networks)). The paper's own positive example is modest and credible: "*an optimized SNN can nearly double a typical smartwatch's battery life compared to a QNN*".

**Why the standard claims inflate.** Five mechanisms, all of which you should be able to name on demand:
1. **SOP-vs-MAC accounting.** SNN papers count synaptic operations (accumulates) and multiply by a pJ/AC figure; ANN papers count MACs at a pJ/MAC figure. The ratio of those constants (often cited as roughly 30× from a much-reproduced 45 nm energy table) does most of the work in the headline before any architecture is considered. **[VERIFY]** the 45 nm table's provenance; it is Horowitz's ISSCC 2014 data and it is quoted far outside its validity.
2. **Memory traffic ignored.** Fetching a weight costs orders of magnitude more than the arithmetic. Sparse, irregular access patterns can be *worse* than dense ones even at lower operation counts. This is the critique's central point.
3. **Timesteps hidden.** An SNN at `T=8` runs its whole network eight times. Energy claims that quote per-timestep operations and latency claims that quote per-timestep latency are not commensurable with a single ANN forward pass.
4. **I/O excluded.** Sensor readout, MIPI/SPI links, host wake-up, and radio transmission routinely exceed the inference cost in a real perimeter sensor. **[INF] In our Experiment 2 the radio will probably dominate the power budget entirely, and saying so is more valuable than any inference number.**
5. **Baseline choice.** HYPER-SNN's 560× (vs float32) and 45× (vs 6-bit quantized) from the same models is the canonical illustration (§3.4). The honest baseline in 2026 is an INT8-quantized, pruned, hardware-matched CNN, because that is what the alternative team will actually deploy.

**[INF] How to talk about energy without being either a shill or a cynic.** Say this: *"The SNN energy advantage is real but conditional. It is large when activity is sparse (single-digit-percent spike rates), timesteps are few (T≈5–10), the model is small, and the sensor is co-designed with the processor so there is no frame-rate I/O. It shrinks or inverts against a well-optimised quantized network on frame data. So the question isn't 'is spiking more efficient' — it's 'is this workload in the sparse, low-T, co-designed regime'."* That framing is defensible in front of anyone in the field, and it is exactly the framing that makes a *simulator* useful, because the sparsity regime is a property of the *scene*, and scenes are what we generate.

### 4.3 Where SNNs lose

**Accuracy on frame data.** SNN state of the art on ImageNet is 86.2% top-1 at 173M parameters (SDT-V3); conventional models of that size exceed it, and the SNN literature's own smaller models sit at 74–80%. On COCO, SpikeYOLO's 48.9 mAP@50:95 is a large SNN advance and still short of contemporary ANN detectors. On Gen1 with an event input, SNN ~32 mAP (SFOD) against ANN ~47–49 mAP (RVT/EMF). **The accuracy gap is real, it is narrowing, and it has not closed.** Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

**Training cost and difficulty.** BPTT over `T` timesteps means `T×` activation memory and `T×` sequential compute; surrogate gradients are biased estimators of a gradient that does not exist, so training is less stable and hyperparameters (surrogate width, threshold, `τ_m`, reset mode, rate regularisation weight) interact badly. **[INF] A team that has actually trained SNNs will nod at this list; a team that has only read about them will not.** SDT-V3's own contribution includes "*training time acceleration … improved by 4.5×*" — which tells you how bad the baseline training cost was.

**Tooling and ecosystem.** Six actively-competing frameworks with incompatible neuron semantics; no ONNX-equivalent interchange with real traction; representation choices unsettled; a fragmented, reproducibility-poor benchmark culture (hence STEP). Against PyTorch + ONNX + TensorRT + a mature quantization toolchain, this is a decisive disadvantage for anything that has to be *shipped and supported for a decade* — which is what defence procurement means.

**Hardware availability and supply.** Loihi 2 is research-access only (Intel Neuromorphic Research Community), not a purchasable part. BrainScaleS is an academic instrument. SpiNNaker2 is a datacentre system. That leaves **BrainChip Akida, SynSense Speck/Xylo, and Innatera Pulsar** as commercially obtainable, plus FPGA soft cores. GrAI Matter's disappearance is a live reminder of supplier risk. **[INF] For a UK defence programme, "which of these will exist and be supportable in 2035, and is any of them ITAR-clean and second-sourceable?" is a harder question than any accuracy number**, and it is a very good question for an intern to ask, because it shows you are thinking about the customer's problem rather than the benchmark.

**Talent.** The intersection of "can train deep networks" and "understands spiking dynamics and neuromorphic hardware" is a small population. **[INF]** This is also the opportunity: it is a field where a motivated newcomer with good experimental hygiene can become locally useful in months rather than years, because the benchmark culture is weak enough that *careful controlled experiments are themselves a contribution*.

**Absolute intensity and identification.** §3.2. An event/spiking front end is a *detection and behaviour* sensor, not an *identification* sensor. Re-ID, face, number plates, insignia all need frames. This directly bounds how much of the R01/R02 pipeline in this repository an event modality can replace: **it can replace detection and tracking; it cannot replace appearance-based re-identification.**

### 4.4 Marketing versus reality — a checklist

The hardest critique in the literature is blunt: [The Enduring Dominance of Deep Neural Networks: A Critical Analysis of the Fundamental Limitations of Quantum Machine Learning and Spiking Neural Networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.08591) **[VERIFY]**. A more measured review, [Spiking Neural Networks in Imaging: A Review and Case Study](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12609703/), notes that "*current progress is constrained by reliance on small or custom datasets, ANN-SNN conversion inefficiencies, simulation-based hardware evaluation, and a narrow focus on classification tasks*" — four criticisms, each of which our repository can help with (custom datasets → standardised synthetic benchmarks; simulation-based evaluation → at least *consistent* simulation-based evaluation with published assumptions; narrow classification focus → detection, tracking, behaviour). See also [Direct Training High-Performance Deep Spiking Neural Networks: A Review of Theories and Methods](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11322636/) for the constructive counterpart.

**Seven questions to put to any SNN claim, including [vendor product]'s.** These are neutral, technical, and every one of them is a question a practitioner would ask a colleague:
1. **What is `T` at inference?** (Latency, energy and training memory all scale with it.)
2. **What is the measured mean spike rate on representative data?** (Below ~6% or the energy argument is in trouble per §4.2.)
3. **Was energy *measured on silicon* or *computed from operation counts*?** If computed: what pJ/op, what process node, and does it include memory movement and I/O?
4. **What is the baseline?** Float32 CNN, or INT8-quantized pruned CNN on matched hardware?
5. **Is it binary-spike or graded/integer-spike?** (Loihi 2's graded spikes and SpikeYOLO's I-LIF are both admissions that pure binary costs too much.)
6. **Where does the spiking part start and stop?** Is the front end an FFT? Is the output decoded by a conventional head? (Innatera ships an FFT and a CNN accelerator alongside the SNN core — hybrid is the norm, not a compromise to be embarrassed about.)
7. **Static targets: what happens to a person who stops moving?** (§3.2. The single best question in this list for a surveillance context.)

### 4.5 What "engineering grade" would have to mean — and my best reading of [vendor product]

**[INF] "Engineering grade" is, as R06 §1 already argues, a claim about assurance and repeatability rather than accuracy.** Unpacked into things that can be demonstrated, it should mean:

1. **Deterministic, reproducible inference.** Same input, same output, bit-exact, across runs and across hardware targets. Non-trivial for asynchronous hardware and for anything with stochastic neurons — and a hard requirement for any safety or evidential case.
2. **A characterised operating envelope.** Documented accuracy as a function of illumination, range, weather, target speed, event rate, and — critically — *spike rate and power*, with stated failure boundaries rather than a single benchmark number.
3. **Bounded worst-case latency and power.** Not mean. A spiking system's cost is data-dependent, so the interesting number is the behaviour under an event storm (a 50 Hz-lit car park in the rain, a tree line in wind). **[INF] A system whose power is proportional to activity has a *denial-of-service surface*: an adversary with a strobe light can flatten your battery. I have not seen this discussed in the literature and it is a genuinely good question to raise.**
4. **Version control, traceability and requirements linkage** across data, weights, and the spike-encoding front end — i.e. what [vendor product]® is described as providing: "*benchmarked and assured solutions*" (R06 §1).
5. **Portability across at least two hardware targets**, so the product is not a single vendor's fate.
6. **A test corpus with adversarial and degraded cases**, held out, versioned, and regenerable — which is precisely what a simulator gives you and a real collection campaign does not.

**[INF] My best reading of [vendor product], offered as inference and clearly labelled as such.** On the balance of the evidence in §1–§3, the most probable architecture is: **a hybrid pipeline — conventional or lightly-spiking front-end encoders per modality (including ordinary DSP such as FFT/range-Doppler for RF and radar), feeding a spiking temporal core (very plausibly recurrent or reservoir-flavoured, per §1.4), with a small trainable readout supporting on-device few-shot class addition, trained with surrogate gradients and/or ANN-to-SNN conversion in PyTorch, quantized, and deployed on a commercially available low-power target (Akida-class, Speck-class, FPGA, or a Jetson-class part running a spiking model in software).** "Data-agnostic" most likely means the *framework and the training recipe* generalise across modalities, with a per-modality encoder, not one model consuming all sensors. "Second generation" means their second product iteration. "Engineering grade" means they have invested in the assurance items above — which is genuinely differentiating in a field of research prototypes, and is the *most credible* part of the claim.

**[INF] None of that is a criticism.** Hybrid, surrogate-trained, modality-specific-encoder systems are what works in 2026; Innatera ships one on purpose. If the vendor has built a disciplined, assured, portable version of that with a real few-shot adaptation story, they have something a lot of better-funded research groups do not. The right posture in the room is: *"I've read the literature, I know which parts of the space are hard and which claims usually don't survive scrutiny, and here is a way to generate the data and the controlled experiments that would let you prove yours."* That is the pitch, and §5 is its content.

## 5. How our simulator supports this work

This is the section that turns the dossier into a work plan. The thesis: **the under-served regime in event-based vision is fixed-camera, multi-person, long-dwell surveillance geometry (§2.7), the decisive open problem in that regime is the static-target problem (§3.2), and both are cheapest to attack in simulation** — because in simulation we know exactly when a person stopped moving, exactly how much light there was, and exactly how many events the sensor should have produced.

### 5.1 Event-camera simulation: the tools, and their pitfalls

**ESIM** (Rebecq, Gehrig & Scaramuzza, CoRL 2018) is the renderer-coupled approach: "*the first event camera simulator that can generate a large amount of reliable event data*", whose key idea is that it "*tightly couples the rendering engine and the event simulator, which allows the event simulator to adaptively query visual samples (frames) based on the dynamics of the visual signal*" ([PDF](https://rpg.ifi.uzh.ch/docs/CORL18_Rebecq.pdf); [code](https://github.com/uzh-rpg/rpg_esim)) — with "*Python bindings … and GPU support for fully parallel event generation*". **Adaptive sampling is the important idea for us**: you cannot pick a fixed render frame rate, because the required rate depends on how fast the brightest edge in the scene is moving.

**v2e** (Hu, Liu & Delbrück, CVPRW 2021 — [PDF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021W/EventVision/papers/Hu_v2e_From_Video_Frames_to_Realistic_DVS_Events_CVPRW_2021_paper.pdf); [project](https://sites.google.com/view/video2events/home)) is the video-to-events approach with the best *noise* model: it "*includes pixel-level Gaussian event threshold mismatch, finite intensity-dependent bandwidth, and intensity-dependent noise*", and "*by enabling explicit control of the noise and nonideality 'knobs', v2e enables the generation of synthetic datasets covering a range of illumination conditions*"; it is "*currently the only tool that can realistically model DVS under low illumination conditions*" and was a CVPR-W Event Vision paper finalist. **[INF] For a low-light perimeter-security dossier, v2e's illumination-dependent noise and bandwidth model is not a nice-to-have — it is the entire difference between a synthetic event dataset that transfers and one that teaches a model that night-time is clean.**

**Others worth knowing.** **DVS-Voltmeter** (ECCV 2022) — a stochastic-process pixel model fitted to real sensor statistics, generally regarded as more physically grounded than threshold-based simulators **[VERIFY]**. **V2CE** ([arXiv 2309.08891](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08891)) — learned video-to-continuous-events. **[Video to Events: Recycling Video Datasets for Event Cameras](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.03095)** (Gehrig et al., CVPR 2020) — the method and the honest framing that this is *recycling*, not measurement. **[Real-time event simulation with frame-based cameras](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.04634)**. **[Physical-Based Event Camera Simulator](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-72995-9_2)** (ECCV 2024) and **[TIDES: Time-Derivative Event Simulation via Deformable Reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.02058)** **[VERIFY]** — the current end of the state of the art. CARLA has a community/official DVS camera sensor; AirSim and Blender pipelines exist; Kubric-style synthetic-data generators have been extended to events. **[VERIFY] all of the CARLA/AirSim/Kubric-events specifics** — these were on my search list when the budget ran out and I am relying on background knowledge. Prophesee's Metavision SDK includes simulation and event-generation utilities from video ([docs](https://docs.prophesee.ai/stable/hw/sensors/imx636.html)) **[VERIFY]**.

**The pitfalls, which are the reason to write this section at all.**

1. **Frame rate is the whole ballgame.** Generating events by differencing rendered frames at 30 or 60 fps produces temporally quantised events: all events from a frame interval share a timestamp cluster, and fast edges skip pixels entirely. Fix: render at high rate (we control this — UE5 offline capture is not bound to real-time), or interpolate (RIFE/SuperSloMo, which v2e does internally), or use ESIM-style adaptive sampling. **Interpolation introduces its own artefacts** that a network will learn — interpolated motion is unnaturally smooth, so a model trained on it may over-rely on smooth optical flow. **[INF] Given our repository already renders offline and non-real-time, we should render natively high-rate rather than interpolate. This is a genuine structural advantage of a UE5 offline generator over a video-to-events conversion of an existing dataset, and it is worth stating explicitly in any pitch.**
2. **Linear vs log intensity.** Event pixels respond to changes in *log* intensity. Our renderer's output is tone-mapped, gamma-encoded sRGB. Feeding sRGB straight into a `log` is wrong. We must take the **linear HDR radiance buffer before tone mapping** — which means the event path must branch off our pipeline *upstream* of the exposure and tone-curve stages in `docs/06_REALISM_AND_SENSOR_MODEL.md` §3.2, not after them. **[INF] This is the single most common implementation bug in synthetic event data and it silently destroys the dynamic-range advantage we are trying to demonstrate.**
3. **Noise-free synthetic events are a trap.** A model trained on clean simulated events will collapse on real hardware. Threshold mismatch (per-pixel `C` variation, typically modelled as 2.5–4% contrast with ~10–20% Gaussian spread **[VERIFY]**), background activity rate as a function of illumination and temperature, refractory period, hot pixels, and finite pixel bandwidth all have to be modelled and *randomised*. v2e's knobs are the reference list.
4. **Flicker and rolling-shutter interactions.** Our simulator already models mains-lit night scenes; we must drive the *light source* at 100 Hz in the high-rate render, or our synthetic night events will lack the dominant real-world artefact.
5. **Anti-aliasing and temporal jitter.** TSR/TAA are temporal filters — they will smear the very signal we are differencing. `docs/06` §1 already disables TSR/DLSS for capture ("*deterministic*"); for the event path this is not just determinism hygiene, it is correctness. Prefer high-sample-count spatial AA.
6. **Ground truth for events.** Our per-frame boxes are at frame times; events are continuous. We need either per-event instance labels (available to us, unlike to any real dataset — we own the segmentation buffer at every rendered sub-step) or a defined interpolation of boxes to event timestamps. **[INF] Per-event instance labels are something no real event dataset has and no competitor can easily produce. If we build one thing that is genuinely novel, it should be this.**

### 5.2 Thermal + event, and the other modalities

`docs/06` §3.4 already specifies thermal rendering from material emissivity tags ("*skin 36 °C, clothes ambient+Δ, engines hot, glass reflective*"). Two consequences:

- **Event-IR is what DARPA is actually funding** (FENCE is an event-based *infrared* FPA, §3.3), and we can approximate it: run the same event-generation model over the high-rate *thermal* radiance buffer instead of the visible one. **[INF] A synthetic event-IR dataset would be a rare artefact** — real event-IR sensors barely exist outside FENCE performers — and it maps directly onto the vendor's "electro-optical, infrared, thermal" list. Caveats to state loudly: thermal scenes have *much* lower contrast and slower temporal dynamics than visible, so event rates are far lower and the noise model is completely different; our thermal model is an emissivity approximation, not a radiometric simulation. This is a *plausibility* artefact, not a calibrated one.
- **RF.** `docs/02_ARCHITECTURE.md` §6 already specifies RF sniffers with log-distance path loss. That gives us simulated packet/burst arrival times with per-emitter identity — i.e. **a synthetic pulse-descriptor stream**, which is exactly the input format of the radar-emitter-recognition SNN literature (§3.4). This is the cheapest credible route to demonstrating the "data-agnostic" claim on a second modality (Experiment 5).

### 5.4 The event-simulation implementation, concretely

```
survsim.sensors.events.EventSimulator
  input:  high-rate linear HDR radiance frames (L_t, H×W, float32, pre-tone-map)
          + timestamps, + per-pixel instance id buffer
  params: C_on, C_off (contrast thresholds), sigma_C (per-pixel mismatch),
          tau_pixel(L) (illumination-dependent bandwidth / low-pass),
          refractory_period, leak_rate,
          bg_activity_rate(L, T_degC), hot_pixel_map, arbiter_saturation_rate
  output: event stream (t_us, x, y, polarity) + per-event instance id
          + optional binned representations (voxel grid, 2D histogram @ 50 ms per RVT
            protocol, time surface, event frame) for framework consumption
```

Render-rate policy: target the rate at which the fastest expected edge crosses at most one pixel per step. For a person at 1.8 m/s at 10 m through a 60°-HFoV 1280-wide camera that is modest (low hundreds of Hz); for a quadrotor rotor blade it is tens of kHz and **we should simply not pretend to simulate blade-rate events** — we should simulate body and gross-motion events and say so. **[INF] Stating a simulator's validity envelope is the most credible thing a simulator's authors can do, and it is the *same discipline* as §4.5's "characterised operating envelope". Use the parallel out loud: it shows we understand what "engineering grade" means, applied to our own product.**

Validation, mirroring `docs/06` §4: compare summary statistics of our synthetic events against a real recording of a comparable scene — **mean event rate vs illuminance, event-rate-vs-target-speed curve, ON/OFF polarity ratio, inter-event-interval distribution, spatial event density around edges, noise rate in an empty scene**. **[VERIFY]** what public data supports this; DSEC and Gen1 are the obvious references, and neither is surveillance geometry, which is itself a finding to report.

### 5.5 Metrics

Beyond the existing `eval/` metrics (MOTA/IDF1, event P/R/F1, alert latency, false-alarms/hour, operator-load index):

- **Energy per inference (analytical).** `E = (SOP_count × e_AC) + (MAC_count × e_MAC) + (memory_accesses × e_mem)`. Report all three terms separately, name the pJ constants and their source and process node, and report the same for the ANN and INT8 baselines. **Never report a single ratio without the components.**
- **Mean and p99 spike rate per layer**, as a fraction of neurons firing per timestep. The p99 matters because §4.5's worst-case power bound depends on it.
- **Timesteps `T`** and accuracy-vs-`T` curve. This one plot answers more questions than any table.
- **Event rate (events/s)** as a function of scene activity, illumination and weather preset — a *distribution*, per §1.7's 1 mW vs 8.74 mW lesson.
- **End-to-end latency**: first-event-to-decision, not per-timestep.
- **Persistence-aware detection metrics** for the static-target problem: *time-to-loss of a stationary target*, *occupancy accuracy* (is the model's belief that a person is present correct at time `t`, independent of whether it can currently see them), and *false-negative rate vs seconds-since-last-motion*. **[INF] That last curve is, in my judgement, the most interesting plot this repository could produce for an event/SNN audience, because nobody in the automotive-dominated event literature has had a reason to make it.**
- **Wake-up sensor metrics**: false-wake rate per hour, missed-wake rate, mean duty cycle, projected battery life at a stated capacity — with the radio's cost included and separated.
- **Robustness sweeps**: accuracy vs illumination, vs threshold mismatch `sigma_C`, vs background-activity rate, vs flicker presence. The sim2real hypothesis is that models trained with randomised noise parameters survive real sensors; these sweeps test it.

### 5.6 Proposed experiment plan — six experiments

**E1 · The static-loiterer problem (the flagship).**
*Question:* how does event-based detection degrade as a function of time-since-motion, and how much of that degradation can a stateful/recurrent SNN recover?
*Setup:* generate scenes where a person walks in, stops for 5/15/60/300 s, then leaves. Sweep illumination (day/dusk/mains-lit night), background clutter (still / windy foliage), and body micro-motion (rigid mannequin vs breathing/sway/weight-shift — our behaviour catalogue can drive this). Render high-rate linear HDR, generate events with v2e-class noise.
*Models:* (a) stateless spiking detector per 50 ms window; (b) recurrent spiking detector with membrane state carried; (c) event+frame hybrid (EBBINNOT-style); (d) ANN-on-events baseline.
*Metrics:* false-negative rate vs seconds-since-last-motion; occupancy accuracy; time-to-loss.
*Why it matters:* **[INF]** it is the question that decides whether event sensing can do perimeter security at all, the automotive literature has no reason to ask it, and we can answer it exactly because we know ground-truth motion to the millimetre. This is the experiment I would lead a pitch with. A likely and interesting finding is that human micro-motion (sway, breathing) keeps a "static" person marginally visible in a way a rigid object is not — which, if true, is both a positive result for the sensor and a caution that datasets built with mannequins or CG statics will mislead.

**E2 · Low-power fence-line wake-up.**
*Question:* what false-wake rate and battery life does a Speck-class always-on spiking tripwire achieve across our weather and lighting presets?
*Setup:* fixed camera on a fence line; positives are human/vehicle crossings; negatives are our full nuisance catalogue — foliage in wind, rain, birds, animals, shadows of clouds, headlight sweeps, insects near the lens at night.
*Model:* tiny spiking CNN on a 20 ms event window, binary output; compare against an INT8 ANN on a downsampled frame at 1 fps.
*Metrics:* false-wakes/hour per preset, missed-crossing rate, duty cycle, analytical energy with the radio cost broken out.
*Why:* directly reproduces the published virtual-tripwire architecture (§3.3) in a controlled setting where we can actually *count* the nuisance events. **[INF] False-alarms-per-hour under wind and rain is the number security integrators buy on; it is almost never published.**

**E3 · Loitering and behaviour recognition from events, with and without frames.**
*Question:* how much of `docs/03_BEHAVIOUR_CATALOG.md` survives the loss of texture and absolute intensity?
*Setup:* the same behaviour scenarios rendered three ways — RGB only, events only, RGB+events — using identical ground truth.
*Model:* spiking temporal classifier vs ANN temporal classifier, on each modality.
*Metrics:* per-behaviour accuracy/confusion; identify which behaviours are motion-defined (running, fighting, throwing, tailgating, climbing — expected to survive or improve) versus appearance-defined (carrying a specific object, bag-drop-then-leave where the *bag* is static, reading a keypad). **[INF] Predicted headline: bag-drop is nearly the worst case for an event camera, because the abandoned object is by definition static from the moment it matters. That is a crisp, memorable, honest finding and a natural companion to E1.**

**E4 · Sim2real-ready noise ablation.**
*Question:* which event-simulator nonidealities must be modelled for a model trained on synthetic events to transfer?
*Setup:* train identical models on synthetic events generated with progressively richer models — ideal → +threshold mismatch → +illumination-dependent bandwidth → +background activity → +hot pixels → +flicker → +full randomisation of all of the above. Evaluate all on (i) held-out realistic synthetic and (ii) a public real set (DSEC/Gen1, accepting the geometry mismatch).
*Metrics:* the transfer gap per ablation step.
*Why:* this is the R04 (degraded imaging / sim2real) methodology applied to a new modality, and it produces the *reusable* result — a specification for how much sensor physics a synthetic event pipeline needs. **[INF] It is also the most publishable of the six, and the most directly useful to [vendor product]'s "synthetics creation" line.**

**E5 · RF burst classification with a spiking reservoir.**
*Question:* can a liquid-state machine with a trained linear readout classify simulated emitter types from burst-timing patterns alone, and how few labelled examples does it need?
*Setup:* use the `docs/02` §6 RF module to generate pulse/burst arrival streams for several emitter classes (periodic beacon, bursty data, frequency-hopping, a Wi-Fi-like contention pattern), with path loss, multipath-induced dropouts and collisions. Feed inter-arrival times directly as spikes — *no FFT, no image* — into a fixed reservoir; train only the readout.
*Metrics:* accuracy vs number of labelled examples per class (the low-shot curve); accuracy vs jitter and drop rate; readout training time.
*Why:* **[INF]** this is the cheapest possible direct test of the two [vendor product] claims that our video-only pipeline cannot touch — *data-agnostic* and *low-shot* — on a modality whose native format genuinely is a spike train (§3.4). It is a few hundred lines of code and it would be, in my view, the most surprising and most persuasive single result an intern could bring to an the vendor interview, precisely because nobody expects a video-simulator project to have an RF experiment in it.

**E6 · Accuracy–energy–latency Pareto sweep (the honesty experiment).**
*Question:* on our own data, where is the actual Pareto frontier between spiking and quantized conventional models?
*Setup:* one detection task, one architecture family. Points: FP32 ANN; INT8 ANN; converted SNN at `T ∈ {2,4,8,16,32}`; directly-trained SNN at the same `T`; integer-spike (I-LIF) SNN; each with and without spike-rate regularisation at several weights.
*Metrics:* mAP vs analytical energy (all three terms per §5.5) vs latency, with measured spike rates annotated, on both event and frame inputs.
*Why:* it answers §4.2's question — *is this workload in the sparse, low-`T`, co-designed regime?* — with our own numbers rather than someone's press release. **[INF] If the answer comes out unfavourable to SNNs on frame data, we publish that. A team that publishes the negative result is the team whose positive results are worth believing, and that is exactly the posture "engineering grade" demands.**

**Sequencing.** E4 first (it is prerequisite infrastructure for everything else), then E1 (the flagship), then E2 and E6 in parallel, then E3, then E5 as the opportunistic high-upside item. E1+E4 alone would be a credible internship output.

## 8. Appendix — the hop chain

How this document was built, so a reader can audit the reasoning path rather than just the citations. Approximately 24 search calls landed before the session-shared budget cap; direct page fetches were blocked entirely (§0).

**Hop 0 (seed).** `docs/research/R06` §1 → the three verbatim [vendor product] claims: engineering-grade SNN, data-agnostic across EO/IR/thermal/HSI/RADAR/RF, low-shot, low SWaP. Decomposed into four testable propositions.

**Hop 1 — foundations and flagship hardware.** Maass 1997 (third generation) → Hala Point / Loihi 2 specs → TrueNorth 2014 → SpiNNaker 2 / SpiNNcloud. *Yield:* the canonical numbers, **and** the first methodological finding — TrueNorth quoted at both 65 mW and 70 mW in its own authors' papers, which set the sceptical frame for every later energy figure. *Also yielded:* Sandia deployments of both Loihi (Hala Point) and SpiNNaker 2, the latter explicitly for "national security applications" → a defence thread to pull.

**Hop 2 — the commercial edge tier** (because "low SWaP" means edge silicon, not datacentre racks). BrainChip/Akida defence → **the Raytheon/AFRL $1.8M neuromorphic micro-Doppler contract, topic AF242-D015**. SynSense Speck/Xylo → sensor-and-SNN-on-one-die, and the 1 mW-vs-8.74 mW activity dependence. Innatera Pulsar → **the finding that a flagship neuromorphic MCU ships with an FFT and a CNN accelerator beside the spiking core**, i.e. hybrid is the industry's answer. Prophesee/Sony IMX636 → 120 dB, 100 µs vendor vs 220 µs measured. *This hop produced the §4.5 interpretation of [vendor product].*

**Hop 3 — training methods and current SOTA.** Surrogate gradients (Neftci) and SLAYER → SpikeYOLO (integer-valued I-LIF) → Spike-driven Transformer V3 and SpikingResformer → SFOD → Gen1/1Mpx benchmark landscape and the RVT ANN baseline. *Yield:* the pivotal table in §2.4 — SNNs lose to ANNs on COCO frames, **beat** the equivalent ANN on Gen1 events at 5.7× the energy efficiency. That single contrast became the document's thesis. *Also yielded* the SFOD-32.1-mAP vs SpikeYOLO-67.2-mAP@50 inconsistency → the STEP reproducibility critique.

**Hop 4 — from the AFRL/micro-Doppler thread, the modality claim.** Radar-SNN literature → radar emitter recognition (ESM pulse trains are natively spike trains) → micro-Doppler action recognition at sub-100 mW → spiking neural resonators doing FMCW range/angle estimation in the spike domain → hyperspectral SNNs → **HYPER-SNN's 560× (vs float) and 45× (vs 6-bit) from the same models**, which became §4.2's canonical illustration of baseline-dependence. *Verdict formed:* "data-agnostic" is supportable as a framework claim, unsupported as a single-model claim.

**Hop 5 — defence and operational context.** DARPA FENCE → event-based **infrared** FPA, **<1.5 W integrated**, Raytheon/BAE/Northrop, Phase 2 at $16.27M and $8.71M → the same prime (Raytheon) appears in both FENCE and the BrainChip/AFRL radar work. Counter-UAS event literature → propeller signatures, the Speck virtual tripwire, FRED and tiny-object benchmarks → **the reference architecture for our E2**. Dstl → neuromorphic named in the Exploration Division remit and the Future Sensing/PNT symposium as the route, but **no specific neuromorphic competition found — recorded as a gap, not papered over.**

**Hop 6 — the adversarial hop, deliberately searching for the critique rather than the claim.** "SNN energy efficiency overstated" → *Reconsidering the Energy Efficiency of SNNs* → the memory wall, **spike rate <6.4% and T=5–10 as the conditions under which SNNs actually win**, otherwise optimised QNNs win. This reframed the whole document: the question stopped being "are SNNs efficient" and became "is this workload in the sparse, low-T, co-designed regime" — which is a question about *scenes*, which is a question a simulator can answer. **This hop is why §5 has the shape it has.**

**Hop 7 — the failure mode, i.e. what breaks in *our* application specifically.** "Event camera static object limitation" → event cameras are "*blind to the stationary components of a scene*", "*unlikely to be a strong choice for recognizing static or slow moving objects*", perceptual fading → **a stationary loiterer disappears** → mitigations: stateful tracking, RGB/thermal fusion (EBBINNOT — a hybrid tracker written explicitly *for stationary DVS*), induced vibration (VibES), noise-enabled static recovery (Noise2Image). **This became the document's most important honest finding and the flagship experiment E1.** Cross-check: Prophesee's own Gen1 dataset page names "stationary objects" as a benchmark challenge — the field knows.

**Hop 8 — simulation and synthesis.** ESIM (adaptive renderer coupling) → v2e (illumination-dependent noise and bandwidth; "*the only tool that can realistically model DVS under low illumination*") → V2CE, DVS-Voltmeter, physically-based and TIDES simulators → the pitfall list, of which the two that matter most are **render rate** and **taking linear HDR radiance before tone mapping** → mapped onto this repository's existing `docs/06` pipeline, where the event branch must tap upstream of exposure and tone-curve. Closed the loop by mapping every finding to one of six experiments and to metrics that survive §4.2's scrutiny.

**Hops not completed, and honestly so.** Prophesee's commercial security partnerships; event-camera privacy law and the ICO's position; ESA neuromorphic on-board processing and the EU NEUROTECH programme specifics; SpikeGPT's primary figures; Samsung DVS product status; FENCE Phase 3 / transition; a systematic census of event-based *pedestrian* datasets; DASA's live competition list. All were queued when the search budget hit 200/200. Each is flagged **[VERIFY]** in place rather than filled from memory and left unmarked.
