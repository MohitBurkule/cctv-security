# PART A — MULTI-SENSOR FUSION FOR PERIMETER, BORDER AND CNI SECURITY

## 1. The operational context: who buys this, and what they are actually buying

### 1.1 The doctrine that governs everything: detect → assess → delay → respond

Every credible perimeter security design in the UK/US/NATO world is a variant of one loop: **detection** (a sensor says something happened), **assessment** (a human or algorithm decides whether it matters), **delay** (the physical barrier buys time), **response** (someone arrives). The single most important engineering consequence is that *detection performance is worthless without assessment*, and assessment is the bottleneck, because assessment is a human looking at a camera.

This is why radar/fence/RF sensors in this market are almost never sold alone. They are sold as a **cue** into a camera. The UK's National Protective Security Authority — the body formerly known as CPNI, and the national technical authority for physical and personnel protective security — frames Perimeter Intrusion Detection Systems as advance warning rather than as an answer: PIDS is described as detecting "*intruders attempting to gain illegal entry onto or into your site*", and as "*a generic term which covers a wide range of technologies designed to provide advance warning of an intruder gaining access to a secure area*" ([NPSA, Perimeter Intrusion Detection (PIDS)](https://www.npsa.gov.uk/building-protection/video-surveillance-access-control-detection-control-rooms/perimeter-intrusion-detection-pids); see also the [NPSA Guide to PIDS](https://www.npsa.gov.uk/system/files/documents/2c/80/Guide-to-PIDS.pdf)).

"Advance warning" is the load-bearing phrase. A PIDS alarm is a *pointer*, and the system's real output is an operator's decision. Anyone who designs a fusion architecture without modelling operator load has designed a system that will be switched off within eighteen months. **[lore]**

### 1.2 UK: NPSA, the Catalogue of Security Equipment, and CAPSS

The UK route to market for CNI physical security is assurance-scheme-shaped, and knowing the scheme names is itself a credibility marker. NPSA operates functional evaluation schemes for PIDS products ([NPSA, PIDS Evaluation Schemes](https://npsa.gov.uk/perimeter-intrusion-detection-systems-pids-evaluation-schemes)), and separately operates **CAPSS — Cyber Assurance of Physical Security Systems**, under which "*perimeter intrusion detection hardware and software are examples of potential products available to be assured*", and where "*[s]ystems that successfully pass CAPSS will be entered into the NPSA Catalogue of Security Equipment (CSE) with full details of all the components tested published*" ([NPSA CAPSS](https://www.npsa.gov.uk/cyber-assurance-physical-security-systems-capss-evaluations), [CAPSS guidance PDF](https://www.npsa.gov.uk/system/files/documents/capss-guidance.pdf)).

Two practitioner readings of this:

- **CAPSS is a cyber scheme applied to physical security products.** It exists because a perimeter sensor network is a large, physically-exposed, poorly-patched IP estate sitting inside a CNI boundary. The fence controller in a field is a route onto the operational network. Any fusion platform proposed into UK CNI will be asked about CAPSS, and "we're an analytics layer, not a security product" is not an accepted answer. **[lore]**
- **Being in the CSE is a procurement gate, not a performance claim.** Catalogue entry means "tested, components published", not "best". A veteran reads a CSE entry to find out *which exact firmware and component set* was tested, because vendors routinely sell a newer variant than the one assured. **[lore]**

The parallel imagery scheme is **i-LIDS** (Imagery Library for Intelligent Detection Systems), the Home Office benchmark that video analytics must pass to be used for operational alerting in UK government sites. "*[T]he imagery library for the intelligent detection systems (i-LIDS) sterile zone dataset has been published by the UK Home Office for the PID (Perimeter Intrusion Detection) task*", and "*[t]he PID task in this dataset consists of detecting the presence of people in a sterile zone*" ([Perimeter Intrusion Detection by Video Surveillance: A Survey, *Sensors* 22(9):3601](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/9/3601)). Vendors advertise the approval explicitly — e.g. "*Vi-System Ver. 3.3 SP1 is i-LIDS approved as a primary detection system for operational alert use in sterile zone monitoring applications*" ([Vi-System brochure](https://www.slideshare.net/wilnet40/vi-system-product-brochure)) — and note the phrase **"primary detection system"**, which is a distinct, higher i-LIDS category than "event recording"/secondary use.

The i-LIDS scoring rule matters far more to an ML engineer than the approval badge, and is quoted in §5.2 below.

### 1.3 US: the Integrated Surveillance Tower programme, and a two-decade history of over-promising

The US southwest border is the largest continuously-operating multi-sensor perimeter in the world, and the most thoroughly audited — which makes it the best public source of operational truth. GAO describes the Integrated Surveillance Towers (IST) programme as consisting of "*803 fielded towers located along US borders that detect items of interest, such as people or vehicles*", with the Autonomous Surveillance Tower (AST) line being solar-powered units that "*provide autonomous surveillance operations 24 hours per day, 365 days per year*" (GAO, as reported by [FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/anduril-sentry-towers-cbp/) and the [EFF's 2024 analysis](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/10/us-border-surveillance-towers-have-always-been-broken)). Expansion is on a decade-scale plan: DHS approvals for additional ASTs across 2022–2024, "*with the program expected to be completed by March 2034, with 2,300 deployed by that date*". `[audit R4 2026-09-26: confirmed in GAO-26-108118 (DHS Annual Assessment, June 2026) — p.98 "The IST program consists of 803 fielded towers…"; p.99 "IST currently has 830 fielded towers and plans to deliver another 175… FOC in March 2034, at which point it expects to have deployed 2,300 towers". The report itself gives both 803 and 830; press (EFF, Reason) uses 830. Say "~800+ fielded, 2,300 planned by FOC March 2034". Text via [DocumentCloud p.98](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/28483437/pages/gao-26-108118-p98.txt) / [p.99](https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/28483437/pages/gao-26-108118-p99.txt).]` The AST construction contract went to **Anduril Industries** ([FedScoop](https://fedscoop.com/anduril-sentry-towers-cbp/)); 2026 reporting describes roughly a "*$1 billion*" expansion of autonomous tower surveillance ([Reason](https://reason.com/2026/07/30/homeland-security-is-spending-1-billion-on-autonomous-border-surveillance-towers/), [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2026/07/explosion-surveillance-towers-coming-us-borders-costing-over-1-billion)).

The veteran's framing of this programme is not "AI towers work" but **"this is the fourth attempt"**. The lineage is ISIS → SBInet (cancelled) → Integrated Fixed Towers (Elbit) → Autonomous Surveillance Towers (Anduril), and the EFF headline — "*U.S. Border Surveillance Towers Have Always Been Broken*" — is a summary of a long audit trail of availability and maintenance failures rather than of detection-algorithm failures ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/10/us-border-surveillance-towers-have-always-been-broken)). Separately, on privacy: "*In 2024, the Government Accountability Office assessed CBP's surveillance tower programs against six baseline privacy protections derived from the Fair Information Practice Principles, and CBP failed all six*" **[VERIFY]** ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/10/us-border-surveillance-towers-have-always-been-broken)).

**What to take from this into a simulator/eval design:** the dominant failure mode of a deployed multi-sensor perimeter is not "the classifier had 92% recall instead of 95%". It is **uptime, power, comms, calibration drift, and maintenance economics** — a solar tower in a desert with a dirty panel and a drifted PTZ home position. A benchmark that only measures detection quality is measuring the wrong variable. **[lore]**

### 1.4 EU: Frontex and EUROSUR — the "situational picture" as a legal object

EUROSUR is the cleanest existing definition of what the vendor calls a "unified, multi-sensor situational picture", and it is worth adopting the vocabulary because it is legally defined. EUROSUR, established in 2013, is "*a framework for information exchange and cooperation between Member States and Frontex to improve situational awareness and increase reaction capability at the external borders*", and it "*takes data from a variety of sources, including satellites, sensors, drones, ships, vehicles and other means operated both by national authorities and Frontex itself*" ([European Commission, EUROSUR](https://home-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies/schengen/eurosur_en); [Frontex Information Fusion Centre briefing](https://media.frag-den-staat.de/files/foi/821089/8-fx-eurosur-situational-awareness-at-external-borders-red.pdf)).

The formal definition of the deliverable: situational pictures are "*an aggregation of geo-referenced near-real-time data and information received from different authorities, sensors, platforms, and other sources*" ([Statewatch analysis](https://www.statewatch.org/analyses/2019/monitoring-secondary-movements-and-hotspots-frontex-is-now-an-internal-surveillance-agency/)). Three design requirements fall straight out of that sentence and should be treated as non-negotiable schema requirements for any fusion product:

1. **Geo-referenced** — every track, detection and alarm carries a world coordinate, not a camera-pixel or a zone name. This is why STANAG-style geo-metadata (§4.6) exists.
2. **Near-real-time** — there is an explicit latency budget, and "near" is an admission that fusion adds delay.
3. **Aggregation across authorities** — multi-tenancy, provenance and release-marking are first-class, not an afterthought. Who may see which layer is part of the data model.

Also note the critical literature exists and is organised: EUROSUR is argued to have a "*problematic impact on the rights of the people on the move*" ([ITSS Verona](https://www.itssverona.it/european-border-surveillance-system-eurosur-and-its-problematic-impact-on-the-rights-of-the-people-on-the-move); see also [Völkerrechtsblog](https://voelkerrechtsblog.org/under-eurosurs-watchful-eye/)). A 20-year veteran can name the critics unprompted. That is what distinguishes a professional from a salesperson in a government room. **[lore]**

| Class | Typical technology | Real role | Characteristic false alarm |

The veteran's reading of that table: **there is exactly one column that closes an alarm, and it is "camera".** Everything else is a cue. Design accordingly.

### 1.6 Nuisance alarms: the number that decides whether the system survives

Terminology first, because getting it wrong marks you as an outsider. **False alarm** = the sensor alarmed with no actual physical stimulus (electrical noise, fault). **Nuisance alarm (NAR)** = there *was* a real stimulus, but not an intruder (a fox, a gust, a lorry). Almost all field alarms are nuisance, not false — and this distinction is why "improve the classifier" and "reduce the alarm rate" are different projects.

The literature is blunt about causes: "*vibrations caused by ambient environmental conditions like heavy rain, strong wind, and passing vehicles easily result in many nuisance alarms*", and barrier-mounted PIDS are "*prone to false alarms generated by weather conditions, accidental contact by wildlife, humans, blown debris, or overgrown vegetation at the fence line*" ([Self-Contained Reference Sensors to Reduce Nuisance Alarm Rate in φ-OTDR-Based Fence Intrusion Detection System, MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-3269/4/2/24); [Detection Technologies / VibraTek](https://www.detection-technologies.com/product/fence-security-protection/)).

The most useful single quantitative claim found, and its crucial caveat, is from the same φ-OTDR work: a reference-sensor approach "*can reduce the NAR by more than 90%*", but "*this approach based on RMS values of reference sensors can only reduce the NAR caused by surrounding environment conditions such as heavy rain and strong wind; it cannot distinguish alarms caused by wildlife*" ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-3269/4/2/24)). `[audit R4 2026-09-26: ">90%" confirmed verbatim in the abstract (Optics 4(2):24, 2023) via the [A*STAR OAR record](https://oar.a-star.edu.sg/communities-collections/articles/19075); MDPI returns 403. The wildlife-limitation sentence is not in the abstract and stays unconfirmed (body text).]`

**Read that pair of sentences again — it is the single most instructive quotation in Part A.** Weather nuisance is *common-mode*: it hits every sensor on the perimeter simultaneously, so a reference channel or a cross-zone correlation kills it cheaply. Wildlife nuisance is *local and intruder-like*: a fox on a fence produces a signal with the same spatial extent, duration and energy profile as a human hand on a fence. This is the structural reason wildlife dominates residual NAR after any decent weather-suppression stage, and it is why the honest fusion answer to wildlife is not a better fence algorithm but **a thermal or radar cue that resolves target height/size**, i.e. cross-modal corroboration. **[lore]**

Public per-km/per-day NAR figures for fence sensors are conspicuously scarce; searching directly for them returns discussion of the problem rather than operational metrics ("*the search results do not contain specific quantitative data about false alarm rates measured in alarms per kilometer per day*"). That absence is itself a finding: **vendors do not publish NAR because NAR is site-specific, and any vendor who quotes you a NAR figure without a site survey is quoting a lab number.** Ask instead for (a) a 30-day logged trial on *your* fence, (b) alarms broken out by zone and by hour, (c) the weather record for the same period. **[lore]**

Note also the marketing register to be sceptical of: Magos advertises a fused radar+camera+analytics stack "*providing comprehensive situation awareness while achieving zero nuisance alarm and maximum sensitivity and detection probability*" ([Magos Systems](https://magossystems.com/four-reasons-to-combine-radar-with-cameras-for-critical-infrastructure-perimeter-security/), [Magos on sensor fusion](https://magossystems.com/radar-sensor-fusion-a-game-changer-in-eliminating-false-alarms/)). "Zero nuisance alarm" is not a measurable claim; treat it as a statement of intent. The underlying *mechanism* they describe, however, is exactly right and is the core of Part A: radar cues, camera confirms, classifier labels "human, animal, vehicle, vessel".

## 2. RF sensing of people: what an antenna can actually tell you

### 2.1 Wi-Fi probe requests and the MAC randomisation cliff

The original promise: phones periodically broadcast 802.11 probe requests containing a globally-unique MAC, so a passive sniffer counts and tracks devices for free. The industry built footfall analytics, queue measurement, and "people counting" on this.

Then the OS vendors closed it. "*Apple added MAC address randomization to its devices starting from iOS 8, where randomized addresses are only used while unassociated and in sleep mode*", while "*Android added MAC randomisation for probe requests in Android 8 (2017), made it the default for all Wi-Fi connections in Android 10 (2019), and added a per-session randomised MAC option in Android 12 (2021)*" ([macaddress.io FAQ on MAC randomization](https://macaddress.io/faq/mac-address-randomization-in-wifi-probe-requests); see also [Ariadne, Wi-Fi Probe Sniffing Footfall in 2026](https://www.ariadne.inc/resources/blogs/wifi-probe-sniffing-footfall/)).

The direct consequence for counting is an over-count, not an under-count: randomisation schemes "*make conventional MAC-address-based crowd monitoring systems count the same device more than once, creating a need for new crowd monitoring systems tolerant to MAC address randomization*" ([MAC address randomization tolerant crowd monitoring system using Wi-Fi packets](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02161); [ACM AINTEC version](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3497777.3498547)).

The research response is de-randomisation by fingerprinting everything *except* the MAC:
- Information elements + sequence numbers + RSSI: **Cappuccino** "*captures the association of probe requests under MAC address randomization by estimating pairwise frame correlation and then associating frames over time, employing a self-supervised estimator that jointly considers information elements, sequence number, and received signal strength*" ([Self-Supervised Association of Wi-Fi Probe Requests Under MAC Address Randomization, IEEE TMC](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TMC.2022.3205924)).
- Timing side channels: work "*presents attacks to defeat MAC address randomization through observation of the timings of network scans with off-the-shelf Wi-Fi interfaces*" ([Efficient Association of Wi-Fi Probe Requests under MAC Address Randomization](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353490399_Efficient_Association_of_Wi-Fi_Probe_Requests_under_MAC_Address_Randomization)).
- Content + temporal hybrids: "*MAC address de-randomization for WiFi device counting: Combining temporal- and content-based fingerprints*" ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1389128622004273)); signature-based association in **Bleach** ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1570870524002348)); learning-free counting in **RateCount** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.03873)); and sequence-number/hierarchical-clustering estimators such as **TrueSight** and **Vision**, which "*use data packets and beacon packets in addition to probe requests*" ([arXiv 2112.02161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02161)).

**Veteran's summary of Wi-Fi counting in 2026:** it is a *trend sensor*, not a *census sensor*. It tells you Tuesday is busier than Monday. It does not tell you how many people are in the compound, and anybody who sells it as an occupancy count for life-safety is selling a liability. It degrades gracefully into usefulness when fused with a camera count that calibrates the scaling factor per site. **[lore]**

### 2.2 BLE, UWB, and the device-you-issued vs the device-you-observe distinction

The single most important RF taxonomy in this field is **cooperative vs non-cooperative**:
- *Cooperative*: a badge, a BLE tag, a UWB anchor network, a lone-worker device — you issued it, you control its transmit schedule, you get metre-or-better positioning and a real identity. Reliable, and legally clean because the wearer consented.
- *Non-cooperative*: a stranger's phone. Randomised, duty-cycled, opaque, and legally fraught. Low reliability and high sensitivity.

Product decisions collapse when teams write "RF person tracking" in a requirement without saying which one. In `survsim` terms (`sensors/rf.py`), this must be an explicit per-emitter attribute: cooperative emitters get a stable ID and a fixed beacon interval; non-cooperative emitters get randomisation, duty-cycle gaps, and an ID that rotates. Any fusion algorithm that works on the simulator without that distinction is cheating. **[lore]**

### 2.3 CSI sensing: pose and identity from Wi-Fi reflections

The research frontier that reliably makes stakeholders sit up. MIT CSAIL's **RF-Pose** "*provides accurate human pose estimation through walls and occlusions by leveraging wireless signals in WiFi frequencies that traverse walls and reflect off the human body*", and the training trick is cross-modal: it "*is trained using a state-of-the-art vision model to provide cross-modal supervision, and once trained, RF-Pose uses only the wireless signal for pose estimation*" ([RF-Pose project page](https://rfpose.csail.mit.edu/); [CVPR 2018 paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/papers/Zhao_Through-Wall_Human_Pose_CVPR_2018_paper.pdf)).

Two quoted results that matter:
- Generalisation: "*[t]he network was never explicitly trained on data from the other side of a wall*", yet "*[w]hen tested on visible scenes, the radio-based system is almost as accurate as the vision-based system used to train it*" ([RF-Pose](https://rfpose.csail.mit.edu/)).
- Identification: the system "*can accurately identify people 83% of the time out of a lineup of 100 individuals*" [audit 2026-09-26: confirmed by MIT News, 12 Jun 2018 — "83 percent of the time out of a line-up of 100 individuals"] ([RF-Pose](https://rfpose.csail.mit.edu/); [DSIAC summary](https://dsiac.dtic.mil/articles/mits-artificial-intelligence-system-can-detect-people-postures-and-movements-through-walls/)).

Note *how* that 83% is framed — closed-set re-identification within a known lineup of 100, in a controlled environment. That is a different task from open-set identification of an unknown intruder, and the gap between the two is where most RF-sensing product disappointment lives. **[lore]** The strategically important part of RF-Pose for our simulator is the **cross-modal supervision pattern itself**: a camera teaches a non-camera sensor. A synthetic multi-sensor simulator with per-frame ground truth is precisely the apparatus for doing that at scale, without the privacy cost of collecting through-wall data on real people.

### 2.4 mmWave radar for person detection and counting

The commodity end of radar sensing is now a single chip. TI's IWR6843-class device "*can detect up to 250 objects and track up to 20 people for indoor and outdoor people counting applications*" with "*a field of view of ±60º in the azimuth (horizontal) plane*" ([TI TIDEP-01000](https://www.ti.com/tool/TIDEP-01000); [People Tracking and Counting reference design, TIDUE71](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/tidue71d/tidue71d.pdf); [TIDA-010022](https://www.ti.com/lit/ug/tidued6b/tidued6b.pdf)).

Reported accuracies, and note the spread — this is the whole lesson:
- "*98.86% accuracy for people counting (0–3 persons) and 95.45% accuracy across 6 movement classes in typical indoor environments*" ([A Robust mmWave Radar Framework for Accurate People Counting and Motion Classification, *Sensors*](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/4/1289)). `[audit R4 2026-09-26: confirmed from the abstract on [PMC12944725](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12944725/) — "95.45% accuracy across 6 classes of movements and 98.86% accuracy for people counting (0–3 persons)"; 60 GHz radar, *Sensors* 2026]`
- "*a human counting accuracy of 85.63%*" using an IWR6843AOP MIMO radar ([ibid. / TI forum discussion](https://e2e.ti.com/support/sensors-group/sensors/f/sensors-forum/1031752/iwr6843isk-people-counting-and-tracking-using-mmwave-radar-sensor-with-sub-1-ghz-reference-design--tida-010022)).
- "*an overall identification accuracy of 93.9% for individual user identification in groups of 10 individuals*" ([Application of mmWave Radar Sensor for People Identification and Classification](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10146058/)).
- Static people — historically radar's blind spot — are now addressable: recent work counts static people "*in dense indoor spaces (up to three people per square meter) by extracting ultra-low frequency signals from breathing and micro-scale body movements*" ([mmCounter, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10357)).

**The number that tells you the truth is 0–3 persons.** mmWave counting accuracy is a function of occupancy: excellent at low counts, degrading sharply as bodies shadow each other. Quote "98.86%" without the parenthetical and you will be embarrassed in a technical review. **[lore]**

### 2.5 Ground surveillance radar, radar-camera fusion and slew-to-cue

This is the workhorse of the outer perimeter, and the canonical fusion pattern in the whole industry.

Ranges, as advertised: Magos "*SR-500 (with a human detection range of 400m and vehicle range of 600m)*" and "*SR-1000 (human – 800m, vehicle – 1,000m)*", with a product family spanning "*detection ranges ranging from 250 meters up to 1000 meters*" ([Magos radars](https://magossystems.com/radars/), [Magos on combining radar with cameras](https://magossystems.com/four-reasons-to-combine-radar-with-cameras-for-critical-infrastructure-perimeter-security/)).

The slew-to-cue loop, described operationally: "*Once Magos radar units detect a potential threat, the integrated PTZ camera slews to cue producing clear images regardless of conditions and enabling the MASS+AI software to classify the detected object as human, animal, vehicle, vessel, etc.*", with "*auto PTZ slew-to-cue that is fully integrated with most PTZ camera brands*" ([Magos](https://magossystems.com/four-reasons-to-combine-radar-with-cameras-for-critical-infrastructure-perimeter-security/); integration detail in the [MASS user guide hosted by Milestone](https://www.milestonesys.com/globalassets/marketplace/uploaded-assets/0012000001jihmbaap/mass_userguide.pdf)). The academic framing of the same problem space is surveyed in [Radar and Camera Fusion for Object Detection and Tracking: A Comprehensive Survey (arXiv 2410.19872)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.19872).

**Slew-to-cue is where most integrations actually fail, and the failure modes are unglamorous** **[lore]**:
1. **Registration error.** The radar's world frame and the PTZ's pan/tilt frame must agree. A 1° bearing error at 600 m is a ~10 m miss — the target is out of frame at any useful zoom. Every deployment needs a per-site calibration walk with GPS, and re-calibration after any mast work.
2. **Zoom-vs-uncertainty coupling.** Correct behaviour is to zoom *inversely* to positional covariance: wide while the track is young, tighter as it stabilises. Systems that zoom to a fixed preset lose the target constantly.
3. **Contention.** One PTZ, several simultaneous cues. You need an explicit arbitration policy (priority by zone criticality, by classification confidence, by track age) and it must be visible to the operator, because "the camera ignored my alarm" destroys trust faster than any false alarm.
4. **Latency budget.** Radar detect → track confirm → cue → mechanical slew → autofocus → frame → classify. The mechanical and autofocus legs dominate and are *not* in anyone's data sheet. Measure them.
5. **Dwell starvation.** A PTZ that is slewing is not guarding. Under a multi-cue storm (weather front, herd of animals) a slew-to-cue system degrades to useless precisely when you most need it. Mitigation: a fixed camera per critical zone as well as the PTZ.

These five are directly simulatable in `survsim` — `CameraDirector` already owns "PTZ schedules, tamper events, sync jitter"; adding cue arbitration, slew dynamics and autofocus delay turns it into a slew-to-cue testbed, which is a differentiated capability.

### 2.6 Distributed fibre sensing (DAS): the long perimeter answer

DAS turns a single optical fibre into a distributed microphone array: "*DAS is a Rayleigh backscatter based fibre optic sensing system that has been deployed in real-world, commercial applications to detect acoustic wave propagation and scattering along perimeter lines, and classify intrusions accurately*", and "*[b]y utilizing existing or dedicated optical fibers as thousands of virtual microphones, DAS enables real-time detection, classification, and localization of activity along borders and perimeters*", detecting "*fence climbing, digging, walking, or vehicle movement*" ([AP Sensing, perimeters and borders](https://www.apsensing.com/en/application/critical-infrastructure-monitoring/perimeters-and-borders); [Fibre Optic Distributed Sensing for Perimeter Monitoring, US20170039826A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20170039826A1/en)).

Now the numbers that a vendor will not lead with — and they are the best public illustration in this entire document of the gap between a benchmark number and a field number:

- Fence-mounted deployment: "*achieves 81.8% accuracy on a fence perimeter installation in detecting and classifying various intrusion events*".
- Buried deployment: "*achieves 60.4% accuracy on a buried system application*" ([A Feasibility Study of Automated Detection and Classification of Signals in Distributed Acoustic Sensing](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12431091/)) **[VERIFY]**.
- Railway intrusion: "*accuracy rates can reach more than 90%*".
- Best-case method papers: "*an accuracy of 96.14% at a detection speed of 22.83 frames per second*" ([AI-driven distributed acoustic sensing technology and engineering application, *PhotoniX*](https://photonix.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s43074-025-00160-z); see also [STNet](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10934202/), [DTU: Advancing Perimeter Security — integrating DAS and CNN](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/400052508/Advancing_Perimeter_Security_Integrating_DAS_and_CNN_for_Object_Classification_in_Fiber_Vicinity.pdf), and [acoustic-to-hyperspectral learnable filters, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40797737/)).

**81.8% on a fence vs 60.4% buried vs 96.14% in a method paper.** Same physics, same family of algorithms. The deltas are coupling (a fence transmits vibration efficiently into the fibre; soil attenuates and disperses), event vocabulary, and above all *who curated the test set*. Internalise this spread and you will never again read a single accuracy figure without asking which of those three regimes produced it. **[lore]**

### 2.7 Drone RF detection and Remote ID

Counter-UAS at a CNI site is now a standard line item, and it is mostly an RF problem before it is a radar or camera problem. DJI's **AeroScope** — the reference passive-RF product — "*allows detection of UAS within a radius of up to 30–35 km and consists of a radio receiver and a backend server*", and beyond presence it "*is able to accurately determine its serial number, display elements on a satellite map ... record characteristics, flight trajectory, aircraft coordinates throughout the flight, takeoff point and location coordinates of the operator*" ([Gulf Times / PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/qatar/gulf-times/20191230/282054803949817); background in [The Register, 2017](https://www.theregister.com/2017/10/12/dji_drone_aeroscope/)).

The **operator location** field is why RF detection beats radar for security response: radar tells you where the drone is; RF often tells you where the *pilot* is, which is the thing a response team can actually act on. **[lore]**

Regulation has partially standardised this: "*Remote ID creates a common and consistent way for authorities to monitor airborne drones and identify who is flying them*", requiring most drones in US airspace to broadcast "*the identity, location, and altitude of the drone and its control station or take-off location*", with US compliance dated from "*September 16, 2023*" ([DJI Remote ID support](https://support.dji.com/help/content?customId=en-us03400007712&spaceId=34&re=US&lang=en); [FAA Part 89 guide](https://jrupprechtlaw.com/remote-identification/); [DJI Remote ID FAQ](https://support.dji.com/help/content?customId=en-us03400007747&spaceId=34&re=US&lang=en)).

The security reading of Remote ID is uncomfortable and you should say it out loud: **Remote ID is a cooperative signal, and a threat actor is non-cooperative.** It gives you excellent coverage of the careless and no coverage of the deliberate. A counter-UAS posture built only on Remote ID/AeroScope detects hobbyists; detecting the drone that matters needs radar, RF signature analysis of non-DJI/analogue links, and acoustic or EO/IR confirmation. This is a fusion problem by construction. Dedicated drone radars exist in the same product families (e.g. [Magos AR-300 drone detection radar](https://magossystems.com/ar-300-drone-detection-radar/)). **[lore]**

### 2.8 Cellular interception and the legality wall

For completeness, because clients ask: IMSI catchers / cell-site simulators. "*Cell-site simulators currently operate in a legal gray area that challenges the bedrock of Fourth Amendment protections in the digital age*", and the core objection is scope: "*Stingrays can collect this information — and are sometimes deployed without a warrant — from anybody carrying cell phones within their radius, regardless of whether they are suspected of a crime*" ([Berkeley Technology Law Journal](https://btlj.org/2026/05/cell-site-simulators-operate-in-a-legal-grey-area/); [Berkeley Law primer](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/2016-4-28_Cell-Site-Simulator-Primer_Final.pdf)). Some jurisdictions have closed it: California's CalECPA "*mandates a warrant for all cell-site simulator deployments and requires strict minimization of data collected from non-targets*" ([POGO issue brief on the Cell-Site Simulator Warrant Act](https://www.pogo.org/fact-sheets/issue-brief-the-cell-site-simulator-warrant-act)). In the UK, use "*was confirmed in use by UK law enforcement following a 2016 Privacy International investigation*" and is regulated under "*[t]he Investigatory Powers Act 2016*", though "*[t]he use of IMSI Catchers in the UK is not confirmed by British police*" ([Open Rights Group wiki](https://wiki.openrightsgroup.org/wiki/IMSI_Catcher); [Wikipedia, IMSI-catcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMSI-catcher)).

**Professional position:** active cellular interception is a lawful-intercept capability, not a site-security capability. A commercial fusion platform should not touch it. Passive *presence* sensing of cellular uplink energy is a different, greyer matter, and still needs legal sign-off. Saying this clearly in a customer meeting is a credibility multiplier. **[lore]**

## 3. Audio analytics: the highest-value, highest-controversy sensor

### 3.1 Gunshot detection, and the best-documented accuracy dispute in the industry

If you learn one case study in security analytics, learn this one — it is the cleanest available lesson on how a vendor accuracy metric and an operational outcome metric can both be honest and completely disagree.

**The vendor claim:** ShotSpotter (now SoundThinking) has long marketed accuracy in the high nineties — "*ShotSpotter's claims of 97% accuracy*" ([SoundThinking, ShotSpotter facts](https://www.soundthinking.com/shotspotter-facts-myths-questions/)).

**The independent findings:** a MacArthur Justice Center study of Chicago Police Department data found that over 21 months "*89% of ShotSpotter deployments turned up no gun-related crime and 86% led to no report of any crime at all*" ([MacArthur Justice Center, Williams v. City of Chicago](https://www.macarthurjustice.org/case/williams-v-city-of-chicago/); [MJC, "ShotSpotter is a Failure. What's Next?"](https://www.macarthurjustice.org/blog2/shotspotter-is-a-failure-whats-next/)). The City of Chicago's Office of Inspector General independently "*found that ShotSpotter generates tens of thousands of unjustified CPD deployments each year*" and concluded that "*CPD responses to ShotSpotter alerts can seldom be shown to lead to investigatory stops which might have investigative value and rarely produce evidence of a gun-related crime*" ([Chicago OIG, 24 Aug 2021](https://igchicago.org/2021/08/24/oig-finds-that-shotspotter-alerts-rarely-lead-to-evidence-of-a-gun-related-crime-and-that-presence-of-the-technology-changes-police-behavior/)).

**The mechanism of the disagreement**, which is the actual lesson: "*ShotSpotter's so-called 'accuracy' percentage is based on a system in which an alert is only marked inaccurate if police happen to file a voluntary complaint*" ([MJC](https://www.macarthurjustice.org/blog2/shotspotter-is-a-failure-whats-next/)). The vendor measures **precision-as-adjudicated-by-non-complaint** (a metric whose denominator is defined by absence of feedback). The auditors measure **positive predictive value against a downstream outcome** (evidence of a crime). SoundThinking's rebuttals argue the auditors are measuring the wrong thing ([SoundThinking on the OIG report](https://www.soundthinking.com/blog/soundthinkings-commentary-on-chicago-oig-report/), [on the MJC report](https://www.soundthinking.com/blog/soundthinkings-response-to-the-macarthur-justice-center-report/)). Both sides can be arithmetically correct simultaneously.

The commercial consequence was real: cities have dropped the technology, with critics saying "*it's ineffective, biased and costly*" ([CNN, Feb 2024](https://www.cnn.com/2024/02/24/us/shotspotter-cities-choose-not-to-use)), and Chicago's contract ended after an organised campaign ([Prism, Dec 2024](https://prismreports.org/2024/12/02/how-chicago-organizers-rid-the-city-of-shotspotter/)).

**The three transferable rules** — these belong in any eval protocol we write, including `survsim`'s `eval/alerts` module **[lore]**:
1. **Define the denominator before the demo.** "Accuracy" is meaningless without stating what counts as a positive, who adjudicates, and what happens when nobody responds. Alerts with no ground-truth adjudication path are unfalsifiable.
2. **Detection accuracy ≠ operational value.** A gunshot detector can be *acoustically* correct and still generate a stream of dispatches with no actionable outcome, because gunfire near a sensor is not the same event class as a crime a patrol can resolve.
3. **Measure the second-order effects.** The OIG's most damaging finding was not about accuracy at all — it was that "*presence of the technology changes police behavior*" ([Chicago OIG](https://igchicago.org/2021/08/24/oig-finds-that-shotspotter-alerts-rarely-lead-to-evidence-of-a-gun-related-crime-and-that-presence-of-the-technology-changes-police-behavior/)). Every alerting system changes the behaviour of the humans attached to it. That change is part of the system's performance.

### 3.2 Aggression and scream detection

The commercial products are mature and widely deployed: Louroe Electronics with Sound Intelligence offer software where "*sounds such as aggression, fear, gunshots and breaking glass*" are "*identified in complex indoor and outdoor situations*" ([Campus Safety Magazine](https://www.campussafetymagazine.com/news/louroe_electronics_and_sound_intelligence_aggression_detector_software/5048/); [Sound Intelligence, Aggression Detection](https://www.soundintel.com/products/overview/aggression/); [Louroe audio analytics PDF](https://www.louroe.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/04/Digital-Use.pdf)). Deployment scale: the detector "*is used in hundreds of schools, health care facilities, banks, stores and prisons worldwide, including more than 100 in the U.S.*" ([ProPublica](https://features.propublica.org/aggression-detector/the-unproven-invasive-surveillance-technology-schools-are-using-to-monitor-students/)).

The critical evidence is equally clear. ProPublica's investigation is titled "*Aggression Detectors: The Unproven, Invasive Surveillance Technology Schools Are Using to Monitor Students*", found the algorithm "*contained high rates of false positives, misclassifying laughing, coughing, cheering, and loud discussions*", and recorded a vendor representative who "*guaranteed 100% that the system will at times misconstrue innocent behavior*" ([ProPublica](https://features.propublica.org/aggression-detector/the-unproven-invasive-surveillance-technology-schools-are-using-to-monitor-students/); catalogued as [AI Incident Database, Incident 404](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/404/)).

The mechanism of the failure is stated plainly and is a physics/semantics problem, not a bug: "*[t]he program analyzes noises through advanced algorithms and detects specific sounds such as verbal aggression, similar to how the human ear processes audio*", but "*[t]he detector analyzes sound but doesn't take words or meaning into account*" ([Campus Safety](https://www.campussafetymagazine.com/news/louroe_electronics_and_sound_intelligence_aggression_detector_software/5048/); [ProPublica](https://features.propublica.org/aggression-detector/the-unproven-invasive-surveillance-technology-schools-are-using-to-monitor-students/)).

**The veteran's reading:** aggression detection is *arousal* detection. Loudness, spectral roughness, harsh voicing, high pitch variance — these are the acoustic correlates of shouting, and they are shared by cheering, laughing, arguing about football, and a genuine assault. The class boundary the customer wants ("violence about to happen") is not present in the acoustic signal. So the *only* defensible deployment architecture is as a **cue to a camera and a human**, never as a standalone alert, and never as an input to a disciplinary or enforcement record. Write that into the integration spec. **[lore]**

### 3.3 Glass break, and the honest academic baseline

Rare-event acoustic detection has a clean public benchmark lineage in DCASE. DCASE 2017 Task 2 targeted exactly the three security-relevant classes: "*detection of three target sound event classes: babycry, glassbreak, and gunshot*", with training material of "*1500 30-second audio mixtures*", "*474 unique events*" and "*1121 30-second audio recordings*" of background ([DCASE 2017 Task 2 results](https://dcase.community/challenge2017/task-rare-sound-event-detection-results)).

Best reported performance, and the architecture/class interaction is genuinely useful knowledge: "*DNN-based systems were more efficient in detecting glass break and gun shot events, while CNN-based systems performed better on human-generated baby cry events*", and the winning combination "*(CNN for baby cry and DNN for glass break and gunshot) achieved an F-score of 90.0% and an error rate of 0.18*", against single-model figures of "*F-score to 85.1% from 72.7% of the baseline*" and "*an F-score of 88.0% and an error rate of 0.22*" ([DNN and CNN with Weighted and Multi-task Loss Functions for Audio Event Detection, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.03211); [A simple model for detection of rare sound events](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.06676); [DCASE 2017 results](https://dcase.community/challenge2017/task-rare-sound-event-detection-results)).

The wider label space comes from AudioSet, "*an expanding ontology of 632 sound event classes and a collection of 2 million human-labeled 10-second sound clips drawn from YouTube videos*" ([DCASE 2017 large-scale task](https://dcase.community/challenge2017/task-large-scale-sound-event-detection)). And critically, the research community itself flagged that the benchmark was too easy: "*Beyond the DCASE 2017 Challenge on Rare Sound Event Detection: A Proposal for a More Realistic Training and Test Framework*" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341083459_Beyond_the_Dcase_2017_Challenge_on_Rare_Sound_Event_Detection_A_Proposal_for_a_More_Realistic_Training_and_Test_Framework)).

**An F-score of 0.90 on synthetic 30-second mixtures at controlled SNR is the *ceiling*, on the easiest possible framing of the task.** Field conditions add: unknown and non-stationary noise floors, reverberation, wind on the microphone, clipping, codec artefacts from an audio-over-IP path, and a base rate of real glass breaks that may be one per year per site. At a base rate that low, even 0.99 precision on the benchmark yields an alert stream that is overwhelmingly nuisance. This is Bayes, not engineering pessimism, and it is the single most common analytical error made by teams new to security analytics. **[lore]**

### 3.4 Localisation: DOA, beamforming, and arrays on cameras

Direction-of-arrival is what makes audio a *fusion* sensor rather than a *notification* sensor: a bearing can be handed to a PTZ exactly like a radar bearing. DCASE formalised the joint task as SELD — "*Sound Event Localization and Detection Evaluated in Real Spatial Sound Scenes*" ([DCASE 2023 Task 3](https://dcase.community/challenge2023/task-sound-event-localization-and-detection-evaluated-in-real-spatial-sound-scenes)) — and the existence of a dedicated *localisation* error metric alongside detection F-score is the tell that bearing accuracy is a hard, separately-measured problem.

On the camera side this is now edge-resident: Axis audio analytics "*run 100% on the camera and can detect sounds like aggression, gunshots and breaking glass*", and can "*detect and identify critical sounds such as screams, shouts, glass breaking, and speech, as well as measure noise pollution*" ([Axis, sound detection](https://www.axis.com/en-us/solutions-by-application/sound-detection); [AXIS Audio Analytics user manual](https://help.axis.com/en-us/axis-audio-analytics)). Third-party edge apps ship as ACAP plugins — Sound Intelligence's "*Axis ACAP Plugin (version 3.1.0) ... an edge-based audio analytics solution*", and "*Sound Event Detector edge software [that] runs natively on Axis (ACAP) smart camera devices and can detect gunshots, panic screams, and glass breaks*" ([Milestone marketplace, Sound Intelligence plugin](https://www.milestonesys.com/marketplace/sound-intelligence/sound-detector-plugin-for-milestone-xprotect/); [Sound Event Detector](https://www.jalud.eu/cs/detekce/graffiti-a-sprej/); [Axis discussion of gunshot detection on ACAP](https://github.com/AxisCommunications/acap-computer-vision-sdk-examples/discussions/177)).

Microphone placement dominates everything. Axis notes the trade explicitly: integrated microphones "*enable audio capture without the need for any additional accessories*", while "*products with mic-in/line-in/digital-in allow for external microphones to be connected for optimal placement and tailored performance*" ([Axis microphones](https://www.axis.com/products/microphones)). **A microphone inside a camera dome, on a mast, in the wind, behind a plastic bubble, is an acoustically terrible microphone.** The dome resonates, wind noise dominates below a few hundred Hz, and the dome geometry destroys the inter-microphone phase relationships DOA depends on. Integrated audio is for convenience; real acoustic detection uses external, wind-screened, purpose-sited microphones. **[lore]**

### 3.5 The legal constraint that kills most audio projects

In the UK this is close to dispositive, and knowing it before the customer raises it is a strong credibility signal. Audio captured by CCTV "*will almost always be 'personal data' because voices and conversations can identify a person (directly or indirectly), meaning the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 apply*", and the regulator's stance is restrictive: "*[t]he ICO is very clear that organisations should switch audio recording off by default unless there is a much stronger justification*", and "*organisations should not normally use surveillance systems to directly record conversations between members of the public*" ([LegalVision UK on audio recording, CCTV and GDPR](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/audio-recording-cctv-gdpr-dpa-ico/); [Sprintlaw UK](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/cctv-audio-recording-essential-uk-compliance-rules-for-businesses/); [ICO, home CCTV systems](https://ico.org.uk/for-the-public/home-cctv-systems/)). The governing instruments are "*the Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA 2018), the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), and the Surveillance Camera Code of Practice*" ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/is-recording-audio-on-security-cameras-legal-in-the-uk/); [Surveillance Camera Code of Practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance_Camera_Code_of_Practice)), and workplace use is singled out: employers "*must avoid excessive surveillance*", and "*[u]sing audio for routine monitoring or performance management is unlikely to be lawful*" ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/cctv-audio-recording-essential-uk-compliance-rules-for-businesses/); [Advantex](https://www.advantex.uk.com/cctv-with-audio/)).

**The architectural answer, and it is a genuinely good one:** build audio analytics so that the *only* thing leaving the microphone is a classified event with a timestamp, a class, a confidence and a bearing — never the waveform, never a transcript. `docs/02_ARCHITECTURE.md` §6 already specifies exactly this shape: `(t, mic_id, event, spl_db, doa_deg)`. That is not merely a convenient simulator schema; it is **the data-minimisation design that makes the capability deployable in the UK at all.** Say that sentence in a DPIA meeting and you will have earned the room. **[lore]**

## 4. Fusion methods: what "fusion" actually means when engineers say it

### 4.1 The reference model everyone cites: JDL

The vocabulary of this field comes from the Joint Directors of Laboratories model. "*The Data Fusion Model maintained by the Joint Directors of Laboratories (JDL) Data Fusion Group is the most widely-used method for categorizing data fusion-related functions*", where "*[d]ata fusion involves combining information — in the broadest sense — to estimate or predict the state of some aspect of the universe*" ([Steinberg, Bowman & White, *Revisions to the JDL Data Fusion Model*, DTIC ADA391479](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA391479.pdf)). The 1998/1999 revision broadened it to "*Data fusion is the process of combining data to refine state estimates and predictions*", and the 1999 paper "*began by revisiting the basic definition(s) of Data Fusion both conceptually and in terms of the 'Levels' ... including the introduction of a 'Level 0' to the model*" ([ibid.]; [Revisions to the JDL data fusion model, SPIE 3719](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/1999SPIE.3719..430S/abstract)).

The later revisit added the management and human layers: Bowman and Steinberg "*refine[] the L0–L3 as Fusion Levels, define[] Level 4 as being within the 'Dual' resource management 'Levels' and define[] Level 5 as part of the human-machine interface functions*" ([Llinas, Bowman et al., *Revisiting the JDL Data Fusion Model II*, DTIC ADA525721](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA525721.pdf); see also [A blueprint for higher-level fusion systems](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1566253508000389) and [Rethinking the JDL data fusion levels](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233881998_Rethinking_the_JDL_data_fusion_levels)).

The working translation to a perimeter system, which is the version to use in conversation:

| JDL level | Perimeter equivalent |
|---|---|
| L0 sub-object / signal | raw fibre strain, radar I/Q, audio frames, pixel motion |
| L1 object | "there is a human track at (x, y), heading 270°, confidence 0.8" |
| L2 situation | "that track came from the treeline, crossed zone 4, and there are two more behind it" |
| L3 impact / threat | "this is a coordinated breach toward the substation, not a lost hiker" |
| L4 process refinement | "re-task the PTZ; raise fibre sensitivity in zone 4; wake the UAV" |
| L5 human-computer interaction | what the operator sees, and what they are able to do about it |

**Where money is wasted: everyone builds L1 and calls it fusion.** Where operational value lives: L2/L3 (is this one event or a pattern?) and L4 (change the sensor tasking in response). Where systems are accepted or rejected: L5. In twenty years, the number of programmes killed by a bad L5 exceeds the number killed by a bad L1 by a wide margin. **[lore]**

### 4.2 Detection-level vs track-level fusion, and why the choice is mostly political

- **Detection-level (early/centralised) fusion**: every sensor sends raw-ish detections to a central tracker, which associates across modalities. Statistically optimal in principle — you use all information — and requires precise time and spatial registration, high bandwidth, and one vendor's tracker to accept another vendor's detections.
- **Track-level (late/decentralised) fusion**: each sensor produces its own tracks; a fusion node associates tracks. Robust, bandwidth-cheap, vendor-friendly, and the near-universal choice in commercial security because each subsystem is a separate procurement. Its known pathology is **correlated/rumour-propagated estimates** — the same underlying measurement re-enters the fusion node by two paths and the fused covariance becomes over-confident. Covariance intersection and similar conservative combiners exist precisely for this. **[lore]**
- **Hybrid** — track-level across subsystems, detection-level within a subsystem (e.g. radar+PTZ inside one vendor's appliance, as in the MASS+AI pattern of §2.5) — is what actually ships.

**Practical guidance:** in security, choose track-level by default, and be explicit about which sensor is *authoritative for geometry* (usually radar or LiDAR) and which is *authoritative for classification* (usually camera). Most integration disputes are really arguments about which sensor's opinion wins, never resolved in writing. Write it down. **[lore]**

### 4.3 Time alignment: the silent killer

Nothing in fusion works if the clocks disagree. The ordered list of what goes wrong, in descending frequency **[lore]**:
1. **No common time base.** Camera on NTP with a 200 ms offset, radar on GPS, access control on a Windows server that drifts, fibre interrogator with its own epoch. Association then fails for *moving* targets specifically, which are the only targets that matter.
2. **Latency is not offset.** A camera's analytic event is timestamped at inference, not capture; encode+network+decode+inference easily adds 200–800 ms. Fusion must record *both* capture time and event time, and associate on capture time.
3. **Variable latency.** Motion-triggered encoders, adaptive bitrate and GOP structure make the delay jitter, so a fixed compensation constant is wrong.
4. **Out-of-order arrival.** Any fusion node must buffer and re-order, which means accepting a deliberate lag (typically 0.5–2 s) — the price of correctness, and a direct trade against the "near-real-time" requirement of §1.4.

Our simulator already models "*per-camera clock offset & drift*" (`docs/02_ARCHITECTURE.md` §1.1) and "*time-sync jitter*" — that is the right primitive, and it should be extended to every non-camera sensor, because a fusion algorithm that has never been tested against clock skew is untested.

### 4.4 Associating RF identities with visual tracks

This is the highest-value fusion pairing for the the vendor brief (RF + imagery), and there is real literature.

- **RFCam** "*is a system for human identification with a fusion of Wi-Fi and camera data that uses a multi-antenna Wi-Fi radio to collect CSI of Wi-Fi packets sent by mobile devices, and a camera to monitor users in the area*" — and note the title's framing, "*Uncertainty-aware Fusion of Camera and Wi-Fi for Real-time Human Identification with Mobile Devices*" ([ACM IMWUT 6(2)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3534588)). *Uncertainty-aware* is the honest part: the association is probabilistic and must be allowed to remain ambiguous.
- **MaRPA** "*is a multimodal training approach that incorporates both videos and Wi-Fi probe requests to simultaneously promote tasks of probe requests association and person re-identification (ReID)*" — i.e. the two problems help each other; visual ReID disambiguates randomised MACs and vice versa.
- The commercial/patent literature spells out the mechanism: "*[a] person can be identified by a visual identity (e.g., a face in an image captured by a camera) or a device identity (e.g., MAC address captured by an RF receiver), with a link inferred between identities belonging to the same person*" ([US10997474, apparatus for person detection, tracking and identification using wireless signals and images](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10997474); [US10354145, associating a user identity with a mobile device identity](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10354145)), with implementations that use "*cosine similarity or other similarity measures to determine whether semantic concepts from camera and Wi-Fi are similar, and once similarity is found, the MAC address is tagged against the bounding box of the person for future tracking*" ([US10210728](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/10210728)).
- Historical grounding: "*I know your MAC Address: Targeted tracking of individual using Wi-Fi*" ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271960115_I_know_your_MAC_Address_Targeted_tracking_of_individual_using_Wi-Fi)).

**How the association actually works, mechanically:** it is a *motion-correlation* problem, not a signal-strength problem. You have an RSSI time series per MAC and a world-coordinate trajectory per visual track. Convert the trajectory to expected path-loss using a log-distance model, correlate against observed RSSI, and solve an assignment problem over a window. It succeeds when one person moves distinctively and fails in crowds — because everyone's RSSI series looks alike when they walk together, and two MACs in one pocket look like one person. **[lore]**

**This is the single best-justified feature in our simulator.** Real RF-vision association datasets barely exist, are privacy-toxic to collect, and require per-person device ground truth. `survsim`'s RF module emits `(t, sniffer_id, mac, rssi)` with "*log-distance path loss + shadowing + wall attenuation from zone geometry*" and keeps "*Truth: mac↔uid mapping*" (`docs/02_ARCHITECTURE.md` §6). That is exactly the labelled corpus this research area lacks. Add: multi-device people, device-less people, randomisation schedules, duty-cycle gaps, and crowd co-movement — the four things that make the problem hard.

### 4.5 False-alarm reduction by corroboration: the arithmetic, and its trap

The pitch is simple: require two independent sensors to agree and multiply the false-alarm rates together. If a fence sensor fires 20×/night and a thermal analytic fires 30×/night, independent coincidence within a 5-second window is vanishingly rare, so an AND-gate all but eliminates nuisance. Vendors quantify the effect: Ambient.ai claims a Physical Access Control System is used "*to match video with access data and focus on real security threats, reducing false positives by 95%*" ([Ambient.ai, AI video analytics buying guide](https://www.ambient.ai/blog/ai-video-analytics-in-physical-security)).

**The trap: the independence assumption is usually false, and it fails in the worst direction.** Wind shakes the fence *and* moves the vegetation the video analytic is watching. Rain loads the fibre *and* streaks the lens *and* raises radar clutter. Nuisance alarms are **positively correlated across modalities** because they share a physical cause — so the AND-gate's real-world reduction is far smaller than the product of the marginals, and precisely at the worst moment (the storm). Meanwhile the AND-gate silently reduces probability of detection: a genuine intruder who is quiet on the fence but visible to thermal is now missed.

The competent designs therefore do not use a hard AND. They use **confidence accumulation with modality-aware weighting and an escalation ladder** — one sensor alone produces a low-grade "activity" record; corroboration promotes it to "alarm"; a weather channel or an all-zones-simultaneous pattern actively suppresses. And crucially: **log the suppressed events.** A system that silently discards single-sensor detections cannot be audited after an incident, and "the fence alarmed but we filtered it" is a career-ending sentence at an inquiry. **[lore]**

### 4.6 Interoperability: the standards you must be able to name

- **ONVIF Profile M** — the metadata standard for analytics. Released as Release Candidate "*on September 29, 2020*" with "*[t]he final release of Profile M ... published on June 30, 2021*" and "*current specification version ... v1.1 from April 2024*". It "*supports analytics configuration and information query for metadata, as well as filtering and streaming of metadata, with interfaces for generic object classification and specified metadata for geolocation, vehicle, license plate, human face and human body*", and "*defines interfaces for rule configuration for events, event handling for apps that support people counting, and face and license plate recognition, and events using JSON and the MQTT protocol for IoT applications*" ([ONVIF Profile M release](https://www.onvif.org/pressrelease/onvif-releases-profile-m-for-metadata-and-events-for-analytics-applications/); [Profile M page](https://www.onvif.org/profiles/profile-m/); [Profile M overview](https://www.onvif.org/video/profile-m-metadata-and-events-for-analytics-applications/)).   **Why an engineer cares:** Profile M is the closest thing to a standard schema for *our* output. ## 5. Evaluating a fused system

### 5.1 The curve, not the number: POD vs NAR

The correct performance statement for a perimeter system is a **curve**, not a point: probability of detection (POD, sometimes Pd) plotted against nuisance alarm rate, parameterised by sensitivity, and stated **per threat scenario** (walk, run, crawl, climb, cut, bridge, tunnel, vehicle ram) and **per environmental condition** (clear/rain/fog/wind/night). A single "99% detection" claim without a NAR and a scenario set carries no information.

Three consequences practitioners internalise **[lore]**:
- **The knee is the product.** Every sensor can reach POD ≈ 1.0 if you accept unlimited nuisance. The engineering is the shape near the knee.
- **NAR must be normalised**: alarms per zone per day, or per km per day, plus the weather record. Otherwise winter and summer numbers are incomparable.
- **Detection latency is a third axis.** A detection that arrives after the intruder has crossed the delay zone has POD = 1 and value = 0. This is why the i-LIDS rule below is a *timed* rule.

### 5.2 i-LIDS: the one evaluation protocol detail to memorise

"*[F]or the i-LIDS evaluation protocol, if there is at least one alarm in the first 10 s of intrusion, it is counted as a TP (True Positive). However, alarms after 10 s are counted as an FP (False Positive).*" ([Perimeter Intrusion Detection by Video Surveillance: A Survey, *Sensors* 22(9):3601](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/9/3601))

Sit with that second sentence. **A correct detection, arriving late, is scored as a false positive.** It is not a scoring quirk — it encodes the doctrine of §1.1: an alarm after the response window has closed is not a detection, it is noise that costs operator attention. Three engineering implications:
1. Never train or tune a perimeter detector on frame-wise IoU or mAP alone. The objective is *time-to-first-alarm within a window*.
2. Persistence filters (require N consecutive frames / M seconds of track) trade directly against this metric. Every frame of debounce you add to suppress nuisance is a frame spent from a 10-second budget.
3. Repeated alarms on the *same* ongoing intrusion are penalised, so alarm de-duplication and event lifecycle management ("one intrusion = one alarm object, updated") is part of the algorithm, not the UI.

`survsim`'s `eval/` already lists "*alert latency*" and "*false-alarms/hour*" alongside event P/R/F1. **Implement the i-LIDS rule verbatim as one of the scoring modes.** It is the protocol UK government buyers actually recognise, and being able to report against it is a commercial asset.

### 5.3 Standards-based grading: the IEC 62676 series

The formal standards frame is the IEC/BS EN 62676 series for video surveillance systems, which "*include the use of security grading*" ([BSIA Form 218, Graded requirements under BS EN 62676](https://www.bsia.co.uk/zappfiles/bsia-front/pdf/218vssen62676gradinginstallersspecifiersmanufacturers.pdf)). The parts that matter to an analytics vendor:

- **Part 1-2** — transmission performance: "*general requirements for video transmissions on performance, security and conformance to basic IP connectivity, with minimum performance requirements on video transmission for security applications in IP networks*", noting that "*[i]n surveillance applications the requirements on timing, quality and availability are strict*" ([GlobalSpec, IEC 62676-1-2](https://standards.globalspec.com/standards/detail?docId=1640983)).
- **Part 4** — application guidelines; the 2025 edition moves the industry on from the familiar DORI (Detect/Observe/Recognise/Identify) pixel-density rules toward a visual-performance framing ([Axis, *From DORI to Visual Performance in IEC 62676-4:2025*](https://newsroom.axis.com/en-us/blog/iec-62676-4-video-surveillance); [EN IEC 62676-4:2025](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/clc/bb18ecb5-ea6b-4db3-8756-f1016718049a/en-iec-62676-4-2025)).
- **Part 5** — camera image quality: "*defines recommendations and requirements for representation and measuring methods of performance values for video surveillance camera equipment*" ([IEC 62676-5:2018](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/34391); measurement practice at [Imatest](https://www.imatest.com/imaging/iec-62676/)).
- **Part 6 (2026) — the important new one for us**: "*Performance testing and grading of real-time intelligent video content analysis devices and systems for use in video surveillance applications*", which "*specifies the functions, performance, interfaces, environmental adaptability, test methods, performance evaluation and grading rules of real-time intelligent video analysis in surveillance systems*" ([IEC 62676-6:2026](https://webstore.iec.ch/en/publication/59704); [EN IEC 62676-6:2026](https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/clc/ac9d05b3-d456-421c-a625-68e3935c0e2f/en-iec-62676-6-2026); [BSI edition](https://www.bsbedge.com/standard/video-surveillance-systems-for-use-in-security-applications-performance-testing-and-grading-of-real-time-intelligent-video-content-analysis-devices-and-systems-for-use-in-video-surveillance-applications-bs-en-iec-62676-6-2026/BSI30363051)).

**Part 6 is the headline for any video-analytics business in 2026.** Video analytics is being brought inside a formal grading regime with prescribed test methods and *environmental adaptability* as a graded property. The strategic read: a synthetic environment that can systematically sweep weather, light level, occlusion and image degradation — which is exactly what `survsim`'s degradation pipeline is — becomes the cheapest possible apparatus for generating Part-6-shaped evidence. That is a concrete, defensible commercial argument for the simulator. Useful caveat from the BSIA guidance: "*[t]he majority of requirements given by the BS EN 62676 series of standards are not grade dependent*" ([BSIA Form 218](https://www.bsia.co.uk/zappfiles/bsia-front/pdf/218vssen62676gradinginstallersspecifiersmanufacturers.pdf)) — grading applies to a specific subset, so do not over-claim.

### 5.4 The metrics nobody publishes but everyone eventually measures

**[lore]**, and this is the list that distinguishes an experienced evaluator:
- **Alarms per operator per hour**, against a realistic span of control. Above roughly 10–15/hour of low-value alarms, operators begin filtering by habit, and the system's effective POD collapses regardless of sensor performance.
- **Time-to-assess** — cue to operator decision, not cue to alarm. This is the number that fusion genuinely improves, because a pre-slewed camera with a bounding box removes the search step.
- **Availability** per sensor per month, and **mean time to repair** — see §1.3; this, not accuracy, is what audits find.
- **Calibration drift rate** — how long before a PTZ home position, a radar bearing offset, or a zone polygon needs re-doing.
- **Alarm-to-action ratio** — of alarms raised, how many produced any documented response. This is the ShotSpotter lesson (§3.1) generalised, and it is the metric a customer's CFO will eventually ask for.
- **Adversarial POD** — detection rate against someone who knows the system exists: slow crawl, thermal masking, approach along a camera's optical axis, tailgating a legitimate access event. Our simulator's "camera-aware avoidance" behaviours exist for exactly this and are a differentiator, because nobody can collect this data legitimately in the field.

# PART B — MEDIA MONITORING & NATURAL-LANGUAGE RETRIEVAL OVER LIVE AND ARCHIVED VIDEO

## 6. The task taxonomy — be precise, because the words are routinely confused

Customers say "search my video with AI". That sentence hides six different research tasks with different metrics and wildly different maturity:

1. **Video-text retrieval** — given a query, rank *whole clips*. Mature. Metric: R@1/5/10, nDCG.
2. **Moment retrieval / temporal sentence grounding (TSG)** — given a query and *one long video*, return the start/end times. Semi-mature; this is what "find the sequence" actually means.
3. **Highlight detection** — score saliency per clip segment, no query or a weak one.
4. **Dense video captioning / indexing** — pre-generate text for everything, then search the text. The workhorse in production.
5. **Long-video QA** — answer a question about hours of video. Immature.
6. **Streaming / online understanding** — answer *while the video is still arriving*. The least mature, and the one the the vendor brief requires ("*even during live broadcasts*").

**Everything in the market that works at scale today is (1) + (4).** Everything that demos well and disappoints is (2) + (5). (6) is a live research frontier. Being able to state this taxonomy cleanly, and say which one a given vendor is really selling, is the core of Part B expertise. **[lore]**

## 7. Embedding-based retrieval: the production substrate

The dominant architecture is: segment the video → embed each segment into a joint vision-language space → embed the query into the same space → nearest-neighbour search in a vector index.

The lineage runs CLIP → video adaptations. **X-CLIP** is "*End-to-End Multi-grained Contrastive Learning for Video-Text Retrieval*" ([arXiv 2207.07285](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.07285)) — the "multi-grained" part matters: matching at frame-word, frame-sentence, video-word and video-sentence granularity, because a security or broadcast query is often about a small detail inside a long segment.

**InternVideo2** is the strongest open video foundation family: it is "*a new family of video foundation models that achieve state-of-the-art results in video recognition, video-text tasks, and video-centric dialogue*", evaluated "*on zero-shot and finetuned video retrieval tasks in both text-to-video (T2V) and video-to-text (V2T) modes across six popular benchmarks: MSR-VTT, LSMDC, DiDeMo, MSVD, ActivityNet, and VATEX*" ([InternVideo2, arXiv 2403.15377](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15377v3); [ECCV 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-73013-9_23); predecessor [InternVideo](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.03191)).

And here is the scaling reality check, which is the most useful sentence in this subsection: "*[s]caling the video encoder from 1B to 6B parameters leads to notable improvements in generalization for video-retrieval by 1.9% in zero-shot settings*" ([InternVideo2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15377v3)). **A 6× parameter increase bought 1.9 points.** Retrieval quality is not compute-bound at this point; it is bound by query/annotation semantics and by what the segmentation throws away. Any plan of the form "we'll just use a bigger encoder" is a plan that buys 1.9 points at 6× the inference cost. **[lore]**

Absolute performance is sobering. In independent 2026 benchmarking comparing "*Gemini, Twelve Labs Marengo, X-CLIP, SigLIP 2, and InternVideo2 on text-to-video retrieval with graded relevance*", "*[t]he best open-source model (X-CLIP) scores 0.470 NDCG@10*" ([Mixpeek, Video Embedding Benchmark 2026](https://mixpeek.com/blog/video-embedding-benchmark-2026)) **[VERIFY]**. An nDCG@10 near 0.47 means **roughly half the top-10 results are wrong or poorly ranked**. Product design must assume that: return ranked candidates for human triage, never a single confident answer. Also note the event-understanding critique — "*SPOT! Revisiting Video-Language Models for Event Understanding*" ([arXiv 2311.12919](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12919)) — that these models often match *appearance* rather than *event structure*, which is exactly the failure a security user hits when asking for "person climbing the fence" and receiving "person standing near the fence".

## 8. Moment retrieval and temporal grounding: where "find me the clip" lives

### 8.1 Benchmarks and the DETR lineage

**QVHighlights** is the reference benchmark, introduced as "*Detecting Moments and Highlights in Videos via Natural Language Queries*" and notable because it "*is the only existing dataset that supports both Video Moment Retrieval and Highlight Detection*" ([arXiv 2107.09609](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.09609); [NeurIPS 2021](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2021/file/62e0973455fd26eb03e91d5741a4a3bb-Paper.pdf)). Metrics are "*Recall@1 with IoU threshold 0.5 and 0.7, mean average precision (mAP) with IoU threshold 0.5 and 0.7, and mAP with a series of thresholds [0.5:0.05:0.95]*" ([ibid.]).

**Moment-DETR** (introduced with QVHighlights) recast moment retrieval as set prediction. Its published result profile is the crucial technical detail: "*[c]ompared to the best baseline XML+, Moment-DETR performs competitively on moment retrieval, achieving significantly higher scores on lower IoU thresholds with >7% absolute improvement on R1@0.5 and mAP@0.5, but obtaining lower scores on higher IoU thresholds like R1@0.7 and mAP@0.75*" ([arXiv 2107.09609](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.09609)).

**Memorise that asymmetry.** It generalises across the whole field: these models are good at finding *roughly where* and bad at finding *exactly where*. Loose-IoU wins, tight-IoU losses. For a broadcast clipping workflow where a producer needs frame-accurate in/out points, "roughly where" means a human still trims every clip — which is fine, as long as the product is scoped as *navigation* rather than *extraction*. **[lore]**

The lineage since: **QD-DETR** (query-dependent video representation, CVPR 2023) ([GitHub](https://github.com/wjun0830/QD-DETR)), **MH-DETR** ([arXiv 2305.00355](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.00355)), **DiffusionVMR** (diffusion for joint MR+HD) ([arXiv 2308.15109](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.15109)), **LD-DETR** ([arXiv 2501.10787](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10787)), **MS-DETR** (joint motion-semantic learning) ([arXiv 2507.12062](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.12062)), keyword-attention approaches ([arXiv 2501.02504](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.02504)), LLM-encoded priors with pseudo-event regulation ([arXiv 2407.15051](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.15051)), and MLLM frame-segmentation approaches ([arXiv 2512.12246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.12246)).

### 8.2 The LLM-based grounders

The 2024–2026 shift is from purpose-built regressors to video LLMs that emit timestamps as text. "*TimeChat, VTimeLLM, and LITA perform temporal grounding using a fully text-to-text approach through instruction-tuning datasets*" ([Grounded-VideoLLM, arXiv 2410.03290](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03290v1)).

**Grounded-VideoLLM** is the instructive example of what it takes to make that work: it is "*a video large language model adept at fine-grained temporal grounding*" that "*excels in grounding tasks such as temporal sentence grounding, dense video captioning, and grounded VideoQA*", achieved via "*[a]n additional temporal stream to encode relationships between frames and discrete temporal tokens enriched with specific time knowledge to represent timestamps*" plus "*[a] multi-stage training scheme beginning with simple video-captioning tasks and progressively introducing video temporal grounding tasks of increasing complexity*". It reports "*an average score of 3.24 on VCG-Bench*" and "*surpasses all other Video-LLMs on the Temporal Understanding task with a score of 3.12*" ([arXiv 2410.03290](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03290v1); [code](https://github.com/WHB139426/Grounded-Video-LLM); EMNLP 2025 Findings).

**The recurring architectural insight across this whole family: a vanilla LLM cannot read a clock.** Every successful approach adds explicit time representation — discrete timestamp tokens (Grounded-VideoLLM), timestamp knowledge injection in **VTG-LLM** ([AAAI 2025, GitHub](https://github.com/gyxxyg/VTG-LLM)), or causal event structure in **TRACE** ([arXiv 2410.05643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.05643)). Frames-as-tokens with no temporal encoding produces fluent, confidently wrong timestamps. The field is surveyed in [A Survey on Video Temporal Grounding with Multimodal Large Language Model (arXiv 2508.10922)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.10922) and tracked at [Awesome-MLLMs-for-Video-Temporal-Grounding](https://github.com/ki-lw/Awesome-MLLMs-for-Video-Temporal-Grounding); recent entries include [UniversalVTG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08522) and [Foresee-to-Ground](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21973). UniVTG's contribution was unifying the fragmented task zoo (moment retrieval, highlight detection, video summarisation) under one label space and pretraining scheme. **[VERIFY — UniVTG not directly sourced this session.]**

### 8.3 The evaluation scandal you must know about

This is the single most important thing in Part B for anyone who will be shown a benchmark table.

On Charades-STA: "*a bias-based baseline model that only makes predictions by sampling moments from the frequency statistics of ground-truth annotations in the training set unexpectedly surpasses several state-of-the-art deep models, indicating that Charades-STA has obvious moment location annotation biases*". On ActivityNet Captions: "*approximately 40% of queries refer to moments occupying over 30% of the temporal range of the whole input video*" ([A Closer Look at Temporal Sentence Grounding in Videos: Dataset and Metric, arXiv 2101.09028](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.09028)).

Read that first sentence again: **a model that ignores the video and the query entirely, and samples from the training-set distribution of start/end times, beats published deep models.** And the second explains why: if a typical "moment" occupies a third of the video, predicting "the middle 40%" scores well on IoU without any understanding at all.

The community's response was to re-split: researchers "*propose[] re-splitting these datasets to reduce ground-truth moment annotation biases and introduce[] new metrics to alleviate the inflating performance of state-of-the-art models, building a more reasonable and reliable evaluation protocol*" ([ibid.]) — the Charades-CD / ActivityNet-CD changing-distribution splits and the dR@n,IoU@m metric. Related debiasing work: [Reducing the Vision and Language Bias for Temporal Sentence Grounding](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.13457), and human-study evidence of "*disagreement among different annotators*" on moment boundaries ([ibid.]).

**Three professional habits follow** **[lore]**:
1. **Always ask for the bias baseline.** If a vendor shows moment-retrieval numbers without a distribution-only baseline on the same split, the number is uninterpretable.
2. **Annotator disagreement bounds the metric.** If humans do not agree on where an event starts, IoU@0.7 against a single annotation is measuring annotation style, not capability. This maps precisely onto the i-LIDS 10-second-window design in §5.2, which sidesteps the problem by scoring *timeliness within a window* instead of boundary overlap. **The security world solved this evaluation problem before the video-language world noticed it existed.** That observation, delivered in a meeting, is worth a great deal.
3. **Prefer in-domain evaluation.** Charades is indoor daily activity; ActivityNet is YouTube. Neither resembles a CCTV view or a news broadcast. Expect a large drop in-domain — which is, again, an argument for a simulator that can generate in-domain grounded query/moment pairs with exact ground truth.

## 9. Long-video and streaming understanding: the live-broadcast requirement

### 9.1 Long-video QA benchmarks

- **Video-MME**: "*900 videos, totaling 254 hours and 2,700 multimodal question-answer pairs, with video durations ranging from 11 seconds to 1 hour*".
- **MLVU**: "*a long video benchmark for assessing long video understanding ability with videos ranging from 3 mins to 2 hours*" ([MLVU, arXiv 2406.04264](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04264v2)).
- **LongVideoBench**: "*6,678 human-annotated multiple-choice questions in 17 fine-grained categories*" ([HuggingFace paper page](https://huggingface.co/papers/2407.15754)).
- **EgoSchema**: "*a diagnostic benchmark for long VideoQA with over 5000 multiple-choice questions, challenging AI models with long-term understanding where current state-of-the-art models achieve significantly lower accuracy compared to human performance*".

Frontier-model performance, and the number is the point: "*GPT-4o achieves the leading performance in the MLVU experiment, attaining an average score of 64.6% in multi-choice tasks*" [audit 2026-09-26: confirmed, MLVU dev-set M-Avg, Table 2 of arXiv 2406.04264], and "*LongVideoBench presents significant challenges even for the most advanced proprietary models like GPT-4o, while their open-source counterparts show an even larger performance gap*" ([MLVU](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04264); [LongVideoBench](https://huggingface.co/papers/2407.15754)). Mitigation research focuses on compression and hierarchy: [LongVU](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.17434) (spatiotemporal adaptive compression), [VideoARM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.12360) (agentic reasoning over hierarchical memory), [InternVideo3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.12195).

**~65% on multiple-choice** is the honest state of long-video comprehension in 2026 — on *multiple choice*, where random guessing already scores 25%. Anyone promising reliable narrative comprehension of hours of CCTV is describing a research goal.

The deepest critique is that these benchmarks may not measure what their names claim: "*Breaking Down Video LLM Benchmarks: Knowledge, Spatial Perception, or True Temporal Understanding?*" ([arXiv 2505.14321](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14321v1)) — many "long video" questions are answerable from a single frame plus world knowledge. **The sharpest single question to ask a video-AI vendor: "how does your system score if I give it one random frame instead of the video?"** If the drop is small, it is not doing video understanding. **[lore]**

### 9.2 Streaming: answering while the video is still arriving

This is the direct technical requirement behind "*even during live broadcasts*", and the honest status is *early*.

- **VideoLLM-online** "*is one of the few existing models specifically designed for real-time, streaming video understanding*", proposing "*the LIVE framework to enable temporally aligned, long-context, and real-time conversation over continuous video streams, introducing a streaming training objective, data generation scheme, and an optimized inference pipeline with a continuous KV cache*".
- **Flash-VStream** "*simulates the memory mechanism of humans and is able to process extremely long video streams in real-time and respond to user queries simultaneously*", via "*Spatial-Temporal-Abstract-Retrieved (STAR) memory*" that "*compress[es] necessary visual information and update[s] memory in an online and real-time manner*", with a two-tier structure where "*CSM is updated by clustering low resolution feature maps on an inter-frame level, and DAM is updated by retrieving high resolution feature maps of the most informative frames from a feature bank*" ([arXiv 2406.08085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08085); [ICCV 2025](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2025/papers/Zhang_Flash-VStream_Efficient_Real-Time_Understanding_for_Long_Video_Streams_ICCV_2025_paper.pdf)).
- **Dispider** frames the hard part correctly: "*Enabling Video LLMs with Active Real-Time Interaction via Disentangled Perception, Decision, and Reaction*" ([arXiv 2501.03218](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03218)). Related: [Event-VStream](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.15655), [StreamAgent](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.01875), [segment-level streaming memory](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.11896).

The formal definition worth quoting in a design document: streaming video understanding "*involves models operating online and interactively in real-time while processing continuous video streams to ensure long-term temporal consistency and enable interactive responses*".

**The architectural crux — and this is the insight that transfers from Part A.** Streaming forces a *decision* the offline setting never faces: **when to speak.** Dispider's "decision" module is precisely this. A live system must decide, at every instant, whether the accumulated evidence warrants an output — which is structurally identical to the perimeter alarm threshold problem of §4.5 and §5.1: fire early and you generate false alarms; wait for certainty and you miss the response window. **Live NL video monitoring is an alarm-threshold problem wearing a language-model costume.** All the POD-vs-NAR machinery in Part A applies directly, including the i-LIDS-style timed scoring. Making that connection explicitly is the highest-value synthesis in this entire document, and it is the natural technical bridge between the vendor's two product lines. **[lore]**

The pragmatic production pattern for "live" remains a sliding-window index: ingest, segment on a 2–10 s cadence, embed and caption each segment, upsert into a vector index and a text index, and let queries run against an index that is a few seconds behind live. It is not end-to-end streaming comprehension, but it is deployable today, latency is explicit and tunable, and it supports standing queries (a saved query re-evaluated against each new segment = a live alert). **[lore]**

## 10. Person-centric search: the security-specific retrieval task

Generic video-text retrieval does not answer the query the customer actually types: *"man in a red jacket with a black backpack"*.

The task is **text-based person search / text-to-image person retrieval**, benchmarked on **CUHK-PEDES**, ICFG-PEDES and RSTPReid. The reference method **IRRA** is "*a cross-modal Implicit Relation Reasoning and Aligning framework that learns relations between local visual-textual tokens and enhances global image-text matching without requiring additional prior supervision*", with "*a novel cross-modal matching loss (SDM) and an Implicit Relation Reasoning module*"; it reports "*a Rank-1 accuracy of 73.38% and mAP of 66.13%*" on CUHK-PEDES, "*surpassing the previous state-of-the-art method CFine by a notable margin of about 3–9% in Rank-1 accuracy*" ([IRRA, CVPR 2023 — GitHub](https://github.com/anosorae/IRRA); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Cross-Modal-Implicit-Relation-Reasoning-and-for-Jiang-Ye/1637bf131f2b251c0a457ca4fa94d40eaa9f7a1c)). Subsequent directions: part-level correspondence ([arXiv 2501.00318](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.00318)), calibrated representations ([SCMM, arXiv 2304.02278](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02278)), image-specific information suppression ([arXiv 2208.14365](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.14365)), and — significantly — explicit **uncertainty** modelling ([Cross-Modality Relation and Uncertainty Exploration for Text-Based Person Search, ACM TOMM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3747185)).

**Rank-1 ≈ 73% is a benchmark number, on curated crops, with descriptions written by annotators who could see the image.** Real deployment differs on every axis: the witness's description is wrong or partial, the target's crop is 40 pixels tall and motion-blurred, lighting changes the apparent garment colour between cameras, and the gallery is millions of crops rather than thousands. Expect a large drop, and design the product as **candidate ranking for human review**, with the gallery aggressively pre-filtered by time and camera before any semantic matching. **[lore]**

**The bias problem is acute and must be raised proactively.** Text person search conditions on garment colour, body shape, hair, and — implicitly, through training descriptions — on apparent ethnicity, gender and age. The consequences are foreseeable: (a) attribute accuracy is unequal across skin tones and garment/lighting combinations, so equal-effort queries yield unequal precision across demographic groups; (b) a free-text field invites operators to type protected characteristics directly; (c) a ranked list presented as "matches" invites confirmation bias in an investigation. Mitigations that a serious vendor implements: an attribute vocabulary that excludes protected characteristics, per-subgroup accuracy reporting, mandatory human adjudication, and full query audit logging. **[VERIFY — no specific fairness-audit study of text-based person search was sourced in this session; state this as a design position and a known risk, not as a cited finding.]**

## 11. The commercial landscape: who sells what, and what they are really selling

### 11.1 Security video search

- **BriefCam** — the incumbent in forensic video search. Its "*Video Synopsis and forensic search capabilities enable security and management teams to quickly understand activity by applying object class and attribute filters, visual layer heatmaps and more*", condensing "*hours of video into minutes by overlaying and filtering moving objects captured at different times*". Search criteria include "*object size, colour, direction, dwell and speed*", plus filtering by "*license plate numbers, vehicle make and model*" and "*vehicle and facial attributes*" ([BriefCam federal capabilities statement](https://www.briefcam.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/BriefCam-FederalCapabilitiesStatement-2023-1.pdf); [v5.3 datasheet](http://www.cctv-forensic.co.kr/briefcam/download/BriefCam_v5.3_datasheet_FINAL.pdf); [IPVM examination of BriefCam's search technology](https://ipvm.com/reports/examining-briefcams-surveillance-search-technology); deployment notes at [Milestone](https://www.milestonesys.com/resources/content/articles/briefcam-intro-questions-answers/)).
  **Note what that feature list is: a structured attribute filter over an object index, not a language model.** "Red jacket, moving left, dwelling >30 s" is a database query. It is unglamorous, fast, auditable, explainable to a court, and it solves the majority of real investigation tasks. **Do not let an LLM-shaped roadmap discard the structured-attribute index; it is the part that works.** **[lore]**
- **Verkada** — the mass-market NL search. "*In May 2024, Verkada transformed investigations with AI-powered search that understands natural language queries like 'FedEx truck on January 15' and returns relevant clips in seconds*", and — the engineering detail worth stealing — it "*uses Dynamic Score Thresholding to set the threshold level for what counts as a 'match' to maintain accuracy while avoiding false positives*" ([Verkada, *Scaling Natural-Language Video Search*](https://www.verkada.com/blog/scaling-natural-language-video-search-inside-verkadas-breakthrough/); [Verkada AI-powered search updates](https://www.verkada.com/en-GB/updates/?category=cameras&anchor=ai-powered-search)). Dynamic thresholding is the correct answer to the hard problem of embedding search: cosine similarity has no absolute meaning, so a fixed cut-off returns garbage for narrow queries and nothing for broad ones.
- **Ambient.ai** — behaviour-context positioning. Its NL forensics let "*operators submit plain-language requests, the search engine scans recorded frames and returns exact clips*", with queries like "*anyone wearing a red jacket near Building C between 14:00 and 16:00*" ([Ambient.ai buying guide](https://www.ambient.ai/blog/ai-video-analytics-in-physical-security); [ai-info](https://www.ambient.ai/ai-info); [file-analysis extension](https://techstrong.ai/articles/ambient-ai-extends-ai-ability-to-analyze-video-files-to-improve-physical-security/)). Its framing of the competitive claim is the industry's standard critique of legacy VCA: "*[t]raditional video analytics rely on motion detection or single-frame object recognition, which generate high false positive rates and cannot reason about behavior or context*" ([ibid.]). Note also the **PACS fusion** claim quoted in §4.5 — "*reducing false positives by 95%*" — which is Part A fusion sold inside a Part B product.
  Observe the shape of that example query: *attribute + place + time window*. **The time and place filters are doing most of the work.** A "natural language search" product is, in production, a natural-language *front end* onto structured filters plus embedding re-ranking. **[lore]**
- Others to be able to place: Vintra (acquired into Alarm.com), Spot AI, Coram, Lumana, Axon. **[VERIFY — not sourced this session.]**

### 11.2 Video AI infrastructure

**Twelve Labs** is the clearest example of the retrieval-as-infrastructure model, and their two-model split maps exactly onto the taxonomy in §6:
- **Marengo** (retrieval): "*Twelve Labs' foundation model for generating video embeddings — compact vector representations capturing the meaning of video content*", which "*turns video into data: spatiotemporal embeddings that make every moment findable by what's actually in it, not metadata someone typed*" ([Marengo 3.0](https://www.twelvelabs.io/blog/marengo-3-0); [models overview](https://www.twelvelabs.io/product/models-overview)).
- **Pegasus** (comprehension): "*Twelve Labs' video-language model designed for interactive analysis of video content*" — "*[g]eneral-purpose models sample frames and guess. Pegasus reasons continuously over the full temporal arc of any asset, up to two hours*" ([ibid.]).

The reference production pipeline is documented concretely: "*integrating the Twelve Labs Marengo 2.7 model on Amazon Bedrock with Elasticsearch, generating 1,024-dimensional multimodal embeddings from video segments, storing them across five vector index types, and querying them using natural language through Elasticsearch's k-NN search*" ([Elasticsearch Labs](https://www.elastic.co/search-labs/blog/twelvelabs-marengo-video-embedding-amazon-bedrock); [Twelve Labs + Elastic](https://www.twelvelabs.io/blog/twelve-labs-and-elastic-search); [Bedrock deployment](https://www.twelvelabs.io/blog/marengo-pegasus-on-amazon-bedrock); [hybrid Marengo+Pegasus pattern](https://jtanruan.medium.com/hybrid-multimodal-video-understanding-with-twelve-labs-pegasus-and-marengo-e1e72d4114f2)).

**The retrieve-then-reason split is the right architecture and you should adopt it by default**: a cheap embedding model over *everything* for recall, an expensive VLM over the *few* retrieved candidates for precision and explanation. Running a VLM over every frame of every camera is economically impossible; running one over 20 candidate clips is trivial. Google Cloud Video Intelligence occupies the commodity-labelling tier of the same market. **[lore]**

### 11.3 Broadcast and media monitoring

This industry has been doing "search live TV" since long before video LLMs, using ASR + closed captions + OCR + logo detection, and it is the best proof that **the boring modalities carry most of the signal**.

- **TVEyes** "*delivers global media monitoring across broadcast, online video, and podcasts*", and its visual search "*enable[s] you to be notified, search for appearances and receive an estimated value when your logo or ad appears on TV*" ([TVEyes](https://www.tveyes.com/); [logo detection / ad spotting](https://tveyes.com/visual-search-solutions-logo-ad-spotting/); [development blog](https://blog.tveyes.com/visual-search-of-tv-broadcasts-detecting-logos-and-other-objects-of-interest); [API/data feed](https://www.tveyes.com/api-partners/)).
- **Kinetiq** "*includes proprietary logo and closed caption recognition to ensure no brand impression goes unrecognized*" ([Kinetiq platform](https://kinetiq.tv/platform/)).
- **Veritone** "*offers natural language processing, transcription, and recognition technology to identify logos, on-screen text, faces, and other identifiable assets*", and its discovery product "*lets you easily search live broadcasts, prerecorded programs, and other large media libraries in near real-time*" ([Veritone, Media & Entertainment](https://www.veritone.com/solutions/media-entertainment/)).

Three lessons for anyone building "NL query over live broadcast" **[lore]**:
1. **Closed captions are the highest-ROI index in existence.** Broadcast television ships a human-quality, legally-mandated transcript, time-aligned, free. Fusing captions + ASR (for uncaptioned and foreign feeds) + on-screen OCR (lower-thirds, tickers, scoreboards) + logo/face detection yields a searchable index that beats any pure-visual embedding on the queries people actually ask ("*when did the minister say X*"). Start there; add embeddings for the visual-only residue.
2. **The monetised unit is the *clip with a value estimate*, not the search result.** TVEyes sells "estimated value" of an appearance. The retrieval is the input to a measurement product.
3. **Near-real-time is the accepted term of art.** Even mature vendors say "near". That is permission to define an explicit latency budget rather than promising instantaneity.

Adjacent capability set: BBC Monitoring and Dstl-funded work occupy the open-source-intelligence end (multilingual broadcast/online monitoring for government), and the verification stack — deepfake detection, provenance (C2PA/Content Credentials), reverse image/video search and geolocation — is now a standard component of any serious media-monitoring pipeline, because a retrieved clip's *authenticity* is a separate question from its *relevance*. **[VERIFY — BBC Monitoring, Dstl, C2PA and deepfake-detection claims were not sourced in this session; the search budget was exhausted before these queries ran. Treat this paragraph as a research to-do, not as established fact.]**

## 12. Quoted and documented failure modes

1. **Hallucination is measurable and endemic.** **VideoHallucer** is "*the first comprehensive benchmark for hallucination detection in large video-language models (LVLMs)*", categorising hallucinations "*into two main types: intrinsic and extrinsic*" with subcategories "*Object-Relation Hallucination (ORH), Temporal Hallucination (TH), Semantic Detail Hallucination (SDH), Extrinsic Factual Hallucination (EFH), and Extrinsic Non-factual Hallucination (ENFH)*", using "*an adversarial binary VideoQA method ... where pairs of basic and hallucinated questions are crafted strategically*" ([VideoHallucer](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381666694_VideoHallucer_Evaluating_Intrinsic_and_Extrinsic_Hallucinations_in_Large_Video-Language_Models)). Companions: **VidHalluc**, "*the largest benchmark for evaluating hallucinations in video understanding, comprising 5,002 videos and 9,295 QA pairs*" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12408113/)), **VidHal** for temporal hallucination ([arXiv 2411.16771](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16771)), and **EventHallusion** ([arXiv 2409.16597](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.16597)); mitigation work includes [SEASON](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04643v1) (self-diagnostic contrastive decoding), [background-controlled pairs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.31933), [CounterVid](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.04778), [MoHallBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01117), and [spatiotemporal-semantic residuals](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.22574).
   **The security-specific danger is *extrinsic non-factual* hallucination**: a VLM asked "is this person carrying a weapon?" that answers from language priors about the scene rather than from pixels. In an evidential chain that is not a bug, it is a false statement in an investigation record. Architectural mitigation: never let a VLM's free text be the record. The record is the clip plus a structured, thresholded detection; the VLM text is an *unverified aid to navigation*, labelled as such in the UI. **[lore]**
2. **Temporal imprecision** — documented as the Moment-DETR high-IoU deficit (§8.1) and as the reason every LLM grounder needs bolt-on time representation (§8.2).
3. **Benchmark bias** — the distribution-only baseline beating deep models on Charades-STA (§8.3).
4. **Benchmarks not measuring their stated construct** — "*Knowledge, Spatial Perception, or True Temporal Understanding?*" ([arXiv 2505.14321](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14321v1)).
5. **Absolute retrieval quality is moderate** — best open model ~0.470 nDCG@10 (§7).
6. **Appearance-not-event matching** — [SPOT!](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.12919).
7. **Bias in person search** — §10.
8. **Similarity scores have no absolute meaning** — hence Verkada's dynamic thresholding (§11.1).
9. **Cost/latency at scale** — no vendor publishes cost per camera-hour of VLM inference. This is the number that kills deployments, and the reason the retrieve-then-reason split (§11.2) is not optional. **[lore]**

## 13. Open problems, 2024–2026

**Fusion (Part A)**
1. Wildlife-vs-human discrimination at the sensor level — explicitly unsolved by the best current NAR-reduction method (§1.6).
2. Correlated nuisance across modalities: a principled weather/common-mode channel in the fusion model rather than heuristic suppression (§4.5).
3. RF-to-vision association in crowds, under randomisation, with multi-device and device-less people (§4.4) — and the labelled data to train it, which barely exists outside simulation.
4. Calibration that maintains itself: automatic radar-camera and LiDAR-camera registration from opportunistic targets, so drift does not require a truck roll.
5. Cross-site generalisation of DAS/fence classifiers: 81.8% on one fence tells you little about the next fence (§2.6).
6. Adversarial robustness with a published protocol — an "adversarial POD" benchmark does not exist publicly (§5.4).
7. Formalising *assessment* performance: the industry measures detection; the bottleneck is human assessment, and there is no standard metric for it (§5.4).
8. Grading regimes meeting learned systems: how IEC 62676-6 (§5.3) handles a model that is updated monthly is an open governance question.

**Retrieval (Part B)**
9. Frame-accurate temporal boundaries — the persistent high-IoU deficit.
10. Streaming *when to speak* decisions with calibrated confidence (§9.2) — the direct analogue of alarm thresholding.
11. Evaluation protocols robust to annotator disagreement; the security world's timed-window scoring (§5.2) is an under-explored import.
12. In-domain surveillance/broadcast grounding benchmarks — Charades and ActivityNet are not CCTV and not news.
13. Calibrated abstention: a retrieval system that says "no match in this footage" and is trustworthy. Nobody sells this, and investigators need it more than they need better rankings.
14. Cost-efficient continuous indexing: the economics of embedding + captioning every camera-hour.
15. Standing/continuous queries as first-class objects, with false-alarm budgets — the merge point of Parts A and B.
16. Provenance and authenticity fused into retrieval, so a retrieved clip carries a verifiable chain of custody.
17. Privacy-preserving retrieval: searching an index without the index itself being a mass-biometric database — the binding constraint on the UK/EU market.

## 14. What a 20-year veteran knows that papers don't say

1. **The camera is the only sensor that closes an alarm; everything else is a cue.** Design every non-camera sensor as a pointing device, and budget the pointing latency. **[lore]**
2. **Nuisance alarms, not false alarms, are what you fight** — a real stimulus that is not an intruder. Get the terminology right in front of a customer and you are instantly credible. Supported by the weather/wildlife/debris causation quoted at [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-3269/4/2/24).
3. **Weather nuisance is common-mode and cheap to kill; wildlife nuisance is local and intruder-like and is not.** The best published method "*can reduce the NAR by more than 90%*" for weather but "*cannot distinguish alarms caused by wildlife*" ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2673-3269/4/2/24)).
4. **Sensor corroboration multiplies less than the arithmetic promises, because nuisance sources are correlated across modalities** — and an AND-gate silently lowers probability of detection. Use confidence escalation, and log what you suppressed. **[lore]**
5. **The programme dies of uptime, not of accuracy.** Two decades of US border-tower audits are about availability and maintenance, not classifiers ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2024/10/us-border-surveillance-towers-have-always-been-broken)).
6. **A late detection is scored as a false positive.** i-LIDS: an alarm in the first 10 s is a TP, "*alarms after 10 s are counted as an FP*" ([*Sensors* 22:3601](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/22/9/3601)). Every debounce frame you add spends that budget.
7. **"Accuracy" without a stated denominator and adjudicator is marketing.** ShotSpotter's "*97% accuracy*" versus "*89% of ... deployments turned up no gun-related crime*" is the same system measured two ways ([SoundThinking](https://www.soundthinking.com/shotspotter-facts-myths-questions/); [MacArthur Justice Center](https://www.macarthurjustice.org/case/williams-v-city-of-chicago/)).
8. **Every alerting system changes the behaviour of the people attached to it, and that is part of its performance** — the Chicago OIG's most durable finding ([OIG](https://igchicago.org/2021/08/24/oig-finds-that-shotspotter-alerts-rarely-lead-to-evidence-of-a-gun-related-crime-and-that-presence-of-the-technology-changes-police-behavior/)).
9. **Audio "aggression" detection is arousal detection.** It cannot separate a fight from cheering because "*[t]he detector analyzes sound but doesn't take words or meaning into account*", and the vendor itself "*guaranteed 100% that the system will at times misconstrue innocent behavior*" ([ProPublica](https://features.propublica.org/aggression-detector/the-unproven-invasive-surveillance-technology-schools-are-using-to-monitor-students/)).
10. **Low base rates destroy high-precision detectors.** An F-score of 0.90 on DCASE-style 30-second mixtures ([DCASE 2017](https://dcase.community/challenge2017/task-rare-sound-event-detection-results)) still yields a mostly-nuisance alert stream when real glass breaks happen once a year per site. **[lore]**
11. **Ship classified events, not waveforms.** The ICO position — "*switch audio recording off by default unless there is a much stronger justification*" ([LegalVision](https://legalvision.co.uk/data-privacy-it/audio-recording-cctv-gdpr-dpa-ico/)) — makes `(t, mic_id, event, spl_db, doa_deg)` a compliance architecture, not just a schema.
12. **The microphone in the dome is a bad microphone.** Wind, resonance and destroyed inter-mic phase. Axis itself points to external mics for "*optimal placement and tailored performance*" ([Axis](https://www.axis.com/products/microphones)). **[lore]**
13. **Everybody builds JDL Level 1 and calls it fusion.** The value is at L2/L3 (situation, threat) and L4 (re-tasking sensors); acceptance is decided at L5, the human interface ([Steinberg et al.](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA391479.pdf); [Llinas et al.](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/tr/pdf/ADA525721.pdf)). **[lore]**
14. **Write down which sensor is authoritative for geometry and which for classification.** Most integration arguments are that undocumented question. **[lore]**
15. **Clock skew, not algorithms, breaks most fusion deployments** — and analytics events are timestamped at inference, not capture, with *variable* latency. Associate on capture time; buffer for out-of-order arrival. **[lore]**
16. **Slew-to-cue fails on registration, zoom policy, cue contention, mechanical latency and dwell starvation** — none of which appear on a data sheet. A 1° bearing error at 600 m is a ~10 m miss. **[lore]**
17. **RF person sensing is a trend sensor, not a census sensor.** Randomisation from iOS 8 / Android 8–12 makes conventional counting over-count the same device ([macaddress.io](https://macaddress.io/faq/mac-address-randomization-in-wifi-probe-requests); [arXiv 2112.02161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02161)).
18. **Distinguish cooperative from non-cooperative RF in the first five minutes of any RF conversation.** A badge and a stranger's phone are different products, different accuracies, and different legal regimes. **[lore]**
19. **Remote ID detects the careless, not the deliberate.** It is a cooperative broadcast; adversaries are non-cooperative. AeroScope's real gift is the "*location coordinates of the operator*" ([Gulf Times](https://www.pressreader.com/qatar/gulf-times/20191230/282054803949817)). **[lore]**
20. **Read the parenthetical, not the percentage.** mmWave counting at "*98.86% accuracy for people counting (0–3 persons)*" ([*Sensors* 26:1289](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/4/1289)) is a very different claim from counting a crowd.
21. **Same technology, three regimes: 81.8% on a fence, 60.4% buried, 96.14% in a method paper** ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12431091/); [PhotoniX](https://photonix.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s43074-025-00160-z)). Always ask which regime produced a number.
22. **The VMS owns the customer.** Analytics reach operators as plugins and metadata streams — hence ONVIF Profile M and the plugin marketplaces ([ONVIF](https://www.onvif.org/pressrelease/onvif-releases-profile-m-for-metadata-and-events-for-analytics-applications/); [Milestone marketplace](https://www.milestonesys.com/marketplace/sound-intelligence/sound-detector-plugin-for-milestone-xprotect/)). Plan the integration surface before the model. **[lore]**
23. **Catalogue/scheme entry is a procurement gate, not a performance ranking** — and CAPSS exists because perimeter sensor networks are an exposed IP estate inside CNI ([NPSA CAPSS](https://www.npsa.gov.uk/cyber-assurance-physical-security-systems-capss-evaluations)). Check which firmware was actually assured. **[lore]**
24. **Be able to name the critics of the systems you sell** — EUROSUR's human-rights critique, EFF and GAO on border towers, MacArthur/OIG on gunshot detection. Naming them first is how you earn trust in a government room ([ITSS Verona](https://www.itssverona.it/european-border-surveillance-system-eurosur-and-its-problematic-impact-on-the-rights-of-the-people-on-the-move)). **[lore]**
25. **In video retrieval, the time and place filters do most of the work.** "*[A]nyone wearing a red jacket near Building C between 14:00 and 16:00*" ([Ambient.ai](https://www.ambient.ai/blog/ai-video-analytics-in-physical-security)) is a structured query with a language front end. **[lore]**
26. **Never discard the structured attribute index for an LLM.** "*[O]bject size, colour, direction, dwell and speed*" ([BriefCam](https://www.briefcam.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/BriefCam-FederalCapabilitiesStatement-2023-1.pdf)) is fast, auditable and court-explainable. **[lore]**
27. **Embedding similarity has no absolute meaning** — hence "*Dynamic Score Thresholding*" ([Verkada](https://www.verkada.com/blog/scaling-natural-language-video-search-inside-verkadas-breakthrough/)). A fixed cosine cut-off is a bug waiting for a demo. **[lore]**
28. **Retrieve cheap, reason expensive.** Marengo for recall over everything, Pegasus over the candidates ([Twelve Labs](https://www.twelvelabs.io/product/models-overview)). VLM-per-frame-per-camera is not affordable. **[lore]**
29. **Closed captions and ASR beat visual embeddings on the queries people actually type.** The broadcast industry has known this for twenty years ([TVEyes](https://www.tveyes.com/); [Kinetiq](https://kinetiq.tv/platform/); [Veritone](https://www.veritone.com/solutions/media-entertainment/)). **[lore]**
30. **Ask the one-frame question**: "how does your system score on one random frame instead of the video?" Small drop = no video understanding ([arXiv 2505.14321](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14321v1)). **[lore]**
31. **Ask for the bias baseline.** A distribution-only sampler "*unexpectedly surpasses several state-of-the-art deep models*" on Charades-STA ([arXiv 2101.09028](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.09028)). No bias baseline, no interpretable number.
32. **Bigger encoders are not the answer**: 1B→6B parameters bought "*1.9%*" in zero-shot retrieval ([InternVideo2](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.15377v3)).
33. **~65% multiple-choice is the state of long-video comprehension** ([MLVU](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.04264)). Price and scope promises accordingly.
34. **A VLM's prose is never the record.** The record is the clip plus a thresholded structured detection; extrinsic non-factual hallucination is a documented category ([VideoHallucer](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/381666694_VideoHallucer_Evaluating_Intrinsic_and_Extrinsic_Hallucinations_in_Large_Video-Language_Models)). **[lore]**
35. **Live NL video monitoring is an alarm-threshold problem in a language-model costume.** "When to speak" ([Dispider](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.03218)) is POD-versus-NAR (§5.1). Import forty years of alarm-management practice instead of reinventing it. **[lore]**
36. **Investigators want calibrated "not present" more than better rankings.** Nobody sells trustworthy abstention. It is the most under-served requirement in the field. **[lore]**
37. **Above roughly 10–15 low-value alarms per operator-hour, operators start filtering by habit and effective POD collapses** — the system's true performance ceiling is a human attention budget. **[lore]**
38. **Every capability in this document is legally gated somewhere.** Audio in the UK, IMSI catchers nearly everywhere, biometric indexing under UK/EU data protection. The engineer who knows the constraint before the lawyer raises it is the one who gets to build the thing. **[lore]**

## 15. Implications for SurvSim (actionable)

1. **Implement the i-LIDS timed scoring rule verbatim** in `eval/alerts` alongside P/R/F1 — TP if an alarm occurs within 10 s of intrusion onset, FP afterwards (§5.2). It is the protocol UK buyers recognise.
2. **Report POD-vs-NAR curves per scenario per weather condition**, never a single accuracy figure (§5.1).
3. **Model correlated nuisance explicitly**: a weather/common-mode driver that simultaneously raises fence-sensor, radar-clutter, video-motion and DAS alarm propensity (§4.5). Without it, corroboration logic will test as far better than it is.
4. **Add wildlife agents** (fox/deer/bird/livestock classes) with fence-contact and zone-crossing behaviours. This is the residual NAR source that survives every weather filter (§1.6).
5. **Extend clock modelling to every sensor**, and emit both capture time and event time with configurable, *variable* inference latency (§4.3).
6. **Build the slew-to-cue testbed**: bearing error, mechanical slew dynamics, autofocus delay, cue arbitration policy, dwell starvation under cue storms (§2.5). Nobody else can benchmark this.
7. **Make the RF module the flagship dataset contribution**: cooperative and non-cooperative emitters, MAC randomisation schedules, duty-cycle gaps, multi-device and device-less people, crowd co-movement — with `mac↔uid` truth (§4.4). This directly addresses a data gap in the RFCam/MaRPA literature.
8. **Emit ONVIF Profile M-shaped metadata** from the ground-truth writers (§4.6), so simulator output is drop-in for VMS integration tests, and consider a geo-referenced track export in the STANAG idiom for defence conversations.
9. **Generate grounded NL query/moment pairs with exact temporal truth** from the behaviour engine's event log — in-domain surveillance temporal-grounding data with zero annotator disagreement, which is exactly what §8.3 and open problem 12 say does not exist.
10. **Include a distribution-only bias baseline in the NL query benchmark** from day one (§8.3), and a one-frame baseline (§9.1). If our benchmark cannot detect a cheating model, it is not a benchmark.
11. **Model operator load** as a first-class output — alarms per operator-hour and a saturation threshold — because that is the real performance ceiling (§5.4, bullet 37).
12. **Model availability and drift**: sensor outages, dirty lenses/panels, PTZ home-position drift, zone-polygon staleness (§1.3, §5.4). The audit record says this is what actually determines field performance.
