## Q1. Would automatic homography from human sizes work — collect data, scale pixels so
a standing person is the same size everywhere, and derive the homography from that? And
why is homography useful here at all?

**Answer (187 words).** This is the known-height-prior auto-calibration family (Lv,
Zhao, Nevatia, PAMI 2006 [A]): body-axis head–foot lines of a walking person converge to
the vertical vanishing point, giving focal length, tilt and roll, and a nominal stature
gives metric scale — fixing the horizon/vanishing line and camera height-tilt up to
scale, not literally resizing boxes. Measured on our data (H39), letting focal float from
box heights is degenerate: 57.4 m RMSE inside 20 m, 257 m beyond 30 m, focal repeatedly
running to its fit bound, because a long lens at shallow tilt and a true lens at steep
tilt predict almost the same pixel height per row when real stature varies ±10 %.
Fixing focal from the datasheet is far better (~1 m inside 20 m, ~3 m beyond 30 m) but
still not the 0.5 m needed for the ground-plane. Two or four surveyed distances per
camera, and switching from box corners to actual head/foot keypoints (both proposed in
`75` §3 finding 5, not yet run), are the likely fix — a box throws away the very cue the
method needs.

**Why it's useful at all:** one metric plan per camera turns zone/dwell/line-cross/speed
rules into metres instead of pixels, lets two cameras fuse tracks on a shared map (H53),
lets PIDS/radar/gate events match by position, aims PTZ hand-off, rejects wildlife/ghosts
by size-at-range, and lets `alarms.score(position_radius_m=...)` credit alarms by position
(H62) instead of only by identity.

## Q2. Wouldn't anomaly detection be better than building specific detectors, since the
number of possible issue types is large and can be classified after the fact?

**Answer (196 words).** Three-tier design, not a replacement: named rules with fusion
handle the high-consequence events we can specify (loiter, line-cross, tailgate); an
anomaly scorer over tracks sits in the review queue as a *ranking* signal, the same role
ranking already plays operationally (H22/H45: ranked review beats FIFO by 0.075–0.262
recall@5 min across ten operator seeds); and operator verdicts on recurring anomalies
promote them into named classes over time — which is classify-later, done properly, with
a human closing the loop rather than the alarm card itself.

Why anomaly can't be the primary layer: anomaly is not threat. The identical-twins arm
is the sharpest case — 0.488 FA/cam-h with perfect track-only perception, about 12× the
assumed PIDS-style nuisance-alarm anchor, and it is *un*explainable by novelty scoring
because a twin isn't anomalous, it's a false correspondence; only context+sensor fusion
removes 99.0% of it (H53, simulated sensors, at a 2.3-point recall cost — R55's qualified
form). An anomaly score also has no per-attack-method detection probability (`81`), no
alarm-card explanation under `86`/Article 22, and drifts (H34: PSI/KS fire on 100% of
stationary windows at one check/min — false-alarm, not signal).

**Pre-register H63** in `66` per `49`'s template: anomaly-layer recall on held-out
unseen behaviour classes generated by the mock backend, vs its FA/cam-h on the twins
arm.

## Q3. What if an intruder wears a guard's uniform?

**Answer (198 words).** Appearance cannot catch this by construction, not as a gap to
patch: `70`/H47's clothing arms move zero-shot recall by ≤ 0.003 either way — clothing is
not a detection signal at all — and the uniform *is* the authorised attribute; a rule
that matches "looks like staff" will match the intruder exactly as well as the staff
member. The catch has to be corroboration, not better appearance modelling:
roster/schedule/route priors (an authorised uniform out of rostered hours or off its
usual route is itself the anomaly), a badge swipe or RF tag event (H53/H54), and a gate
people-counter for tailgating specifically — H54's simulated counter is the only leg that
catches a tailgater at all, 0.685 recall (61/89, H59's actor-aware caveat applies),
where fusion without it is 0.056; H59
shows that under realistic ID-switch noise the *actor-aware score* on that same fused
decision collapses to 0.100 because the alarm gets credited to the swapped label, not
because the fusion itself fails (position/time scoring recovers 0.715 recall at r = 2 m,
but with 31.3% bystander false-credit — a diagnostic, not a replacement, per H62).
Face-at-gate is a corroboration option only under a stated DPIA and an Article 9 basis;
gait is a weak fallback (SUSTech1K GaitBase 81.46% normal → 49.60% with a clothing change,
25.92% at night). Physical layers — turnstiles, mantraps — corroborate independently of
any camera. The line to say out loud: a camera system should never trust a uniform; it
trusts corroboration (H63, simulated: "looks like a guard" suppression leaves recall 0.027; a badge+RF mismatch rule recovers 0.745, 96 % of the line-cross ceiling, at 0.018 twin FA/cam-h).
